**This is the first Ring 3 module.** Ring 3 = the NIKL Grade-C list —
the essay, the newspaper, the lecture. The learner has all of Grade A
and B; what changes now is REGISTER. All 35 words are the prose
register's adverbs, connectors and pronouns. House rule: adverbs,
pronouns and dependent nouns are all tagged `noun`.

**Theme:** the voice of written Korean. Ring 1 gave the learner the
spoken connectors (그래서/하지만/그런데 M7, 그러나/그러면/그러니까 M29);
this module hands over the words a newspaper column is made of — the
adverbs that steer an argument (따라서, 그리하여, 다만, 아울러, 일단,
결코, 과연, 비로소) and the degree adverbs of prose (다소, 상당히, 대체로,
대개, 사실상, 그야말로, 워낙) — plus the three pronouns fiction runs on
(그녀, 그이, 자네) and the one it insults with (놈). Organizing thesis:
**written Korean is spoken Korean with the seams welded shut** — the
four glue endings are -고, -(으)니까, -지만 and -아/어도 in their formal
coats, and every adverb here has a spoken twin already taught.

**Organizing machines (all owners verified — cite exactly these):**

1. **-(으)며 — the written "and/while" (glue part 1).** Verb--고 from
   M10 lists; -(으)면서 from M38 says "while"; -(으)며 does BOTH jobs in
   print: 그는 웃으며 말했다 (he said, smiling — the novel's line), 값이
   싸며 품질도 좋다 (cheap and good — the ad's line). Vowel/ㄹ stems +
   며, consonant stems + 으며. Plain-form (-다/-ㄴ다) sentences around it
   — the prose register M56 opened with the news verbs; verify what
   M56's md says about 평서형/plain style before citing it.
   Calibration: mouths never say -(으)며; if you hear it, someone is
   reading aloud. Sentence slot: ≥1 narrative sentence with -(으)며.

2. **-(으)므로 — the written "because" (glue part 2).** The third row
   of the because-family M84 just finished: -아/어서 (spoken, neutral),
   -(으)니까 = M10 (spoken, precedes commands), -기 때문에 = M84 (formal,
   objective), and now -(으)므로 — the most formal of all, the notice
   board's and the ruling's: 공사 중이므로 우회하시기 바랍니다. Unlike
   -아/어서 and -기 때문에 it CAN precede a request (the sign above does).
   Radar: 그러므로 (therefore — riding frozen, not on the list) is its
   sentence-initial twin; 따라서 (this module) is what prose actually
   uses. Sentence slot: ≥1 sentence with -(으)므로 + -(으)시기 바랍니다
   or -(으)세요.

3. **-(으)나 — the written "but" (glue part 3).** -지만 = M7 (its md Part 3 §2 is headed -지만 — but, welded on; read it). -(으)나
   is -지만 in print: 노력했으나 실패했다. Vowel/ㄹ stems + 나, consonant
   + 으나. Sentence-initial twin: M29's 그러나 ("but / however (written)"
   — that entry's en gloss; its note is the one to quote if it fits).
   Radar: NOT the (이)나 particle from M43 ("or; a whole") and NOT M1's
   나 — 먹으나 is a verb ending, 밥이나 is a particle. Sentence slot: ≥1
   sentence with -(으)나.

4. **-고서 / -고도 — after doing, even having done (glue part 4).**
   Two riders on verb--고 from M10: -고서 = "and only then / after"
   (밥을 먹고서 약을 드세요 — sequence made explicit), -고도 = "even
   having done" (돈을 받고도 일을 안 했다) — -아/어도 from M39 for a
   completed act. Sentence slot: ≥1 sentence with -고서 or -고도.

5. **The steering adverbs (13) — each with its spoken twin (all
   twins verified taught):**
   - 따라서 — therefore, in prose. M7's 그래서 is the mouth's; M33's
     상황 note already ran the OTHER 따라서 (상황에 따라서 = depending on,
     from M32's 따르다 "to follow; to pour") — RADAR: the conjunction
     따라서 and the verb-form 따라서 are one word, two jobs; the comma
     tells you which.
   - 그리하여 — and so (narrative). Built on M29's 그 + M62's 그리
     ("(not) that much; so") + 하여 (the formal -아/어 of 하다 — riding
     frozen as a form). The storyteller's 그래서.
   - 다만 — only, however (the qualifier that opens a caveat). M30's 만
     ("only / just") wearing 다; radar vs M9's 만 (ten thousand).
   - 아울러 — in addition, together with. Formal; the ministry's 그리고.
   - 일단 — first of all, for now (一旦). M2's 일 (one) + 旦 dawn (a
     first): once-dawn = for the moment. 일단 먹자 — the mouth uses it
     too; the only adverb here that is fully spoken.
   - 결코 — never (with a negative). 決코: the decide-決 of M57's 결정
     (verify its hanja is decoded there; if not, decode as a FIRST) —
     "decidedly not". RADAR: NOT M14's 코 (nose). Twin: M39's 절대
     ("absolutely (never)").
   - 과연 — indeed / as expected; (in questions) really? 果然
     fruit-thus. Twin: M32's 역시 ("as expected; sure enough") —
     역시 is the mouth's, 과연 the essay's.
   - 비로소 — only then, for the first time. Twin: M35's 드디어
     ("finally! (spoken)") and M35's 마침내 ("at last, finally
     (written)") — sort all three: 드디어 cheers, 마침내 concludes,
     비로소 marks the condition that made it possible.
   - 아예 — altogether, from the start, at all (with negatives).
     Twin: M39's 전혀 ("not at all") — 아예 안 갔다 = didn't go at all.
   - 앞서 — beforehand, earlier. PAYS M68's 앞서다 note VERBATIM:
     "Its fossil 앞서 (beforehand — riding free" — quote the whole
     span from the note (lookup.py 앞서다). M6's 앞.
   - 때로 — at times. PAYS M38's 때때로: "essays and lyrics say 때때로;
     texts say 가끔" — 때로 is 때때로 clipped, M25's 때 + 로.
   - 어찌 — how (archaic/rhetorical). M33's 어쨌든 ("Built from 어찌+했+든"
     — verify span) and M34's 어쩌면 ("어찌 + -(으)면 fossilized") both
     pre-decoded it; today the root lands. 어찌 된 일이에요? = what on
     earth happened?
   - 도대체 — on earth, the hell (in questions). 都大體: the 도 is NOT
     M2's particle; 대 is NOT M55's counter — three hanja, one
     exasperation. 도대체 왜? Twin: none — this is the mouth's word
     that prose also keeps.

6. **The degree adverbs of prose (14):** 더욱 (M9's 더 intensified — even
   more; 더욱이 rides frozen), 달리 (differently — RADAR: NOT M17's
   달리다 (run) and NOT M16's 달; 달리 말하면 = in other words), 다소
   (多少 many-few = somewhat; twin M56's 약간), 그저 (just, merely —
   RADAR: not M1's 저 nor M29's 저; M52's 그저께 is unrelated), 널리
   (widely — PAYS M72's 이용되다: "널리 이용되다 (널리 — widely, riding
   frozen)"), 상당히 (相當 — considerably; twin M62's 꽤), 참으로 (truly —
   M29's 참 + 으로; twin M11's 정말), 대체로 (大體 — on the whole), 문득
   (suddenly, out of nowhere — a thought, not a bang; RADAR not M6's 문),
   대개 (大槪 — usually, mostly; RADAR: 개 is NOT M5's counter), 사실상
   (事實上 — in effect; M24's 사실 + 上), 끊임없이 (ceaselessly — 끊다
   (verify owner) + -음 + 없이 (M1's 없다 + -이): without a cutting),
   서서히 (徐徐 — gradually; twin M10's 천천히), 그나마 (even so, at
   least — the consolation adverb), 그야말로 (literally, truly — M4's 말
   inside it: "that-indeed-by-words"), 워낙 (by nature, so very — 워낙
   바빠서 = being so busy to begin with), 실로 (實로 — truly, indeed;
   M72's 실제 shares the 實).

7. **The pronouns of fiction (4):**
   - 그녀 — she. M29's 그 + 女, the female-女 M78's 여 prefix is
     ("female- (prefix)"). A twentieth-century coinage from Japanese
     彼女 — before it, Korean prose said 그 for both; still WRITTEN
     only: nobody says 그녀 aloud except in a reading.
   - 그이 — he/she, "that person" (affectionate; a wife of her
     husband). M29's 그 + 이 (person — the 이 of 이분/저이, riding
     frozen as a bound noun).
   - 자네 — you (to a junior or equal, older-male register). The
     professor's and the father-in-law's "you". RADAR: not M1's 네
     (yes) and not M19's 네 (your); M32's 당신 note ("you (formal/
     spousal — careful)") is the other dangerous you — sort them.
   - 놈 — guy, fellow; (insult) bastard. Dependent noun: 나쁜 놈, 이
     놈 — the drama's all-purpose villain word; affectionate for a
     little boy (우리 놈). Its female twin 년 is a slur — mention it
     exists, do not gloss beyond that.
