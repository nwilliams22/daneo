**Theme:** the native nouns, and the adverbs of manner. 36 words.
THE RECEIPTS (입맛 구석구석 눈부시다 집안일 자랑 보리 창), THE PLACES AND
THINGS (땅속 언덕 벼 친정 끈 밑바닥 울음소리 탁자 꼬마 안팎), THE STATES
(착각 신세 야단 뜻밖 한순간 쓸쓸하다 넉넉하다), THE ADJECTIVES (주요하다
탁월하다 끊임없다 참되다), and THE ADVERBS OF MANNER (확 골고루 그제서야
그런대로 활짝 차마 텅 끝없이). Organizing thesis: **the native noun is
usually a verb wearing the freezer** — 울음소리 is 울다 plus -ㅁ plus 소리,
끊임없다 is 끊다 plus -ㅁ plus 없다, and 자랑, 입맛 and 뜻밖 are all welds —
so the note names which verb went in, the way M113 named the picture for
the words that had no halves at all.

2. **THE PLACES AND THINGS (10):** 땅속 (M22's 땅 + M28's 속: underground,
   beneath the earth — 땅속에 묻히다 (M104's 묻히다); the bury-verb 묻다 is
   NOT taught, only its passive, so do not cite it), 언덕 (a hill, a slope
   — smaller than M11's 산, and the one Korean towns are actually built on),
   벼 (the rice PLANT — and this is the whole note: Korean has three words
   where English has one, 벼 in the paddy, M59's 쌀 in the sack, M1's 밥 in
   the bowl; M102's 논 is where 벼 grows), 친정 (親庭 — 親 of M81's 친절
   (verify that note's decode and quote it) + 庭 (a courtyard, a household
   — a first): a married woman's parents' home, as distinct from 시댁 (the
   husband's family — frozen); the word carries a whole social structure
   and deserves two clauses, not a gloss), 끈 (a string, a strap, a cord —
   신발 끈 (M9's 신발) = shoelaces; and the figurative 끈이 떨어지다 (M36's
   떨어지다) = to lose one's connection), 밑바닥 (밑 (the underside — verify
   whether it is taught, and freeze it if not) + M13's 바닥: the very
   bottom, literal and then figurative — 밑바닥에서 시작하다 (M8's 시작하다) =
   to start from nothing), 울음소리 (M7's 울다 + the -ㅁ freezer of M67 +
   M19's 소리: the sound of crying, and of an animal's call — name the
   freezer, it is the module's thesis), 탁자 (卓子 — 卓 (a table — a first)
   + 子 of M47's 손자 (quote its decode): a table, the low one in a living
   room or a café; M6's 책상 is a desk), 꼬마 (a little kid —
   affectionate, never insulting; sort against M4's 아이 and M35's 어린이:
   어린이 is the official word on signs, 꼬마 is what you call the child in
   front of you), 안팎 (M6's 안 + 밖 (verify owner): inside and out, and
   the far more common use: approximately — 한 시간 안팎 (M1's 시간) = about
   an hour, which is the sense a learner will actually meet).

3. **THE STATES (7):** 착각 (錯覺 — 錯 (to err — a first) + 覺 (to perceive
   — verify a taught 覺 and cite it, else a first): a mistaken impression
   — 착각하다, and 착각이야 = you're imagining it; separate it from M63's
   실수: 실수 is a slip in what you did, 착각 is an error in what you
   believed), 신세 (NIKL hint ~를 망치다: one's lot, one's circumstances —
   almost always bad: 신세를 지다 (that 지다 is the bear-it verb, frozen) =
   to be indebted to someone for their help), 야단 (惹端 — both characters
   firsts: a fuss, a scolding — 야단맞다 (M15's 맞다) = to get told off,
   야단치다 (M17's 치다) = to tell off; the parent's and the teacher's word),
   뜻밖 (M10's 뜻 + 밖: the unexpected — 뜻밖에 = unexpectedly, 뜻밖의 소식
   (M20's 소식) = news out of the blue), 한순간 (한- (a single, the very) +
   M38's 순간: a single instant — the drama's favourite time unit),
   쓸쓸하다 (to be lonely, desolate — of a place, an evening, a person's
   back; sort against M34's 외롭다: 외롭다 is the feeling inside, 쓸쓸하다 is
   the atmosphere around, and Korean uses it of scenery far more readily
   than English uses "lonely"), 넉넉하다 (to be ample, enough and to spare
   — of money, time and fabric: 시간이 넉넉해요 (M1's 시간); sort against
   M39's 충분하다: 충분하다 meets the requirement, 넉넉하다 leaves room over).

4. **THE ADJECTIVES (4):** 주요하다 (主要 — 主 of M27's 주인 (verify and
   quote) + 要 of M56's 요구하다 (verify and quote): to be principal, main
   — and the note is a usage warning: the adjective is rare, and the
   determiner-like 주요 is what people write: 주요 원인 (M86's 원인), 주요 도시
   (M22's 도시)), 탁월하다 (卓越 — 卓 of today's 탁자 + 越 (to surpass — a
   first): to be outstanding — sort against M89's 뛰어나다, which the
   learner owns: 뛰어나다 is the everyday praise, 탁월하다 is the citation on
   an award), 끊임없다 (M20's 끊다 + the -ㅁ freezer + M1's 없다: unceasing —
   끊임없는 노력 (M39's 노력); its adverb 끊임없이 is the commoner form and
   belongs in the same entry), 참되다 (M29's 참 + M10's 되다: to be true,
   genuine — of a person, a life, a friendship; the register is literary
   and slightly old, which is the note: nobody says 참된 커피).

5. **THE ADVERBS OF MANNER (8):** 확 (all at once, with a rush — 확 열다
   (M13's 열다); the mimetic of sudden force, spoken and never written in
   a report), 골고루 (evenly, all round — 골고루 먹다 (M1's 먹다) is what every
   Korean parent says at the table; from 고루 doubled, frozen), 그제서야
   (only then, and not before — the adverb of a delayed realization: 그제서야
   알았어요 (M4's 알다); 그제야 is the shorter form), 그런대로 (well enough,
   passably — the shrug made an adverb: 그런대로 괜찮아요 (M7's 괜찮다); built
   on the 대로 inside M54's 그대로 — quote that note and say how the two
   differ), 활짝 (wide open, and in full bloom — 활짝 열다 (M13's 열다), 꽃이
   활짝 피다 (M7's 꽃, M22's 피다), 활짝 웃다 (M7's 웃다) = to beam; three
   collocations, one picture), 차마 (the negative-polarity one, and this is
   the lesson: 차마 always takes a negative and means one could not bring
   oneself to — 차마 말할 수 없었어요 (M4's 말하다); pair it with M116's 여간 if
   shipped, which works the same way), 텅 (NIKL hint ~ 비다: utterly empty —
   and it has one collocation, 텅 비다, so teach the pair, not the word;
   verify whether 비다 is taught and freeze it if not), 끝없이 (M119's 끝없다
   if shipped + -이: endlessly — quote that note; if M119 has not shipped,
   build it from M28's 끝 + M1's 없다 and say so).
