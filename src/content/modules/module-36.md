# Korean, Word-First — Module 36: Done-To

English hides its passives in word order and auxiliary verbs. Korean throws a party instead: a closed guest list of **happen-verbs**, each built from a do-verb plus one of four small suffixes — **이 · 히 · 리 · 기**. You don't conjugate your way in; you learn who's on the list. The good news: you've been meeting party guests undercover for twenty modules, and tonight the masks come off — 걸리다, 막히다, and the 보이다 hiding inside -아/어 보이다 all get introduced properly.

**How to use this:** every passive here is filed next to its parent. Learn them as pairs (열다/열리다, 잡다/잡히다) and the suffixes start feeling like a pattern instead of forty separate words — which is exactly what they are: a pattern that stopped being productive and became vocabulary.

---

## Part 1 — Do-verbs and happen-verbs

Three rules run the whole party:

1. **The subject flips.** 문을 열다 (I open the door) → 문이 **열리다** (the door opens). What was the object puts on the subject suit.
2. **The agent wears 한테.** When the doer still matters, M27's person-particle picks up the "by" job: 도둑이 경찰**한테** 잡혔어요. (Weather and nature prefer 에: 비**에** 젖었어요.)
3. **Blame dissolves.** 컵이 깨졌어요 names no culprit — the grammar of roommates, weather, and fate. Korean reaches for happen-verbs constantly where English would name names; using them makes you sound both fluent and forgiving.

**Old mysteries, solved tonight:** 길이 막혀요 (M12) = 막다 + 히. 감기에 걸리다 / 시간이 걸리다 (M12/M14) = 걸다 + 리, hang-caught. 멋있어 보여요 (M21) = 보이다, *is seen as*. 바뀌다 (M35) = 바꾸다 + 이. The radar was collecting party invitations all along.

## Part 2 — The bricks

::vocab::

**Guests covered by their neighbors' notes:** 먹히다 (get eaten — the food chain's verb), 읽히다 (get read), 안기다 (be hugged — M28's 안다 receiving), 쫓기다 (be chased — 시간에 쫓기다, chased by time). Low-frequency, high-charm; recognize them and nod.

## Part 3 — The glue: 이 · 히 · 리 · 기

| Suffix | Parent → Passive | Feel |
|---|---|---|
| **이** | 보다 → 보이다 · 놓다 → 놓이다 · 쓰다 → 쓰이다 · 바꾸다 → 바뀌다 | be seen, be placed, be used |
| **히** | 닫다 → 닫히다 · 잡다 → 잡히다 · 막다 → 막히다 · 먹다 → 먹히다 | be shut, caught, blocked |
| **리** | 열다 → 열리다 · 듣다 → 들리다 · 팔다 → 팔리다 · 풀다 → 풀리다 · 물다 → 물리다 | be opened, heard, sold |
| **기** | 끊다 → 끊기다 · 안다 → 안기다 · 쫓다 → 쫓기다 | be cut, hugged, chased |

Which suffix goes with which verb? **There's no rule — only membership.** That's why this is a vocabulary module, not a conjugation lesson.

**And the rest of the language?** Two other machines cover what the party doesn't:

- **-아/어지다 on verbs** (M35's machine, second job): 깨지다, 켜지다, 꺼지다, 사라지다 — the -지다 wing of the party.
- **되다-verbs** for the 하다-family: 시작하다 → 시작**되다**, 준비되다, 걱정되다 — swap the 하다, get the happen-form. (You've said 긴장돼요 and 기대돼요 since M34 — that was this.)

Three machines, one idea: Korean would rather tell you *what happened* than *who did it*.

## Part 4 — Building sentences

**Spot the recycling:** M31's 남산 gets seen, M16's 경찰 finally makes an arrest, M35's 포기하다 refuses to quit after 떨어지다 delivers the bad news, and S2's ○○ 중 sets the scene for a dying phone.

::sentences::

**Gap deck:** relief as putting the heart down (마음이 놓여요), getting hit by the wind (바람맞았어요 — stood up), and the silver lining machine 불행 중 다행.

## What's next

Module 37 — **Making-Do**: the party's mirror twin. The same little suffixes (plus 우) run the OTHER direction — 깨다 → 깨우다 (wake someone), 크다 → 키우다 (M35 saw it early) — causatives: not what happened to you, but what you made happen. Band 4's engine room, two-thirds built.
