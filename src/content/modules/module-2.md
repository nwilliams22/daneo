# Korean, Word-First — Module 2: Numbers, Time, and Talking About Yesterday

Module 1 gave you the frame: bricks (words), a little glue (particles + 요), and the Subject–Object–Verb skeleton. Module 2 does exactly one lap of the same loop — new bricks (numbers, time words, places, things), three new pieces of glue (도, 에서, 하고), and one new trick for the verb: the past tense.

**How to use this:** Same as before — top to bottom, out loud, romanization only until the Hangul clicks. Every sentence in this module is built from words you'll have checked off by Part 2, plus Module 1.

---

## Part 1 — Numbers you can already read

Korean has two number systems. The one you need first is **Sino-Korean** (borrowed from Chinese) — it runs dates, minutes, money, phone numbers, and floors:

| # | Korean | Rom |
|---|--------|-----|
| 1 | 일 | il |
| 2 | 이 | i |
| 3 | 삼 | sam |
| 4 | 사 | sa |
| 5 | 오 | o |
| 6 | 육 | yuk |
| 7 | 칠 | chil |
| 8 | 팔 | pal |
| 9 | 구 | gu |
| 10 | 십 | sip |

**The beautiful part: that's all ten you ever learn.** Everything to 99 is stacking:

- 11 = 십일 (ten-one) · 12 = 십이 · 15 = 십오
- 20 = 이십 (two-ten) · 30 = 삼십 · 90 = 구십
- 21 = 이십일 (two-ten-one) · 99 = 구십구

Watch out for the look-alike pair **일 (1) vs 이 (2)** — only the ㄹ 받침 separates them. (The other system, native Korean 하나/둘/셋, counts things and tells hours — it arrives when we do counting words in a later module.)

## Part 3 — New glue

### 1. 도 — "also / too"

Attach 도 to a noun and it means "too". The catch: **도 replaces the particles you already know** — it never stacks on them.

- 저**도** — me too (not 저는도)
- 음악**도** 좋아해요 — I like music too (not 음악을도)

### 2. 에서 — where the action happens

Module 1's 에 points **at** a place — a destination (가요, 와요) or where something exists (있어요). New glue 에서 marks where an **action happens**:

- 학교**에** 가요 — I go *to* school (destination → 에)
- 학교**에서** 공부해요 — I study *at* school (action venue → 에서)
- 카페**에서** 커피를 마셔요 — I drink coffee at the cafe

One more job for plain 에: **time**. 아침**에** (in the morning), 주말**에** (on the weekend). Time words like 오늘/어제/내일/지금 take no particle at all.

### 3. 하고 — "and" / "with"

- Between nouns: **and** — 우유**하고** 빵 (milk and bread; only the last noun takes 을/를)
- After a noun, before the action: **with** — 친구**하고** 영화를 봤어요 (watched a movie with my friend)

### 4. The past tense — one rule, again

You already know the -요 rule (아요/어요/해요). The past is the same fork with **ㅆ어요** bolted on: drop 다, then —

- last stem vowel ㅏ or ㅗ → **았어요**
- otherwise → **었어요**
- any 하다 verb → **했어요**

| Verb | Present | Past |
|------|---------|------|
| 가다 | 가요 | 갔어요 (went) |
| 보다 | 봐요 | 봤어요 (saw) |
| 먹다 | 먹어요 | 먹었어요 (ate) |
| 마시다 | 마셔요 | 마셨어요 (drank) |
| 만나다 | 만나요 | 만났어요 (met) |
| 사다 | 사요 | 샀어요 (bought) |
| 배우다 | 배워요 | 배웠어요 (learned) |
| 듣다 | 들어요 | 들었어요 (listened) ⚠ ㄷ→ㄹ |
| 하다 | 해요 | 했어요 (did) |
| 있다 | 있어요 | 있었어요 (there was) |

⚠ 듣다 is a **ㄷ-irregular**: the ㄷ softens to ㄹ before a vowel ending. A small family (걷다 walk, 묻다 ask) behaves the same — you'll meet them later.

## Part 4 — Building sentences

**The frame grows one slot: [Time] + [Subject] + [Place-에서] + [Object] + [Verb].** Time words like to go first; the verb still goes last; everything between is held together by its particle, not its position.

::sentences::

**Questions are still free.** 어제 뭐 했어요? — "What did you do yesterday?" (뭐 = what — bank it now, it's everywhere.)

## Part 5 — Practice

Cover the right column and translate; then flip and build the Korean from the English.

1. I met my friend yesterday. → 어제 친구를 만났어요.
2. I drank coffee at the cafe. → 카페에서 커피를 마셨어요.
3. I like music too. → 저는 음악도 좋아해요.
4. I went to school yesterday. → 어제 학교에 갔어요.
5. I bought milk and bread. → 우유하고 빵을 샀어요.
6. I listened to music. → 음악을 들었어요.
7. I watch movies on the weekend. → 주말에 저는 영화를 봐요.
8. I'm meeting my friend tomorrow. → 내일 친구를 만나요.

And read these out loud, no romanization: 십일 · 이십오 · 삼십칠 · 구십구.
