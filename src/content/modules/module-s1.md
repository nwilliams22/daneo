## Part 1 — The rules of the playground

Three machines make nearly all Korean slang:

1. **Shrink a phrase to its first syllables.** 갑자기 분위기가 싸해진다 → **갑분싸**. 얼어 죽어도 아이스 → 얼죽아 (gap deck). Reading 4's pressure rule, cranked to maximum — there's even slang ABOUT this (별다줄, gap deck).
2. **Run English through the adapter, then shrink THAT.** 워크 라이프 밸런스 → **워라밸**. 케이스 바이 케이스 → **케바케**. Reading 5 and Reading 4, in series.
3. **Weld two real words.** 꿀 (honey, M3's shelf) + 재미 → **꿀잼**. 심장 + 쿵 → **심쿵**. The compound machine from M1, off its leash.

Because it's all machinery you own, most new slang is decodable on sight — and when it isn't, the Explore tab inflates it. That's how this page gets refreshed: what you discover feeds the deck.

## Part 3 — Using it without getting burned

- **Register check:** slang is 반말's playground. With elders and strangers, one slang word can undo thirty modules of politeness — deploy 아아 at the cafe counter (harmless, universal) before testing 헐 in a meeting (don't).
- **The freshness test:** if a drama character's PARENT says it, it's aged out. 대박 (M17's gap deck) crossed that line years ago and survives as a classic; most don't.
- **You will be forgiven everything.** A foreigner dropping 갑분싸 correctly gets delight, not judgment — slang is the fastest 정 (M26) builder the language has.
