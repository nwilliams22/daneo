# Korean, Word-First — Module 33: Saying and Asking II

Module 32 built the quote-machine's first gear: statements, -다고. This module installs the rest of the gearbox — quoted **questions**, **suggestions**, and **requests** — plus the quieter machine -는지, which lets you fold a question inside a sentence without quoting anyone at all. The vocabulary is the talk shelf: favors, compliments, apologies, proverbs, and the polite formulas that make strangers answer you kindly.

**How to use this:** everything here composes with what you own. -자고 is M19's -자 plus a syllable; -냐고 carries M10's question words unchanged; the politeness entries (여쭈다, 실례하다, 성함) are the humble ladder from M4/M9/M27 growing new rungs. Nothing is from scratch; it's all assembly.

---

## Part 1 — Three little factories first

Before the gearbox, three word-machines this module's bricks run on:

1. **-게 makes adjectives into manner.** 어떻게 (M24 decoded it) was 어떻다 + -게 all along — and the factory is general: 크게 (loudly), 예쁘게 (prettily), 맛있게 드세요 (eat well!). This module ships the demonstrative trio built on it: **이렇게 / 그렇게 / 저렇게** — like this / like that / like THAT over there.
2. **-히 makes 하다-adjectives into manner.** 특히 (M32) wasn't alone: 조용히, 솔직히, and M10's old-timers 천천히/열심히 — one suffix, the whole calm-and-candid family.
3. **-ㅁ freezes verbs into nouns.** 잠 (M28), 꿈 (M8), 느낌 (M32) — and now 웃음, laughter. Ring 2 will make this official; your deck has been collecting its products for months.

## Part 3 — The glue: the rest of the gearbox

M32's rule stands — shift to plain form, add the quote particle, hand it to a verb of saying. Only the particle changes per speech act:

| Speech act | Gear | Example |
|---|---|---|
| statement (M32) | **-다고** | 바쁘다고 해요 |
| question | **-냐고** | 언제 오냐고 물어봤어요 |
| suggestion (let's) | **-자고** | 같이 보자고 했어요 |
| command | **-(으)라고** | 조용히 하라고 했어요 |
| request for ME | **-아/어 달라고** | 도와 달라고 했어요 |

Three notes and the gearbox is yours:

1. **-냐고 rides the question kit.** M10's 뭐/왜/언제/어디/누구 slot straight in: 왜 우냐고 물어봤어요. Yes/no questions work bare: 바쁘냐고 물어봤어요 (asked if I'm busy).
2. **-자고 is M19's -자 + 고.** The casual let's (먹자! 가자!) is exactly what gets quoted — suggestions travel in 반말 even inside polite reports.
3. **주다 has a quote-only disguise: 달라.** Quoting a 주세요 aimed at YOU swaps 주다 → 달라: 도와주세요 → 도와 달라고 했어요. Aimed at someone else, it stays 주라고. Korean tracks who benefits inside the quote — the give/receive axis (M9) never stops working.

**And the quiet machine: -는지.** Fold a question into a sentence as its object — no quoting, just wondering: 내일 뭐 하는지 궁금해요 · 어디 있는지 몰라요 · 이게 맞는지 알아요? Adjectives take -(으)ㄴ지 (얼마나 큰지), nouns take -인지 (누구인지), past takes -았/었는지. It feeds the wondering verbs: 궁금하다, 모르다, 알다.

## Part 4 — Building sentences

**Spot the recycling:** M19's -자 gets quoted, M15's 물어보다 carries the -냐고, M23's -(으)면 돼요 demos 이렇게, and the give/receive axis from M9 decides 주라고 vs 달라고.

::sentences::

**Gap deck:** the untranslatable first-meeting handshake 잘 부탁드립니다, the proverb your confusables drill has been acting out (아 다르고 어 다르다), and 빈말 — the empty words that are actually full of kindness.
