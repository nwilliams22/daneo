# Korean, Word-First — Module 39: Even If, Only If

Band 4 closes on the fine print: **concession** (-아/어도 — even if), **necessity** (-아/어야 — only if), and **scarcity** (밖에 + negative — nothing but). Small endings, big personality — and one of them walks back to Module 10 and decodes a machine you've trusted since Band 1. The vocabulary is the limits-and-effort shelf: conditions, rules, exceptions, endurance, and the words Korea reaches for when trying hard against long odds.

**How to use this:** watch the polarity. Half this module's grammar demands a negative partner (밖에, 절대, 전혀), and its adverbs announce their endings in advance (아무리 → -아/어도). Korean grammar signs its work early; learn the signatures.

---

## Part 1 — The fine print, three clauses

**Even if:** concede and proceed. **Only if:** the gate stays shut until the condition opens it. **Nothing but:** what's outside the named amount doesn't exist. Three stances toward limits — defiance, dependence, and scarcity — each with its own ending. Every contract, promise, and New Year's resolution in Korean is written in these three clauses.

## Part 3 — The glue: three clauses

### 1. -아/어도 — even if

Conjugate to -아/어, add 도 (M2's oldest particle, doing philosophy): 비가 **와도** 갈 거예요 — rain conceded, plan intact. **아무리** announces it with drama: 아무리 바빠도… (no matter HOW busy). And the fossil you already own: **그래도** = 그렇다 + 아/어도 — *even it being so.*

**The permission frame, at last:** -아/어**도 돼요** = "even if I do it, is it OK?" — 이거 먹어도 돼요? The permission panel completes a nine-band arc: 안 돼요 (M10, forbidden) · -(으)면 돼요 (M23, just do X) · **-아/어도 돼요** (allowed). Answer with 네, 그럼요 — or M10's veto.

### 2. -아/어야 — only if

The gatekeeper: 표가 **있어야** 들어갈 수 있어요 — only with a ticket does the door exist. And the retro-decode Band 1 has waited for: **M10's -아/어야 돼요 was this + 되다 all along** — "only if you do it does it become OK." The obligation machine was an only-if machine wearing a coat. Twenty-nine modules of 해야 돼요, finally X-rayed.

### 3. 밖에 + negative — nothing but

M6's 밖 (outside) became a particle of scarcity: 천 원**밖에** 없어요 — *outside of 1,000 won, nothing exists.* It **demands** a negative (없다, 안, 못, 모르다) — 밖에 있어요 is broken Korean. Compare M30's 만: 만 states an amount; 밖에 laments it. 조금**밖에** 안 남았어요 — the deadline's native tongue.

**Polarity kin:** 절대 and 전혀 also travel only with negatives — 절대 안 돼요, 전혀 몰랐어요. This module's grammar shapes even its adverbs.
