# Korean, Word-First — Module 95: Concession and Choice

M39 taught you to give ground: -아/어도 — that md's rule, "**Even if:** concede and proceed." — and it looked like one ending. It was the bottom rung of a ladder. Korean has four ways to say *even if*, and they climb: -아/어도 is the mouth's (비가 와도), **-더라도** is firmer (비가 오더라도), **-(으)ㄹ지라도** is the essay's (실패할지라도), and **-(으)ㄹ망정** concedes the worst and names its price (굶을망정). Walked the other way, the same ladder becomes CHOICE: **-든지** shrugs at which (가든지 말든지), **대신에** swaps one thing for another, **반면에** weighs two on a scale, and **-는 한** and **-기만 하면** name the single condition that decides. Around the endings sit the nouns of the contest itself — 갈등, 경쟁, 협력, 승부, 양보 — the words for where a person stands (지위, 신분, 소속, 계층, 권위), the words for what opens (개방, 공개, 참여, 지원, 투자), and three determiners that empty or fill a noun (아무런, 온갖, 반면). Thirty-one Grade-C words, four glue parts, and one thesis: **concession is a step given, and Korean counts the steps.**

**How to use this:** learn the ladder as a ladder — say one sentence four ways (Part 2's table) until the rungs sound different, then never build with 망정 in a text message. The nouns are hanja sums on characters you own (爭 力 勝 負 接 階 位 身 分 所 屬 權 威 立 安 定 爲 主 種 別 途 開 放 公 參 與 支 資 反 面) — decode each once and the drawer holds it. Parts 3 to 5 are three short lessons; take them as one afternoon.

## Part 1 — The bricks: four drawers of taking sides

Every drawer opens on a receipt. M86's 비롯되다 note wrote "갈등 — conflict, riding frozen; it belongs to M95"; M83's 학생증 note glossed the ID card as "신분증 (the ID card — 신분 is status)"; M75's 양보하다 note ran the noun on highway signs before it existed; and M81's 보호 note left the verb on a shelf — "보호하다 is the Sino one, and print prefers it". Today they all land:

- **CONTEST (갈등 경쟁 경쟁력 협력 승부 다투다 양보 접촉 침묵 거칠다):** two vines twisting (갈등), a war with running (경쟁) and the strength to run it (경쟁력), strength joined instead (협력), the decision a match exists for (승부), the argument without fists (다투다 against M19's 싸우다), the step given (양보), the touch (접촉), the silence kept (침묵), and the adjective for a wind, a sea, a hand or a word (거칠다 against M41's 부드럽다).
- **STANDING (계층 지위 신분 소속 권위 독립 안정 위주 일종 별도):** the floor of society you live on (계층), the position you earned (지위) and the station you were born to (신분), who you belong to (소속), what you are obeyed for (권위), standing alone (독립), change absent (안정 against M81's 안전 — danger absent), and three nouns that only work in front of or after another: 위주 (centred on), 일종 (a sort of), 별도 (separate, extra).
- **OPENING (개방 공개 참여 참여하다 지원 지원하다 투자 보호하다):** a door opened (개방), a file opened (공개), taking part in a process (참여 against M51's 참가하다, which enters an event), support from an institution — and, by a different hanja, applying to one (지원), assets thrown (투자), and the print verb for keeping safe (보호하다).
- **THE DETERMINERS (아무런 온갖 반면):** M29's 아무 in a fossil badge, needing a negative (아무런 문제가 없어요); M55's 온 with 갖 for every kind (온갖 음식); and 반면, which is a noun here and glue in Part 4.

::vocab::

**Radar patrol:** 소속 is 所屬, the place you belong — NOT M28's 속 (the inside) with M59's 소 (the cow) · 안정 is 安定 (change absent), 안전 is 安全 (danger absent) — a bridge is 안전, a job is 안정 · 참여 gives, 참가 enters — 사회 참여, 대회 참가 · 지원 is 支援 support and, by sound alone, 志願 applying — 정부 지원 versus 회사에 지원하다 · 반면's 반 is the flip-反 of M24's 반대, not M5's 반 (half) nor M15's 반 (the class), and its 면 is a face, not the noodle of M3's 라면 · 별도's 별 is separate-別, not M22's 별 (the star) · 승부's 부 is 負 to bear, not the section-部 of M32's 부분 · and 계층's 층 is the same 層 as M13's 층 — a stratum is a floor.

## Part 2 — The glue: the concession ladder — -더라도, -(으)ㄹ지라도, -(으)ㄹ망정

Four rungs on one verb, from the mouth to the vow:

| Rung | Form | Says | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | -아/어도, from M39 | even if — concede and proceed | 비가 와도 갈 거예요 |
| 2 | **-더라도** | even if it were so — firmer, the -더- of M88 inside | 비가 오더라도 갈 거예요 |
| 3 | **-(으)ㄹ지라도** | even though, even should — the essay's | 실패할지라도 후회는 없다 |
| 4 | **-(으)ㄹ망정** | though I — and I'd sooner; concedes the worst | 굶을망정 거짓말은 안 해요 |
| noun | 에도 불구하고, from M86 | despite (a noun's) | 비에도 불구하고 |

**-더라도** goes straight on the stem (오더라도, 어렵더라도, 학생이더라도) and is the rung M39's 아무리 climbs to when the mouth means it: 아무리 어렵더라도 = no matter how hard it should be. The 더 is the eyewitness syllable of M88 (that md: "-더- is the eyewitness marker") turned toward a scene not yet lived — *even if I were to witness it so*. **-(으)ㄹ지라도** takes the prospective badge (실패할지라도 — M35's 실패하다; 잃을지라도) and belongs to the page: an editorial concedes with it, a speech vows with it, a text message never does. **-(으)ㄹ망정** takes the same badge and names a PRICE: the clause before it is the worst the speaker will accept, the clause after is what they still refuse — 굶을망정 (굶다 — to go hungry, riding frozen) 거짓말은 안 해요 (M19's 거짓말) = I'd sooner starve than lie. It is always this shape: [the worse thing]-(으)ㄹ망정 [the thing I won't do]. And for a NOUN, none of the four: 에도 불구하고 from M86 (that md's frame, "[obstacle]에도 **불구하고**") — 비에도 불구하고 = despite the rain.

**Calibration:** 비가 와도 가요 (I'll go even if it rains) · 비가 오더라도 갈 거예요 (even if it should rain, I am going) · 비가 올지라도 가겠다 (even should it rain, I shall go — the page) · 비를 맞을망정 안 갈 수는 없어요 (though I get soaked, not going is not an option). One rain, four postures.

## Part 3 — The glue: -든지 — whichever, whether or

Stem + **든지** (clipped: -든), usually doubled or hung on a question word: 가든지 말든지 = go or don't — the 말다 that has been the auxiliary of -지 마세요 from M14 all along, finally conjugating; 뭐든지 (M10's 뭐) = anything at all; 언제든지 (M10's 언제) = whenever; 누구든지 = whoever; 어디든지 = wherever. It is the INDIFFERENT choice: the speaker lays the options out and declines to care which. M33's 어쨌든 was the preview — that note's decode, "Built from 어찌+했+든 ('however it went'), which is why it shrugs; the -든 piece is a Ring 2 pattern still in the mail" — and the mail has arrived a ring late. Sort it against the two choosers you own: (이)나 from M43 (or; a whole; or something) OFFERS — 커피나 차 = coffee or tea, pick one; -든지 SHRUGS — 커피든지 차든지 = coffee, tea, whichever. On nouns it sits directly (회원이든지 아니든지); on verbs it takes no tense inside.

**Calibration:** 가요? (are you going?) · 가거나 안 가거나 (going or not going — listed, -거나 the flat or) · 가든지 말든지 (go or don't, I don't care). Only the third has a shrug in it.

## Part 4 — The glue: 대신에 and 반면에 — instead of, whereas

Two nouns you now own, each taking a badge-clause. **대신에**: M39's 대신 (instead; in exchange) after the badge -는/-(으)ㄴ from M24 — 밥을 먹는 대신에 빵을 먹었어요 (M1's 밥, M2's 빵) = ate bread instead of rice; 도와주는 대신에 (M9's 도와주다) = in exchange for helping — the trade-off that note called "the trade-off marker", now with a whole clause on each side. **반면에**: today's 반면 (反面, the turned-back face) after the same badge — 가격은 싼 반면에 맛은 없어요 (M21's 가격, M7's 싸다, M18's 맛) = cheap, whereas it has no taste; adjectives take -(으)ㄴ, verbs -는 (많이 먹는 반면에). Bare 반면 opens a sentence: 반면, … = on the other hand — the written twin of M56's 한편. Sort the three buts: -지만 from M7 and -(으)나 from M85 CONTRAST two clauses and lean on the second; 반면에 BALANCES two facts on a scale and leans on neither — which is why it lives in reviews, reports and verdicts, and why the two halves each take their own 은/는.

**Calibration:** 싸지만 맛없어요 (cheap, but tasteless — a complaint) · 싸나 맛이 없다 (the same, in print) · 싼 반면에 맛은 없어요 (cheap; on the other hand, tasteless — a review). The third has already forgiven the restaurant.

## Part 5 — The glue: -는 한 and -기만 하면 — the conditions

Two ways to name the one condition. **-는 한**: badge -는 + **한** (限 — the limit-限 that M39's 한계 decoded as "限界 limit-boundary"; a dependent noun, riding frozen): 살아 있는 한 (M4's 살다) = as long as I live, 노력하는 한 (M26's 노력하다) = so long as you keep trying, 접촉이 없는 한 = as long as there is no contact. It draws a fence and says the second clause holds inside it. **-기만 하면**: the -기 nominalizer from M13 + 만 from M30 + -(으)면 from M20: 버튼을 누르기만 하면 돼요 (M79's 버튼, M20's 누르다) = you only have to press the button; 참여하기만 하면 돼요 = just take part, that's all. On a noun, 만 + 있으면 does the job: 시간만 있으면 (M1's 시간) = if only I had time. Sort both against -아/어야 from M39 (that md's heading "### 2. -아/어야 — only if"): -아/어야 is the gate that stays shut; -는 한 is the fence that stays up; -기만 하면 is the door that is already open and wants one push.

**Calibration:** 노력해야 성공해요 (only with effort do you succeed — the gate) · 노력하는 한 성공해요 (as long as you keep at it, you succeed — the fence) · 노력하기만 하면 성공해요 (all it takes is effort — the open door). Three conditions, three temperatures.

## Part 6 — Building sentences

**Spot the recycling:** M39's 아무리 announces -더라도 and -(으)면 안 돼요 from M20 vetoes the giving-up; the long negation from M28 refuses the silence under -(으)ㄹ지라도; M3's 안 and the badge -(으)ㄴ from M24 (거친) close the 망정 vow; M19's 아니다 doubles under -든지 and the can-machine from M10 lets anyone in; 씩 from M43 on M9's 조금 and M75's 양보하다 answer 대신에; M39's 심하다 and 부족하다 sit on the two pans of 반면에; 도 from M2 carries 갈등 inside the fence of -는 한; and verb--고 from M10, the -기 of M13, 만 from M30 and -(으)면 from M20 all stack inside one 참여하기만 하면 돼요.
