# Korean, Word-First — Module 155: Learning, Food and Named Places

The final grade-C words move from classrooms and shops to names on a map. A proper name does not always reveal its meaning from its parts, but the parts still help you recognize it.

**How to use this:** Learn the notes before the sentences. Use the English, Korean-order gloss and Korean layers to check the same thought in three views.

---

## Part 1 — School, family and food

M44’s 예습 is study before class; 복습 is study after class. M75’s 예습하다 turns the noun into an action. Today’s 출석하다 contrasts with 결석하다 from M75, while 결석 itself was registered in the previous pack. 아드님 and 따님 add respect when you speak of someone else's son or daughter.

The shop names end in -점 or -실. The food names need context: here 중식 is Chinese-style food and 일식 is Japanese-style food, rather than other same-spelling senses. 깍두기 is radish kimchi, unlike 배추김치 from M154.

## Part 2 — Names on the map

고구려, 백제, 신라 and 고려 are historical polity names. 금강산 and 지리산 are mountains; 안동 and 전주 are cities; 대학로 is a named Seoul street and district. 평양 and 한반도 name a city and the peninsula. Learn these as names, while noticing 山 in the two mountain names and 半島 in the peninsula name.

## Part 3 — Familiar grammar, new nouns

No new glue. 국제선 and 외할아버지 from M154 meet in a new phone call, 체육관 returns during an arrival, and 소아과 is compared with 외과. The remaining sentences place school, food and names in useful contexts.

- **-는 김에 from M91** adds a call while booking a flight.
- **-던 from M88** recalls an action in progress.
- **-다가 from M94** shifts between two map tasks.

**Gap deck:** 작은딸 ranks a daughter within her family; 깍두기 is a named radish kimchi. The whole meaning is narrower than a literal first glance.
