# Korean, Word-First — Module 52: There and Back

Ring 1's M23 taught survival travel — asking the way, 여권, 비자, and a 호텔 to collapse into. This module is the trip itself, end to end: the booking desk (예약, 예매하다, and their enemy 취소하다), the lodging ladder from 호텔 down to the creaking 여관, native day-counts for trip lengths (이틀, 사흘, 열흘), the take/bring grid that splits people from luggage, the post-office run that sends 기념품 home, and the overseas shelf (교포, 유학생, 귀국하다) for the trips that last years. The glue is the ending every traveler eventually needs: **-아/어 버리다** — completely, irreversibly, and with feelings — the machine that has been hiding inside 잃어버리다 since M20.

**How to use this:** the shelf is mostly hanja arithmetic with parts you already own — read the decodes first. Then drill the two grids as GRIDS: things ride 가지다, people ride 데리다, elders ride 모시다; and the 흘-family counts days the way Korean actually books them. The glue asks for feelings — practice it on things you genuinely regret.

## Part 1 — The itinerary decodes itself

- **The 예- desk:** M34's 예상 taught the beforehand-豫; now it runs the booking economy — 예약 (ahead-PROMISE: 약속's 約) holds the table, 예매 (ahead-BUY: the mirror of M43's 판매) owns the seat, and 취소 (取消 take-and-erase) is the other button on every screen: 확인/취소, OK or walk away.
- **The day-line completes:** M2 gave you 어제/오늘/내일; today 모레 and the fossil 엊그제 (어제 + untaught 그저께, welded) finish the panel strip. Below them the native 흘-family counts spans: 하루 (M8), 이틀, 사흘 — three days despite the 사! — up to 열흘, with 나흘 and 닷새 as the untaught tail.
- **The take/bring grid:** THINGS ride M28's 가지다 (가져가다 — the mirror its 가져오다 note promised — and 가져다주다, with M9's -아/어 주다 in the weld). PEOPLE ride 데리다 (데리고 가다/데려오다). And grandmother rides M47's 모시다 — respect is a grid row.
- **The post shelf pays its IOUs:** M27's 우체국 note said it's 'where 편지 and 소포 begin their journeys' — 소포 arrives, M40's 부치다 works its mail shift, and M12's 표 finally licks a stamp: 우표, the mail-ticket. 엽서 is a letter the size of a leaf (M49's 낙엽-葉).
- **The overseas shelf:** M31's 해외 fronts 해외여행, 현지 welds 현재's 現 (M35) to 지역's 地 (M31) — the actual-place, home of 현지 음식 — and the people have names: 교포 (womb-siblings abroad), 유학생 (M44's promised job title), and their homecoming verb 귀국하다.

## Part 3 — The glue: done, gone, and felt

### 1. -아/어 버리다 — the dismay setting

M13's 버리다 (쓰레기를 버리다 — throw away) welds onto any verb: do X and THROW IT AWAY behind you — finished, unrecoverable, and you have feelings about it. 기차를 **놓쳐 버렸어요** = missed the train, and it's GONE (M12's 놓치다, promoted to tragedy). **늦어 버렸어요** = ended up late, and there's no un-lating it. Plain past states a fact; -아/어 버렸어요 files a small grief.

### 2. The relief setting

Same finality, opposite mood: 숙제를 다 **해 버렸어요** = did ALL the homework — off the plate, off the mind. 그냥 **말해 버리세요** = just say it and be free. Whether 버리다 mourns or celebrates depends entirely on whether you wanted the thing gone — the ending only promises it IS.

**The fossils decode.** M20 handed you 잃어버리다 with a wink — its note called 버리다 'an oops-completely auxiliary: lost-and-gone' — and 잊어버리다 did the same to M15's 잊다; M28's 잃다 note then X-rayed the weld. Today the machine gets named: those weren't words, they were this ending, frozen solid. (The fossils spell shut — 잃어버리다; the living ending prefers a space — 놓쳐 버렸어요.)

**Calibration:** don't editorialize everything — 먹었어요 is dinner; 다 먹어 버렸어요 is a confession. Save 버리다 for when completeness itself is the news: the last ticket, the emptied account, the train pulling away.
