# Korean, Word-First — Module 65: Where in the House

Band 6 keeps slicing the long tail, and this slice is the house's geography — the words Korean points with when it's talking about where a thing is in a room, a house, or a gate. Two spines carry the load: a **side factory** built on M23's 쪽 (위쪽, 아래쪽, 앞쪽, 뒤쪽, 안쪽 — the five directions re-shelled as sides) and an **inside factory** built on M28's 속 (물속, 꿈속, 머릿속, 가슴속 — nouns given an interior). Around them, the rooms and gates the factories attach to: 안방, 복도, 옆방, 옆집, 창고, 강당, 교문, 정문, 출입문, and the branch (지점, this slice's lone C-grade word). The outside has its own three: 창밖 (seen through glass), 바깥 (the place outdoors), 눈앞 (right at point-blank). Decision and motion verbs (M35's 바뀌다, M36's 열리다, M36's 닫히다, M32's 통하다, M36's 보이다) do the moving.

**How to use this:** the two factories are the module — drill the side factory as one axis (위/아래/앞/뒤/안 + 쪽 = five sides in one stroke) and the inside factory as the other (물/꿈/머리/가슴 + 속 = four insides in one stroke). Then spend your reps on the same-syllable pairs that are actually different hanja: 창 in 창밖 (窓 window) vs. 창 in 창고 (倉 warehouse); 정 in 정문 (正 main) vs. M26's 정 (情 the feeling); 식 in 식구 (食 eat) vs. M55's 식 (式 style); 양 in 양쪽 (兩 both) vs. M63's 양 (量 quantity); 입 in 출입문 (入 in) vs. M14's 입 (mouth); 눈 in 눈앞 (目 eye) vs. M11's 눈 (snow). One sound, two owners.

## Part 2 — The side factory and the inside factory

::vocab::

**Radar patrol:** The same-syllable-different-hanja splits are the module's sharpest edge. 창 in **창밖** is 窓 (window) — the look-through-the-glass — while 창 in **창고** is 倉 (warehouse) — the place you store things. Same sound, two different hanja, two owners. 정 in **정문** is 正 (main, frontmost) — the gate you enter through — while M26's 정 is 情 (the feeling) — a completely different word that happens to share the sound. 식 in **식구** is 食 (eat) — the mouths that share the table — while M55's 식 is 式 (style) — the register. 양 in **양쪽** is 兩 (both) — the two sides of a pair — while M63's 양 is 量 (quantity) — the amount. 입 in **출입문** is 入 (in) — the going-in — while M14's 입 is the mouth (口) — a different word wearing the same face. And 눈 in **눈앞** is 目 (eye) — the eye, the one that sees — while M11's 눈 is snow (雪) — the weather. One sound, two owners. The 門 family runs its own small kit: 문 (door, M6), 현관 (entryway, M13), 교문 (school-gate, this module), 정문 (main gate, this module), 출입문 (entryway-door, this module). Same syllable, five different registers of "the way in."

## Part 3 — The verb machines

### 1. The side factory

The five directions re-shelled as sides, all built on M23's 쪽: **위쪽** (upper side — the top of a below-above pair), **아래쪽** (lower side — the bottom), **앞쪽** (front side — the forward member of a front-back pair), **뒤쪽** (back side — the rear), **안쪽** (inner side — the inside member of an outside-inside pair). Five directions, one stroke. The opposite pair is always there: 위 vs. 아래, 앞 vs. 뒤, 안 vs. 바깥.

### 2. The inside factory

The four insides, all built on M28's 속: **물속** (in the water — the inside of the water), **꿈속** (in the dream — the inside of the dream-state), **머릿속** (in the head — the inside of the mind), **가슴속** (in the chest — the inside of the heart-seat). The same 속 gives a body and a mind the same kind of inside. M1's 있다 is the verb that puts something in each.

### 3. The rooms and gates

**안방** (the inner room — the head of the household's, the room with the most weight) and **복도** (the corridor — the passage between the rooms) are the two rooms the house runs on. **옆방** (the adjoining room) and **옆집** (the neighboring house) are the next-door pair, built the same way (M6's 옆 applied to M8's 방, then to M1's 집). **창고** (the warehouse — the place you store things) is the outside of the inside. **강당** (the hall — the big room of a school), **교문** (the school-gate — the main entrance of a school), **정문** (the main gate — the front gate as opposed to a side gate), and **출입문** (the entryway-door — the door for going out and in) are the gate family. And **지점** (the branch — 支+점, this slice's only C-grade word) is the branch you walk into, the 支 branch-off of a main 店 shop.

### 4. The outside and the seen

**창밖** (the window-outside — what you see through the glass), **바깥** (the outside — the place outdoors, as opposed to indoors), and **눈앞** (right in front of the eyes — at point-blank range) are the three ways the outside reaches you. M36's 보이다 does the appearing in the seen sentence; M13's 넓다 sets the width.

### 5. The state verbs

**M35's 바뀌다** (switch) changes the window's state in the interior sentence. **M36's 열리다** (open by itself) opens the hall's door on its own; **M36's 닫히다** (close by itself) closes the main gate's entryway-door. **M32's 통하다** (go through) connects the school-gate and the campus-center. The four state-verbs of a room and a gate.

## Part 4 — Building sentences

**Spot the recycling:** M10's -고 chains the room-tour and the adjacent-pair sentences, M20's -면 gates the two "if you look" conditionals in the seen sentence, M6's -로 marks the destination in the room-tour, M35's 바뀌다 does the switching in the interior, M36's 열리다 / 닫히다 / 보이다 do the opening, closing, and appearing, M32's 통하다 does the going-through in the campus sentence, and -아/어 보다 (M16) tries the walk in the seen sentence.

::sentences::

**Gap deck:** the woman of the house (안방마님 — the matriarch who runs the home), and the city that does not sleep (불야성 — the all-night lights) — the house's two most useful names, one for the person at its head and one for the city on the other side of its wall.
