# Korean, Word-First — Module 26: Korea and Culture

The last module of Ring 1, and the last garnish: **-네요** — the *noticing* ending, for things discovered in real time. The theme is the country itself — its flag, its king, its wave, its untranslatable warmth — and the sentences are built, deliberately, from every module before this one. This is the victory lap.

## Part 1 — Reporting vs noticing

맛있어요 states a fact — you could be reading it off a menu. 맛있**네요**! means you just found out — the food is in your mouth and your eyebrows are up. That's all -네요 is: the ending for *real-time discovery*, spoken at the moment the world surprises you.

- 비가 오**네요** — oh — it's raining *(looking out the window)*
- 벌써 십이월이**네요** — it's December already?! *(where did the year go)*
- 한국어를 정말 잘하시**네요**! — you speak Korean really well! *(said TO you, constantly, from your first 안녕하세요)*

That last one is the module's practical payoff: -네요 + M4's honorific -(으)시- is the compliment Korea hands every learner. You'll need a reply; the gap deck's 잘 못 해요 deflection still works, and now you can also say the truth: 아직 배우고 있어요.

## Part 3 — The glue

### 1. -네요 — the noticing ending

Straight onto any stem, tense included: 예쁘**네요** (how pretty!), 왔**네요** (oh, you're here!), 아름답**네요** (said 아름담네요 — the nasal slide's farewell exhibit). With the honorific: 잘하시네요. Use it when the moment surprises you; use plain -아/어요 when you're just filing a report. Koreans read the difference instantly — a -네요 compliment lands warmer because it sounds *discovered*.

### 2. The engine, complete

This was Ring 1's last new piece. Take inventory of what runs now: three tenses, three registers, three badges (-(으)ㄴ/-는/-(으)ㄹ), the can/must/want/try/don't machines, both because-words, if, when, before, after, comparisons, requests from 주세요 to -(으)ㅂ시다 — and roughly a thousand words to feed them. Ring 2 adds refinement, not foundation. There is no sentence in this module — none in this course — you can't now take apart.

## Part 4 — Building sentences

**Spot the recycling:** all of it, this time. 설날 때 입는 한복이 정말 예쁘네요 runs M25's 때, M24's badge, M21's 한복, and M1's 예쁘다 in one breath; 세종대왕이 한글을 만들었어요 finally *says* the story the first reading told; 이제 한국어로 이야기할 수 있어요 is M10's can-machine reporting on itself.

## Part 5 — Practice

Cover the right column and translate; then flip and build the Korean.

1. You speak Korean really well! → 한국어를 정말 잘하시네요!
2. It's the first snow! → 첫눈이 오네요!
3. King Sejong created Hangul. → 세종대왕이 한글을 만들었어요.
4. Koreans have a lot of jeong. → 한국 사람은 정이 많아요.
5. It's already December! → 벌써 십이월이네요.
6. Keeping traditions is important. → 전통을 지키는 것이 중요해요.
7. Now I can converse in Korean. → 이제 한국어로 이야기할 수 있어요.
8. Studying Korean is just beginning. → 한국어 공부는 이제 시작이네요.

The mining drill: pick any sentence in this module and name the module each block came from. You can now source every syllable you speak — that's what "word-first" was for.
