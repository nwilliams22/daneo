# Korean, Word-First — Module 42: You Know, You See

Band 5 opens its emotional wing. M34 built the hedging machines and stocked the first feelings shelf; this module finishes the emotional vocabulary — the -ㅁ factory declared open, fear and flutter, pity and longing — and installs the two endings Koreans actually manage feelings with: **-잖아요** (you know this already) and **-거든요** (here's what you didn't know). A matched pair, and grammar for the space BETWEEN two people: what's shared, what's missing, and who supplies it.

**How to use this:** the glue is social, not structural — both endings bolt onto anything, and the whole difficulty is aim. Run the 눈치 check this module's vocabulary names: does the listener already know it? -잖아요. Do they need it? -거든요. Point the wrong one and grammar turns into attitude.

---

## Part 1 — Feelings happen TO you

Korean feelings are substances with entrances. They **erupt**: 겁이 나요, 울음이 나요 — M28's 나다, the door feelings always use. They **happen**: 긴장돼요, 기대돼요 — M36's 되다-verbs, tension arriving on its own schedule. And they're **received**: 충격을 받다, 감동을 받다 — M9's 받다 running an emotional inbox. English *has* feelings; Korean gets delivered them — and the -ㅁ factory (open at last: 기쁨 · 슬픔 · 울음 · 즐거움) bottles the inventory for storage.

The shelf itself runs on axes: **familiar ↔ strange** (익숙하다 ↔ 낯설다, with 어색하다 as the weather in between), a **pity row** graded by target (불쌍하다 the person · 안타깝다 the situation · 괴롭다 yourself), and **hate ↔ longing** (밉다 ↔ 그립다) — both, tellingly, adjectives: in Korean they're states you're in before they're things you do.

## Part 3 — The glue: two endings that manage the listener

### 1. -잖아요 — you know this already

Points at common ground: 제가 어제 **말했잖아요** — I told you (and you were there). It reminds (아까 말했잖아요), comforts (많이 연습했잖아요 — you KNOW you practiced), and reasons from the shared (내일 시험이 **있잖아요** — as we both know…). Attaches to anything: past inside (했잖아요), nouns via -(이)잖아요 (학생이잖아요). Sentence-openers too: **있잖아요**… = you know what? — Korea's favorite way to start a confession.

**The warmth and the danger:** aimed at real common ground, it's intimacy — *we both know this.* Aimed wrong, it's an accusation: 말했잖아요 to a customer reads "I ALREADY told you." When in doubt, hand the information over fresh instead — that's the other ending's job.

### 2. -거든요 — here's what you didn't know

Hands over missing background: 내일 시험이 **있거든요** — see, I've got an exam tomorrow. The reason-giver: statement first, background second — 오늘은 일찍 가야 돼요. 내일 시험이 있거든요. Also the storyteller's feeder: 어제 카페에 **갔거든요. 그런데**… — setup delivered, story continues, listener leaning in. Its answer-sound is this module's 어쩐지! — *no wonder.*

**The minimal pair — this module's thesis in one sentence, twice:** 내일 시험이 **있잖아요** (as you know — now help me pack) vs. 내일 시험이 **있거든요** (which you didn't know — and now my leaving makes sense). Same words, opposite listener.

**Calibration:** -잖아요 points at ground you share; -거든요 pours new ground under the listener's feet. Both are speech-only — they drop to 반말 (M19's rules) by losing the 요 (말했잖아 · 시험 있거든), and neither survives on paper, because managing a listener requires one.
