# Korean, Word-First — Konglish: the Adaptation Machine

The course has handed you loanwords one at a time — 카페, 피자, 컴퓨터, 셀카 — each tagged "Konglish" and left at that. This reading opens the machine. Loanwords aren't random: English (and German, and Portuguese) enters Korean through a **fixed set of adaptation rules**, and once you know them, you can decode Konglish you've never seen — and predict how a new word WILL be borrowed before Korea borrows it.

---

## Rule 1 — The sound converter

Korean has no F, V, Z, or TH, and the machine substitutes consistently:

| English | becomes | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **f** | ㅍ | coffee → 커피 · France → 프랑스 · fighting → 화이팅 (or ㅎ before ㅘ) |
| **v** | ㅂ | video → 비디오 · service → 서비스 |
| **z** | ㅈ | pizza → 피자 · size → 사이즈 |
| **th** | ㅅ / ㄸ | marathon → 마라톤 · health → 헬스 |
| **r/l** | ㄹ (merged) | radio → 라디오 — one liquid fits all |

And English's crowded consonant endings get vowel cushions: test → 테스트 (one syllable becomes three), Christmas → 크리스마스 (M25's five-syllable holiday). The rule: Korean syllables need vowels, so the machine installs 으 wherever English piles consonants. This is why Konglish is often LONGER than its source.

---

## Rule 2 — The clip machine

Long loans get the 준말 treatment (previous reading) immediately:

- 셀프 카메라 → **셀카** (M20 — coined before "selfie" existed)
- 에어컨디셔너 → **에어컨** (M11)
- 아파트먼트 → **아파트** (M13)
- 리모트 컨트롤 → **리모컨**
- 아르바이트 → **알바** — part-time job, borrowed from GERMAN (Arbeit) via Japanese, then clipped by Korean: three languages, one word

The borrowed word arrives, the shrinker runs, and the clip becomes the real word — say 에어컨디셔너 in full and you'll sound like an instruction manual.

---

## Rule 3 — Made in Korea

The best Konglish isn't borrowed English at all — it's **English parts, Korean assembly**, and English speakers won't understand it:

- **핸드폰** (M6) — hand + phone. English says cell phone; Korea built a better compound
- **원피스** (M21) — one-piece: a dress
- **스킨십** — skin + -ship: physical affection, the word English needed and never made
- **아이쇼핑** — eye shopping: window shopping, upgraded (you shop with eyes, not windows)
- **화이팅** (M15's gap deck) — fighting: the national cheer

Treat these as Korean words wearing borrowed parts — because that's what they are.

---

## Rule 4 — The false friends

The machine sometimes keeps a word and changes its meaning. These are the ones that bite:

| Konglish | is NOT | it means |
|---|---|---|
| 사이다 (M30's gap deck) | cider | clear soda — and a satisfying moment |
| 서비스 (M9's gap deck) | service | a freebie, on the house |
| 미팅 | a meeting | a group blind date |
| 컨닝 (cunning) | cleverness | cheating on a test |
| 샤프 (sharp) | an adjective | a mechanical pencil |
| 비닐 (vinyl) | records | a plastic bag (비닐봉지) |
| 핸들 (handle) | a door part | a steering wheel |
| 헬스 (health) | wellness | the gym (헬스장) |

The pattern: Korea borrowed the word for ONE specific use and let the rest go. When a loanword surprises you, assume specialization, not error.

---

## The long-haul immigrants

Some "English" loanwords aren't from English at all. **빵** (bread, M3) sailed from Portuguese (pão) to Japan to Korea centuries ago — M27's 프랑스 note already confessed the vibes were wrong. 아르바이트 marched in from German. 츄리닝 (sweatpants) mangled "training" through Japanese first. Loanwords have passports full of stamps; Konglish is just the newest arrivals hall.
