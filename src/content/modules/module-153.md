# Korean, Word-First — Module 153: Meals, Places and Family Connections

A shirt button, a palace visit, a hospital discharge and a family meal: this pack gives familiar scenes more exact names. The small distinctions matter. One word tells you which uncle; another tells you which part of the month.

**How to use this:** Learn the words and their notes first. Then read each sentence from natural English through the Korean-order gloss to Korean. Trace matching chunks instead of translating one word at a time.

---

## Part 1 — Read the last piece

생활비 joins the cost family. 영화관 ends in building-館; 고궁 ends in palace-宮. 삼계탕 ends in soup-湯. These endings sort the world before you have memorized every whole word.

볶음 and 튀김 preserve different cooking actions inside nouns. On the quality shelf, 시대적, 현대적, 예술적, 대중적 and 문학적 use the familiar -적 pattern. Put 인 after them before a noun; do not silently treat the bare form as an English adjective.

## Part 2 — A relationship, not a measurement

큰아들 from the last pack counted order among sons. 외아들 counts how many sons there are. 작은아버지 picks a branch of the family, while 장모 and 장인 identify a wife’s parents. Translating all of them as a generic relative throws away the useful part.

초순 covers the first ten days; 하순 starts on the twenty-first. Neither one means a week. And keep the homograph radar active: 인도 from M50 is a sidewalk; today 도장 is a seal and 장인 is a family member.

**Earlier descriptions, new scenes:** 분홍색 and 미끄럽다 are already taught in M60. Reuse the color with clothing and the texture with a path; the new checklist here supplies the food, family and place words around them.

## Part 3 — Building sentences

No new glue. Familiar frames carry the new vocabulary; notice which noun is the subject and which action closes each sentence.

**Spot the recycling:** 와이셔츠, 전시되다 and 큰아들 from M152 return in a button check, an art description and a homecoming meal.

- **-는 김에 from M91 adds a check while getting dressed.**
- **-(으)ㄹ 뿐만 아니라 from M89 adds a second quality.**
- **-(으)므로 from M85 explains the family meal.**

Eight sentences put the vocabulary into familiar grammar frames.
