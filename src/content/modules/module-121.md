# Korean, Word-First — Module 121: Names on Forms and Stages

Written labels and public stages force the learner to sort identical sounds by sense. The 비행 in misconduct is not flight, and the 주문 chanted as a spell is not a restaurant order. Names on forms, in shops, and on stages all need the same careful reading.

**How to use this:** read the three drawers, then use the word notes for the exact sense and register. Every sentence reuses a Ring 3 pattern already taught; there is no new glue in this pack.

---

## Part 1 — Names and registers

The written shelf includes registration, documents, subscribers, formal classifications, and academic labels. The stage shelf brings music, exhibitions, ambassadors, and public speech. Three homographs make the distinction visible: 비행, 주문, and 대사.

**Root and form map:**

| word family | what changes |
|---|---|
| noun → verb | A listed noun and its 하다 or 되다 partner change which event or participant is in focus. |
| root → -적 | The suffix makes a label that modifies a following noun; it does not invent a new root. |
| homograph | A shared sound does not guarantee a shared sense or hanja. Read the noun it travels with. |

**Use the word in context.** The entries give a real setting and a comparison with a word already owned. The small side shelf stays in the pack because the grade-C list is ranked; no extra vocabulary is introduced to make the theme look tidier.

**Radar patrol:** 비행 非行 is misconduct, while 飛行 is flight · 주문 呪文 is a spell, while 注文 is an order · 대사 大使 is an ambassador, while dialogue is another 대사 · 미스 here is Miss, a title.
