# Korean, Word-First — Module 43: Payday

Ring 1 taught the transaction surface — M9's 돈 and 세일, M16's office with its 월급 and 근-verbs. This module clocks in for the whole working economy: the day's rhythm (출근 · 근무 · 퇴근), getting hired (취직, 면접, 계약), the money apparatus Korea actually lives on (통장, 저축, 세금, 보험), and the business world from 공장 to 소비자. The glue is a pair of quantity attitudes: **(이)나** — or / a WHOLE / I-guess, three jobs in one particle — and **씩**, the distributor that turns amounts into habits. By the end you can commute, get hired, get paid, and watch the money leave — all in Korean.

**How to use this:** almost every noun here is Sino-Korean built from pieces you own — read the notes for the decodes and this module halves itself. Spend your reps on the register pairs (월급/임금, 팔다/판매하다, 사업/장사, 세일/할인): Korean runs its economy in two vocabularies, and this is the module that hands you the second one.

## Part 1 — The workday decodes itself

Band 5's rule: nothing here is a stranger.

- **The 근-family completes:** M16 gave you 출근하다, 퇴근하다, 야근하다 — verbs wearing the duty-근. The nouns now step out (출근, 퇴근) and 근무 names the duty itself: M16's 근 + 사무실's task-무. 출퇴근 시간 = rush hour, the compound Korea commutes inside.
- **The 직-family completes:** 직업 the occupation (M16), 직원 the person (M16), and now 직장 the place and 취직 the getting-in. Four words, one duty-직.
- **The 품 shelf:** 제품 is the goods as MADE (공장's output); 상품 is the goods as SOLD (the shelf's inventory) — same object, one register apart, and the 상-radar seats its fifth member.
- **The 者 payroll:** M16's 기자 and M27's 환자 introduced the person-suffix; 소비자 (the spender) and 부자 (the haver) join the roster.
- **The 金 ledger:** 금요일's metal-금 (M5) and 현금 (M9) already banked the gold-root; 세금, 임금, and the garnish 적금 all draw on the same account.

**Homograph patrol:** this module runs on double agents. 수입 is BOTH import (무역's other half) and income — one spelling, two money words, and the note runs both. 임금 is wages in the news and the king in the folktale. ## Part 3 — The glue: two quantity attitudes

### 1. (이)나 — or · a WHOLE · I guess

One particle, three attitudes, all about expectations. **Job 1, or:** between nouns — 커피나 차 주세요 (coffee or tea; the particle rides the first noun, batchim words take 이나). **Job 2, the whopping reading:** after an amount, it means the speaker finds it BIG — 세 시간이나 걸렸어요 = it took three WHOLE hours; 만 원이나 했어요 = it cost a whole ten thousand. **Job 3, the settling shrug:** 라면이나 먹어요 — ramen, I guess; the option chosen because nothing better offered itself. Like 마다 (M31) and 만 (M30), it swallows 이/가 and 을/를 whole. And Band 1 finally shows its receipt: 얼마나 (M11) was this 나 all along.

### 2. 씩 — apiece, each-at-a-time

The distributor: 하나씩 (one apiece — how 동전 enters a jar), 조금씩 (little by little — how it becomes 저축), 한 달에 한 번씩 (once a month, every month — how an 적금 gets fed). It rations an amount across people or across time, sticks straight onto the amount word, and conjugates nothing.

**Calibration:** (이)나 EDITORIALIZES an amount — too big, or good enough, or either one; 씩 SCHEDULES it. 세 시간이나 자요 = he sleeps three whole hours (outrage); 세 시간씩 자요 = he sleeps three hours at a stretch, every time (routine). Same numbers, opposite attitudes — the module's financial advice is built from the second: 조금씩 저축하세요.
