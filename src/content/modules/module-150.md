# Korean, Word-First — Module 150: Private Lives and Public Rituals

A fee is paid, a recording survives a performance, and relatives gather. These words move between a person's inner life and the public institutions around it.

**How to use this:** Learn the checklist, then read each three-layer sentence in order. Trace each Korean chunk through the gloss to the natural English.

> **Warm-up — transfer from Module 149.** Say: (1) Register for a class after finding a lower tuition fee. (2) Meet an angler by the river after a performance. (3) Offer spring water to someone tired from study. Build new scenes from the previous pack's blocks.

---

## Part 1 — The public and the private

사립 marks a privately founded institution; 사생활 names a person's private life. 생활수준 measures material conditions, while 속마음 names feelings that may never be spoken. 무책임하다 evaluates conduct; it is not a description of someone's worth.

## Part 2 — Family, art and ceremony

만화가 creates comics and 머리말 opens a book; 음반 from M51 returns as the released recording. 시댁 names a husband's family home; 장례식 names the ceremony of farewell. M74’s 손잡다 can describe literal hands or cooperation.

## Part 3 — Building sentences

No new glue. The sentence frames are familiar; the new work is choosing the precise word for each role.

**Spot the recycling:** M149's 학비 and 등록하다 return in an enrollment scene; 공연되다 becomes an attributive form, and 약수 appears in a body-sensation sentence.

- **-(으)려고 from M50 states an intention behind payment.**
- **-(으)며 from M85 joins staying awake and writing.**
- **-더니 from M88 links a cat's sound to a visible reaction.**

## Practice

1. Explain how 사립 differs from public ownership.
2. Use 학비 and 등록하다 while keeping the purpose clear.
3. Turn 공연되다 into a modifier before 음악.
4. Describe the difference between 사생활 and 속마음.
5. Reuse M74’s 밤새우다 with a writing task.
6. Return to M149's 강변 during an 올여름 plan.
7. Recall both literal and figurative 손잡다 from M74.
8. Say what the gap phrase 쓴맛을 보다 means beyond tasting.

**Gap deck:** 손잡다 and 쓴맛을 보다 carry meanings beyond their word-by-word readings.
