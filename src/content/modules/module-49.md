# Korean, Word-First — Module 49: Reading the Sky

M11 taught you to SAY the weather (비가 와요, the seasons, the elevator smalltalk); M22 handed you the landscape (강, 나무, 숲). This module walks you out into it: the light shelf (햇빛/햇살/햇볕 — three suns English keeps in one word), the thermometer (기온/온도), the ground underfoot (흙, 모래, 바위), the folklore zoo (호랑이, 곰, 토끼), the storm — and the module's centerpiece word-family, the ㅎ-color adjectives that were hiding inside M7's color nouns all along. The glue is the syllable you've been SAYING since your first meal: **-겠-**. 잘 먹겠습니다 carried it in M18; today the machinery gets named, and it turns out to run the weather forecast, the volunteer's hand, and 알겠습니다 itself.

**How to use this:** drill the 해-triple and 그림자/그늘 as PAIRS — the precision is the vocabulary. The color table is one machine, not five words: learn the ㅎ-drop once and read all of them. And say every -겠- example out loud; this ending lives in speech.

---

## Part 1 — The sky decodes itself

- **The 해-triple:** 해 (M11) + linking-ㅅ (M40's 고춧가루 wedge) builds three suns by what they do to you — 햇빛 you see, 햇살 slants through windows, 햇볕 warms your back. English says "sunlight" three times; Korean never confuses them.
- **The thermometer:** 기온 = 공기's air-氣 (M22) + 온천's warm-溫 (M23) — the forecast's number. 온도 = that same 溫 + 정도's degree-度 (M32) — anything's number. Sky vs everything.
- **The ㅎ-color table:** 파랗다, 하얗다, 까맣다, 노랗다, 빨갛다 — the adjectives BEHIND M7's color nouns (빨간색 = 빨간 + 색: the badge form was in there all along). Same family as M24's 어떻다 (어때요!) and M29's 그렇다: drop the ㅎ before a vowel and fuse — 파래요, 하얘요, 까매요, 노래요, 빨개요. One trick, five colors.
- **The shadow split:** 그림자 is the shape you CAST; 그늘 is the shelter you STAND in — English blurs them into "shadow/shade", Korean never does, and the metaphors split the same way (부모님 그늘).
- **One syllable, three careers:** 철 is the season (딸기 철), the sense that comes with age (철이 들다), and the iron in M6's 지하철. The NIKL ledger lists all three; the note sorts them.

## Part 3 — The glue: -겠-, finally named

### 1. Reading the evidence: it's-going-to / must-be

-겠- states what the evidence in front of you says: 하늘 좀 보세요 — 비가 **오겠**어요 (those clouds!) · 맛있**겠**다! (that plate!) · 춥**겠**다 (that coat, in January). This is the forecast's whole register — 내일은 맑**겠**습니다, 기온이 올라가**겠**습니다 — because weather is inference, never plan. **Calibration vs M8:** -(으)ㄹ 거예요 predicts from schedule (비가 올 거예요 — the app said so); -겠- infers from what your eyes see right now. M34's hedge (것 같다) is softest of all; -겠- commits.

### 2. The volunteer's -겠-: I-will

First person, -겠- pledges: 제가 하**겠**습니다 = I'll do it (hand raised, will engaged) — stronger than 할 거예요's mere plan. Now retro-decode two old formulas: M18's mealtime vow 잘 먹**겠**습니다 = "I WILL eat well" — gratitude with will engaged — and M28's departure formula 학교 다녀오**겠**습니다, the child's pledge at the door. You've been conjugating volition for thirty modules.

### 3. Softened knowing: 알겠습니다 / 모르겠어요

The politeness job: 알**겠**습니다 ("understood" — literally *I would know*, gentler than a bare 알아요) and 모르**겠**어요 ("I really couldn't say" — softer than 몰라요's shrug). Both have haunted this course's notes since M29's 아, 알겠어요; today they're officially yours. When in doubt in Korea, 알겠습니다 is the correct noise.
