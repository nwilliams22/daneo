# Korean, Word-First — Module 11: Weather and the Four Seasons

Welcome to Band 2. The grammar engine is built; from here on, modules are **fuel**: ~40 words on one theme, a single light grammar garnish, and sentences that run entirely on machinery you already own. First tank of fuel: the weather — Korea's favorite small-talk subject and a national identity (사계절, the proud four seasons).

**How to use this:** Band 2 modules can be taken in any order. This one earns its place first because 날씨 talk opens every conversation you'll ever have in Korea — and because it finally *formalizes* the ㅂ-irregular you've been collecting since 맵다.

---

## Part 1 — A year in Korea

Each season has a signature you can now name:

- **봄** (spring) — 벚꽃: two weeks of cherry blossoms and the whole country outdoors under them
- **여름** (summer) — 장마 first (weeks of rain), then heat that earns its 너무; 바다, 휴가, and the sacred 에어컨
- **가을** (fall) — 단풍: the mountains (70% of the country) turn red-gold and everyone hikes
- **겨울** (winter) — 눈, 스키, air so 건조하다 that humidifiers count as furniture

Plus the modern fifth season: **미세먼지** (fine dust) — checking its forecast is as routine as checking for rain.

## Part 3 — The glue

### 1. The ㅂ-irregular family, formalized

You've met the members one at a time; here's the rule that unites them: **ㅂ at the end of a stem becomes 우 before a vowel** — then 우 + 어 contracts to 워:

| Adjective | Polite |
|-----------|--------|
| 덥다 (hot) | 더**워요** |
| 춥다 (cold) | 추**워요** |
| 맵다 (spicy, M3) | 매워요 |
| 가깝다 (near, M6) | 가까워요 |
| 쉽다 / 어렵다 (M7) | 쉬워요 / 어려워요 |

Two footnotes: 돕다 uses 와 instead of 워 (도**와**주세요 — you've been conjugating it since M9), and a few ㅂ-stems are secretly regular — 입다 (M8) stays 입**어요**. When in doubt, the vocab note tells you.

### 2. The degree ladder

조금 (a little) < **아주** (very) < **너무** (too/so) — parked right before the adjective: 아주 더워요, 너무 추워요. Plus the reaction twins **진짜 / 정말** (really): 진짜요?! And to ask about degree: **얼마나** — 얼마나 더워요? (how hot is it?)

### 3. Weather arrives

No dummy "it" — weather acts for itself: 비가 **와요** (rain comes), 눈이 **왔어요** (snow came), 바람이 **불어요** (wind blows), 비가 **그쳤어요** (the rain stopped itself). The gap deck files the pattern.

### 4. Seasons take time-에

봄**에** (in spring), 여름**에**, 겨울**에** — same 에 as 아침에 and 주말에. The time-word system just keeps absorbing new members.

**Watch how much is old.** The only new machinery here is vocabulary — the can-machine, -(으)니까, 부터, 타다, and the copula all just keep working.

## Part 5 — Practice

Cover the right column and translate; then flip and build the Korean.

1. Summer is very hot. → 여름은 아주 더워요.
2. Winter is so cold. → 겨울은 너무 추워요.
3. It's raining now. → 지금 비가 와요.
4. It snowed yesterday. → 어제 눈이 왔어요.
5. The sky is clear today. → 오늘은 하늘이 맑아요.
6. In spring the cherry blossoms are beautiful. → 봄에 벚꽃이 예뻐요.
7. It's raining, so let's take a taxi. → 비가 오니까 택시를 탑시다.
8. My vacation starts next week. → 다음 주부터 휴가예요.

Window check: look outside and report, out loud — sky, temperature, precipitation. Three sentences. You're a weather station now.
