# Korean, Word-First — Module 76: Weights, Measures, and Where Things Sit

M30's 미터 and M49's 온도 opened the measuring shelf; this module ships its last three drawers. The metric loans (그램, 킬로그램, 킬로, 밀리미터, 킬로미터, 톤) and the thermometer (섭씨, 영하) finish what the meter and the degree started; the number ladder tops out at 억 and learns to be vague (몇십, 한둘); five number-nouns (숫자, 계산, 절반, 다수, 일등) and eight more counters (인분, 부, 통, 송이, 호선, 년도, 엔 — and the clipped 땜) fill the drawer M5 opened; and eight position words (가로, 세로, 위아래, 중간, 정상, 꼭대기, 곁, 너머) carry the position nouns of M6 (위, 아래, 옆) to the summit and the far side. The organizing thesis: **Korean measures with borrowed units, counts with native and Sino numbers, and places things with nouns, not prepositions** — M6's 위 note said it in one clause, 'A NOUN, not a preposition', and this whole shelf obeys it. No new grammar; two old rules, run thirty-two times.

**How to use this:** say the number rule once and let the notes prove it — Sino in front of every unit, label, and portion (십 킬로, 이호선, 이 인분), native in front of the things you can hold (수박 한 통, 꽃 한 송이, 보고서 두 부). Spend your reps on the radars the shelf stacks: 억 that counts vs the 억 that remembers, 정상 the peak vs 정상 the normal, 영상 the temperature vs 영상 the video, and 너머 the noun vs 넘어 the verb.

## Part 3 — Two rules, no new grammar

### 1. Sino in front of every unit

M30's 미터 rule — 'Rides like a counter, Sino numbers in front' — is the law for all six loans, the thermometer, and the labels: 백 그램, 오 킬로그램, 십 킬로, 영하 십 도, 섭씨 이십오 도, 이 인분, 이호선, 2026년도, 만 엔, 십억. M30's 서른 filed the split — 'Ages native, money Sino — the split holds' — and every unit sides with money. The native numbers keep what they always had: the things you hold and count one by one — 수박 **한** 통, 꽃 **한** 송이, 보고서 **두** 부, 편지 **한** 통. The test is the counter, not the noun: 세 명이 오 인분을 먹었어요 — native heads, Sino plates.

### 2. Places are nouns — hang them after, then add the particle

M6's 위 note settled it: 'A NOUN, not a preposition'. Every position word since has been a noun you hang after another noun and then case-mark: 산 **정상에** 올랐어요, 창문 **너머로** 보여요, 엄마 **곁에** 있어요, 위아래**로** 흔들어요, 영화 **중간에** 나갔어요. English puts the relation first (beyond the mountain); Korean names the place (the mountain's far side) and points at it with 에 or 로. **The spelling law:** 너머 is the noun (the far side); 넘어 is M36's 넘다 wearing -아/어 (the crossing). 산 너머에 있어요 / 산을 넘어 가요 — natives confuse them constantly, and the spacing interlude's confession applies one rule over.

**Calibration:** 킬로 means kilograms at the scale and kilometers on the road — the noun beside it decides (몸무게 몇 킬로 / 십 킬로 달렸어요). Three tops, three registers: 정상 for climbers and news anchors, 꼭대기 for children and storytellers, 맨 위 (M55's 맨 on M6's 위) for plain description. And 땜: texts and 반말 (M19) only — 때문 in full on anything a 부장님 will read.

## What's next

The measuring shelf is closed: you can read a scale, a forecast, a subway map, a receipt, and a housing headline in their own units, and you can put a thing on top of, in the middle of, beyond, or beside anything — with a noun. The long tail keeps unspooling, root by root. 산 너머에 뭐가 있는지 — go and see. 정상에서 봐요.
