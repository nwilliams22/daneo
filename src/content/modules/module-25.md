# Korean, Word-First — Module 25: Dates and Life Events

Module 24 named two badges and teased a third. Here it lands: **-(으)ㄹ 때** — *when* — plus the date machine 년/월/일, and the days Korean life actually orbits: 설날, 추석, birthdays counted in soup, and the two parties a baby attends before it can walk.

> **Warm-up (Module 24):** build these out loud — Module 24 blocks, combinations you've never seen written: *What kind of story is it?* (어떤 이야기예요?) · *Do you have something similar?* (비슷한 것 있어요?) · *It'll probably be different* (아마 다를 거예요). If the blocks don't snap together, visit Review first.

**How to use this:** dates are pure recycling — every month is a Sino number you've owned since Module 2, and the only new mouth-work is two smoothed syllables (유월, 시월). Learn the frame in ten minutes, then spend the module on the holidays.

---

## Part 1 — The calendar is numbers you already own

Korean dates run **big to small** — 년 → 월 → 일, year-month-day, the order computers sort by. Korea agreed with the machines decades early:

- 이천이십육 년 시월 구 일 — October 9, 2026 (한글날 — the alphabet's own holiday)
- Months have no names, just numbers: 일월, 이월, 삼월… 십이월. Two got sanded smooth for the mouth: June is **유월** (not 육월), October is **시월** (not 십월).
- The asking machines: 오늘 며칠이에요? (what's the date?) · 생일이 몇 월 며칠이에요? · 생일이 언제예요?

And mind the radar: **일** now has three jobs — one (M2), work (일하다), and day. A number in front means date; 하다 behind means job; context referees.

## Part 3 — The glue

### 1. The date machine and the year-line

[Sino]년 [Sino]월 [Sino]일 — assembled entirely from Module 2 parts. The year-line runs 작년 → 올해 → 내년 (last year, this year, next year — 내년 sharing 내일's coming-내), and the week-line 지난주 → 이번 주 → 다음 주. That 지난 is M12's 지나다 wearing M24's past badge, fused into a word: *the passed-week*. The badge builds vocabulary now, not just phrases.

### 2. -(으)ㄹ 때 — when

때 is the when-noun. After an event, it stands bare: 추석 **때** (at Chuseok), 시험 **때**, 그**때** (back then). After a verb, the third badge carries it: batchim → -을 때, no batchim → -ㄹ 때:

- **어릴 때** 부산에 살았어요 — when I was little, I lived in Busan (M21's 어리다)
- 시간이 **있을 때** 전화해 주세요 — call when you have time
- 학교에 **갈 때** 음악을 들어요 — I listen to music when I go to school

(ㄹ-stems already end in ㄹ — 살다 → 살 때, nothing doubles.)

### 3. The badge before any noun

-(으)ㄹ marks a *yet-to-happen* action, and 때 isn't special — any noun takes it: **할 일** (things to do), **먹을 것** (something to eat), **마실 것** (something to drink). Now run the X-ray one last time: 떠날 **거예요** = 떠나다 + badge + 거 (것!) + 예요 — *"a to-leave thing is."* M8's future tense was badge-plus-thing-plus-copula all along, and M10's 할 수 있어요 was "a to-do way exists." All three badges named; every old mystery has an owner.

## Part 4 — Building sentences

**Spot the recycling:** 내일은 아주 특별한 날이에요 runs M24's badge over M11's 날, 지난주 결혼식 어땠어요? fuses M24's machinery into the calendar and asks with 어땠어요, and 젊을 때 여행을 많이 하세요 sends M24's 젊다 through the new when-machine.

::sentences::

**Party check (gap deck):** 미역국 — birthday soup and exam jinx · 돌잡이 — the baby picks its future · 백일 — the hundred days worth a party. Korea counts what it treasures, in days.

## Part 5 — Practice

Cover the right column and translate; then flip and build the Korean.

1. What's the date today? → 오늘은 몇 월 며칠이에요?
2. My birthday is October 9th. → 제 생일은 시월 구 일이에요.
3. Happy birthday! → 생일 축하해요!
4. Happy New Year! → 새해 복 많이 받으세요!
5. When I was little, I lived in Busan. → 어릴 때 부산에 살았어요.
6. Call me when you have time. → 시간이 있을 때 전화해 주세요.
7. I have a lot to do this weekend. → 주말에 할 일이 많아요.
8. While you're young, travel a lot. → 젊을 때 여행을 많이 하세요.

Date drill: say your own birthday aloud — year included — then work through your family's. Then find this year's 설날 and 추석 on a 달력; they move (lunar calendar), which is why the 달력 marks them for you.
