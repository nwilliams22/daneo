# Korean, Word-First — Module 64: The Rule Book

Band 6 keeps slicing the long tail, and this slice is the institution's voice — the words Korean runs its offices, firms, and borders on. Two spines carry the load: a **rule ladder** (명령 hands down an order, 허용하다 lets it through, 위반 fires the moment you cross it) and an **application axis** (신청 → 등록 → 제출 → 확인 → 전달 — file it, register it, submit it, confirm it, get it relayed). Around them, the people and places the rules govern: worker (근로자), youth (청년), the big firm (대기업), the population (인구); and the two exchanges the module splits clean — 교환 (a swap, hand over) vs. 교류 (a flow, running back and forth), with 외교 (the crossing over to the outside, nations meeting) riding the same 交. Decision verbs (권하다, 허용하다, 논의하다, 시도하다, 적용하다, 반대하다) and motion verbs (전달하다, 담당하다, 진행하다, 참석하다, 근무하다, 활동하다, 경험하다, 제출하다) complete the kit — the full bureaucratic vocabulary for walking a thing through a system, the way M63's 잘/잘못 family walks it through a judgment.

**How to use this:** the two axes are the module — drill the rule ladder as one axis (command → permission → breach) and the application chain as the other (five verbs in fixed order). Then spend your reps on the pair that looks identical and isn't: 교환 vs. 교류 — same 交, 換 is a swap, 流 is a flow. And watch the 권-family split: 권하다 (advise — from 權, authority) vs. M5's 권 (the book-counter). One syllable, two owners.

## Part 1 — Bricks owned before this module

A few of the words this module leans on were already in place from earlier shelves — no new scaffolding needed, just the right word next to them:

- **M2's 회사** does the work in every sentence that has a doer — the firm is the firm, M2 through and through.
- **M10's 되다** seals the verdict: 위반이 **돼요**, the breach is *sealed*, and 확인이 **돼요**, the check is *through*. Same verb, two different outcomes.
- **M10's -고** chains the five-step application axis: each verb hands off to the next in one breath.
- **M20's -(으)면** gates the conditional: once the line is crossed, the breach fires — the if-stem that turns a rule into a consequence.
- **M33's 경험** does the building in 경험**이 쌓여요** — the experience piles up, with M61's 쌓이다 doing the stacking, and M3's 시키다 forming the causative 활동**시키다**.
- **M6's -로** marks the means: 교류**로** — by trade, not by force. A nation's 신용 is built the same way a person's is, by letting things flow through.
- **M32's 정부** is the doer in the diplomacy sentence — the government permits the exchange, the credit stacks up.
- **M43's 보고서** is the paper form of today's 보고 (the report, minus the 서-document bolt). One verb, two registers.
- **M33's 선택하다** is the verb form of today's 선택 (the noun, the thing picked) — and M33's -게 adverbializer is the same shelf's tool for spinning that choice into "the way it was made."
- **M9's 카드** is the object, not the subject — the worker requests it, the bank permits credit against it.
- **M1's 좋다** sets the bar: 신용이 **좋으면** — good credit, then the card. The simplest condition in Korean, doing the heaviest lifting.

## Part 2 — The rule nouns

::vocab::

**Radar patrol:** The 權 vs. 권 split is the module's sharpest edge — 권**하다** advices from 權 (authority), but M5's **권** is the book-counter, a completely different syllable wearing the same face. The 交 family runs three different jobs in one module: 교환 (交 cross + 換 swap — hand one thing over), 교류 (交 cross + 流 flow — run back and forth), and 외교 (外 outside + 交 crossing — the outside-meeting). Same first syllable, three different second halves, three different meanings. And the 制 family has two jobs: 제한 (制 control + 限 limit — the rule shrunk to its ceiling) and 강제 (强 force + 制 control — the rule shrunk to its fist). The 제 factory that built 제한 is a different 한자 from the 제 'my' in M17 — same sound, different owner.

## Part 3 — The verb machines

### 1. The rule ladder

The ladder runs top to bottom: **명령** (the order you can't ignore) → **허용하다** (the institution lets it through) → **위반** (the breach fires when you cross). One axis: command → permission → breach. The -면 (M20) conditional makes the third rung conditional on the second: if you're permitted, you can proceed; if you cross, the breach fires — M10's 되다 sealing the verdict either way.

### 2. The application axis

The five-step chain, in fixed order: **신청** (file it) → **등록** (register it) → **제출** (submit it) → **확인** (confirm it) → **전달** (get it relayed). M10's -고 chains them into one breath: 신청하고 등록하고 제출하면 확인이 되고 보고가 전달돼요. The -면 (M20) gates the last two — you don't submit until you've registered, and you don't get confirmation until you've submitted. The whole office in one line.

### 3. The people and places the rules govern

**근로자** (worker — the one who does the diligent labor, formal register) and **청년** (youth — the person in the green years) are who the rules apply to. **대기업** (the big firm) is who runs them. **인구** (population — people counted by their mouths) is what the firm reports to. And the two exchanges: **교류** (the flow, running back and forth) vs. **교환** (the swap, hand over) — same 交, 流 is flow, 換 is swap. **외교** is the crossing over to the outside — nations meeting across a line.

### 4. The decision and motion verbs

**권하다** (advise — soft, not a command), **허용하다** (permit), **논의하다** (discuss — the verb you reach for when a choice is still a choice), **시도하다** (attempt — proposing a course of action to the group), **적용하다** (apply the rule — making it fit the case), **반대하다** (oppose — M24's 반대 noun + -하다). Motion: **전달하다** (relay, hand on), **담당하다** (be in charge of, own the duty), **진행하다** (advance, push forward), **참석하다** (attend, take your seat), **근무하다** (work the shift), **활동하다** (be active, operate).
