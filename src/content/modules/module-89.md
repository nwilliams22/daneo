# Korean, Word-First — Module 89: Degree and Extent

M47 handed you 만큼 and called it the equative; M32 handed you 정도 and called it "the abouter"; M55 handed you 뿐 and said only-ness was a noun. Here is the thesis those three were hiding: **Korean scales a sentence with nouns and particles, not adverbs.** English piles up "very", "so", "extremely"; Korean welds a measuring noun onto the clause — to the DEGREE that (정도로), as MUCH as (만큼), ONLY this (뿐), the MORE of it (수록) — and lets the grammar say how far. Around the welds sit the words the written register measures with: ten measure nouns (수준, 규모, 범위, 평균, 단위, 비중, 균형, 증가 and the two big-大 sizers 대형/대규모), two change verbs (증가하다, 줄어들다), seventeen scale adjectives from 상당하다 to 진지하다, and five prose adverbs (도저히, 가령, 예컨대, 더욱이, 나란히) that steer an argument. The glue is the three heavy particles — 조차, 마저, -(이)야말로 — and the three clause-takers above them. Thirty-four Grade-C words, four glue parts, and the sentence Koreans sigh when the hill gets steeper.

**How to use this:** the nouns are hanja arithmetic on characters you own — 水準, 規模, 範圍, 平均, 單位, 比重 — so read each decode once and let the sound do the rest. Drill the adjectives as PAIRS (거대하다/위대하다, 드물다/흔하다, 뛰어나다/못지않다, 완전하다/완벽하다, 심각하다/진지하다): every one is a calibration against a word you already have. Then take the four glue parts as one idea — a noun bolted to a clause — with a different noun each time.

## Part 1 — The bricks: rulers, sizers, and the words that grade

M57 built the native rulers — 크기, 길이, 높이, 무게 — from adjectives, and its md set the chassis: "the thing OWNS its number". Today's rulers are Sino, and they measure what has no length:

- **The measure nouns:** 수준 (水準 — where the water stands = the level), 규모 (規模 — the scale of an undertaking), 범위 (範圍 — the fence around what counts; M72's 확대되다 note carried it "riding frozen"), 평균 (平均 — everything leveled, one number), 단위 (單位 — the one place you count from), 비중 (比重 — the weight of a part against the whole), 균형 (均衡 — the scale-beam, level), 증가 (增加 — the arrow up). 대형 (大型) sizes a THING; 대규모 (大規模) sizes an UNDERTAKING — one truck, one project.
- **The change verbs:** 증가하다 is M35's 늘다 in print; 줄어들다 is M35's 줄다 with M28's 들다 entering — the shrink you can watch. Their mirrors 감소하다 and 늘어나다 (M56) complete the chart.
- **The scale adjectives, in pairs:** 거대하다 (vast — mountains) / 위대하다 (great — people); 엄청나다 (huge, eyebrows up — the mouth) / 상당하다 (considerable — the report, paying M85's 상당히 IOU); 드물다 (rare) / 흔하다 (common — M62's 흔히 was pressed from it); 뛰어나다 (leaps out) / 못지않다 (no less than — M3's 못 with -지 않다 from M28, one word); 풍부하다 (abundant) / 불과하다 (merely — [noun]에 불과하다, the disappointment built in); 강력하다 (powerful — force behind it) beside M63's 강하다 (strong); 일정하다 (constant) / 특정하다 (specific — both on the fix-定 of M33's 결정하다); 완전하다 (whole) beside M39's 완벽하다 (flawless); 철저하다 (thorough — to the 底 bottom) / 엄격하다 (strict — the 格 frame of M44's 합격); 진지하다 (earnest — the person) beside M63's 심각하다 (grave — the situation).
- **The prose adverbs:** 도저히 (cannot possibly — with a negative, joining M39's 절대/전혀), 가령 (supposing) and 예컨대 (for example — M33's 예 with a fossil tail), 더욱이 (moreover — M85's 더욱 note left it frozen), and 나란히 (side by side — the one that measures nothing).

::vocab::

**Radar patrol:** 균형's 형 is 衡 the scale-beam, 대형's 형 is 型 the mould, and neither is M4's 형 the brother · 드물다 has no 물 in it — M1's 물 (water) is a syllable coincidence · 일정하다 (一定, constant) is not M23's 일정 (日程, the schedule): 일정이 일정하지 않아요 uses both · 비중 is 比 + 重, not M11's 비 (rain) + S2's 중 (in the middle of) · 진지하다's 진지 is not the honorific rice in M47's 잡수시다 note · 평균 and 균형 share 均 (even), so the average is the balance's cousin · and 강력하다 is said 강녁하다 — the ㄹ nasalizes exactly as in M64's 명령.

## Part 2 — The glue: -(으)ㄹ수록 — the more, the more

Prospective badge + **수록**: 볼수록, 알수록, 먹을수록, 살수록 (ㄹ-stems keep the ㄹ). The clause it closes is the dial; the main clause is what the dial moves: 볼수록 매력 있어요 (M45's 매력 — "매력이 있어요 = attractive") = the more you look, the more charm there is. Doubled for emphasis with -(으)면 from M20 on the same verb: 하면 할수록 어려워요 (M7's 어렵다) = the more you do it, the harder it gets — the learner's confession. On nouns through the copula: 학생일수록 = the more of a student one is. Two fossils every column runs on: **갈수록** (M1's 가다: as time goes on — gap deck, with a mountain) and 나이가 들수록 — the frame M35's 늙다 note preferred, "나이가 들다 (age comes in — M28's 들다, entering you)" — = the older you get.

**Calibration:** 많이 보면 좋아요 (if you look a lot, it's good) · 볼수록 좋아요 (the MORE you look, the better) · 갈수록 좋아요 (it keeps getting better). The first is a condition; the second and third are a slope.

## Part 3 — The glue: -(으)ㄹ 정도로 / -(으)ㄹ 만큼 — to the extent that

Two nouns you own, taking a clause. M32's 정도 ("程度 measure-degree: the abouter") after a NOUN says about — 십 분 정도 (M2's 십, M5's 분) = about ten minutes; after a badge-clause it says how far: 죽을 **정도로** 힘들어요 (M14's 죽다, M15's 힘들다) = so hard I could die; 믿을 수 없을 정도로 = unbelievably. M47's 만큼 does the same job — that md's rule, "만큼 EQUATES (형만큼 커요: a tie, measured)", holds when a clause stands where the noun was: 먹을 **만큼**만 가져가세요 (M52's 가져가다, M30's 만) = take only as much as you'll eat; 놀랄 만큼 (M26's 놀라다) = surprisingly, to a startling degree. Both nouns take the prospective badge; 정도로 measures INTENSITY, 만큼 measures AMOUNT, and in most sentences they swap without loss — English reaches for an adverb (아주 힘들어요); Korean reaches for a noun and a clause. M42's 그만큼 was the frozen preview — "그렇게 (M33) points at manner; 그만큼 points at amount".

## Part 4 — The glue: -(으)ㄹ 뿐(만 아니라) — only, and not only

M55's 뿐 drew the line — "만 picks one item off the menu; 뿐 declares the menu has one line" — and taught its two seats: [noun]뿐이다 (너뿐이야) and badge + 뿐이다: 그저 볼 **뿐이에요** (M85's 그저) = I merely watch. Today the third seat: **-(으)ㄹ 뿐만 아니라** = not only… (but also): 가격이 쌀 **뿐만 아니라** 맛도 좋아요 (M21's 가격, M7's 싸다, M18's 맛) = not only is it cheap, it tastes good too. Anatomy: 뿐 + 만 (M30) + 아니라 (M19's 아니다 in its -아/어 coat) = it is not only-this — and the second clause wants 도 from M2 on its noun to land the "also". On nouns it drops the badge: 학생뿐만 아니라 선생님도 = not only students but teachers too. RADAR against 밖에 from M39: that md's rule was "만 states an amount; 밖에 laments it" and "밖에 있어요 is broken Korean" — 밖에 DEMANDS a negative; 뿐 takes a plain closer. 물밖에 없어요 and 물뿐이에요 say the same thing, one complaining, one stating.

## Part 5 — The glue: 조차, 마저, -(이)야말로 — the heavy particles

Three particles that, like 도 from M2 ("REPLACES 은/는/이/가/을/를 — never stacks on them"), sit where the case particle sat. Two are evens with a slant; the third is a crown:

| Particle | Owner | Says | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 도 | M2 | also, even (neutral) | 물도 못 마셔요 — water too |
| 까지 | M6 | all the way to, even (a reach) | 물까지 못 마셔요 — even water, that far |
| 조차 | this module | even (the least expected — negative contexts) | 물조차 못 마셔요 — not even water |
| 마저 | this module | even (the LAST one — the final straw) | 물마저 떨어졌어요 — even the water ran out |

**조차** names the bottom of the scale — the item so basic that failing it means failing everything: 이름조차 몰라요 (M1's 이름, M4's 모르다) = doesn't even know the name; it almost always closes negative. **마저** names the last item — everything else already gone, and now this too: 마지막 버스마저 놓쳤어요 = missed even the last bus; on a person it is the betrayal particle (너마저 — gap deck). M6's 까지 ("여섯 시까지 (until 6) · 학교까지 (as far as school)") reaches; 조차 sinks; 마저 finishes. **-(이)야말로** is the crown, and it pays M85's 그야말로 note — "a Band 7 pattern still in the mail, so hear it here, don't build with it yet" — the mail has arrived: 건강이야말로 = health, of all things, is what…; 지금이야말로 (M2's 지금) = now, if ever; consonant-final nouns take 이야말로, vowel-final 야말로. It replaces the subject particle and says THIS one, above all the candidates.

**Calibration:** 너도 (you as well) · 너까지 (even you — you too, now) · 너조차 (even you, of all people) · 너마저 (even you, the last) · 너야말로 (YOU are the one). Five particles on one pronoun, from a shrug to a crown.

## Part 6 — Building sentences

**Spot the recycling:** -아/어지다 from M35 turns 크다 into the slope 커질수록; the can-machine from M10 runs twice, negated, under 믿을 수 없을 정도로 and 갈 수 없었어요; the double subject M24 first showed (한식은 반찬이 많아요) carries the doctor's two virtues; -아/어서 from Ring 1 sets the cause under 엄격해서 and 놓쳐서; -지만 from M7 concedes the ten customers; -기 위해서 from M44 aims the inspection; the -게 adverbializer from M33 hands 철저하다 to its verb; and M85's 상당히 and M86's 피해 return from the last two shelves.
