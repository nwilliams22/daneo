# Korean, Word-First — Module 107: Native Verbs IV, and the Adjectives

M94 opened the native verb shelf with motion and mishap, M102 welded old verbs into new ones, and M104 pulled the two parties apart at the seams. This is the fourth shelf — the last of the native verbs — and it arrives with the adjectives that keep them company. Thirty-six Grade-C words, no new grammar, and one instruction that does most of the work for you: **do the sum before you read the note.** Ten of today's words are arithmetic on words you already hold — 뒤 + 늦다, 맛 + 보다, 힘 + 쓰다, 뛰다 + 넘다, 집다 + 넣다, 돌다 + 다니다, 빠지다 + 나오다, 하다 + 내다, 다르다 + 없다, 폭 + 넓다 — and two more look like sums and are not, which is the other half of the lesson.

**How to use this:** cover the right-hand column of the weld table and read the left one aloud; whatever you guess will be close enough to keep. Then take the four drawers in order and spend your reps on the Sino adjectives, because each is two characters and you own most of them already — 貴 and 抑 are firsts, while 鬱, 堂, 健 and 全 all landed in earlier modules. Six earlier notes wrote IOUs on this shelf, and today they clear: M74's 멋 promised 멋지다, M94's 빠져나가다 promised 빠져나오다, M101's 코드 promised 꽂다, M74's 귀엽다 ran the bare 척 before 척하다 existed, M88's 깨닫다 ran 뒤늦게 before 뒤늦다 existed, and M88's 한마디 spent a proverb's 빚 without owning 갚다. Read the Radar patrol slowly: this shelf is built almost entirely out of syllables you have already met doing other jobs.

## Part 1 — Native verbs IV, and the adjectives

The weld table first. Two taught words, joined bare or on -아/어, and the meaning is the sum:

| Weld | = | Reads as |
|---|---|---|
| 뒤늦다 | M6's 뒤 + M8's 늦다 | late from behind = belated, after the point |
| 맛보다 | M18's 맛 + M1's 보다 | put a taste in the eye = taste, experience |
| 힘쓰다 | M14's 힘 + M10's 쓰다 | spend strength = strive, work at |
| 뛰어넘다 | M28's 뛰다 + M36's 넘다 | cross it with a jump in it = leap over, transcend |
| 집어넣다 | M66's 집다 + M28's 넣다 | pick it up and put it in = stuff in |
| 돌아다니다 | M37's 돌다 + M16's 다니다 | go round and round as a routine = wander about |
| 빠져나오다 | M35's 빠지다 + M28's 나오다 | fall out and come this way = get out |
| 해내다 | M1's 하다 + M28's 내다 | do it and put the doing out = pull it off |
| 다름없다 | M24's 다르다 + -ㅁ + M1's 없다 | no difference exists = as good as |
| 폭넓다 | M60's 폭 + M13's 넓다 | wide in the width = broad, wide-ranging |

Two more sit in the same drawer and refuse to add up. **뒤지다** carries M6's 뒤 and nothing else you can name: it rummages (가방을 뒤지다, the NIKL frame) and, from the other end of that same 뒤, it lags behind (뒤지지 않다 = second to none). **맞이하다** is 맞이 (a welcoming — riding frozen) + M1's 하다, and its 맞 is a greet-맞다, a different verb from M15's 맞다 that happens to share a spelling; 새해를 맞이하다 (M25's 새해) is the sentence every December broadcast runs. Then the other three drawers:

- **THE HAND (날리다 덜다 배다 새다 갚다 끼우다 다듬다 헤매다 꽂다):** nine things a hand or the weather does to a thing — make it fly, take some off, soak it through, let it leak, pay it back, slot it in, trim it, lose the way, stand it upright. Two are party products in disguise: 날리다 is M28's 날다 and 끼우다 is M21's 끼다, both through the causative party of M37, and 끼우다 wears the 우 that module recruited. The rest are plain native verbs with famous collocations: 부담을 덜다 (M86's 부담), 냄새가 배다 (M41's 냄새), 비가 새다 (M11's 비), 빚을 갚다, 머리를 다듬다 (M8's 머리), 길을 헤매다 (M12's 길), 꽃을 꽂다 (M7's 꽃).
- **THE ADJECTIVES (두렵다 멋지다 환하다 귀하다 억울하다 당당하다 건전하다):** three native and four Sino. Native: 두렵다 (the fear you admit, ㅂ-irregular), 멋지다 (M74's 멋 + -지다), 환하다 (light that opens a face). Sino, and each decodes: 귀하다 is 貴 alone (a first), 억울하다 is 抑 (a first) + the 鬱 of M34's 우울하다, 당당하다 is the 堂 of M65's 강당 doubled, 건전하다 is sound-健 plus the all-全 of M31's 전국.
- **THE AUXILIARY AND THE ADVERBS (척하다 저절로 거듭 이윽고 모처럼 꽉):** one auxiliary — 척하다, the badge -(으)ㄴ/-는 from M24 plus 척하다, which pretends where M87's 듯하다 merely seems — and five adverbs the page reaches for before the mouth does: by itself (저절로), over and over (거듭), before long (이윽고), for once in a long while (모처럼), and tight (꽉).
- **TWO NOUNS (흉내 덩어리):** the copy made with the body, which rides M28's 내다 (흉내를 내다), and the lump of matter with no shape of its own, which sits against M60's 조각 — a 조각 broke off something, a 덩어리 never did.

::vocab::

**Radar patrol:** 새다 leaks, and the 새다 inside M38's 밤새다 dawns — that note glossed it "새다 (to dawn)" — two verbs, one spelling, and neither is M13's 새 (new) or M22's 새 (the bird) · 배다 is a verb; M3's 배 is the stomach, the boat and the pear · 귀하다 is 貴 and has nothing to do with M14's 귀 (the ear), while M74's 귀엽다 is native and holds no 貴 at all · 당당하다 stands tall, M86's 당하다 has something land on you — three shared letters, opposite ends of one story · the 매 of 헤매다 is not M60's 매다 (to tie) · 꽂다 (ㅈ) sticks the 꽃 (ㅊ) of M7 into a vase, and both say 꼳 · 모처럼 contains M41's 처럼 by pure accident · the 지다 of 뒤지다 is not M17's 지다 (to lose) and not the -아/어지다 wing from M35 · 날리다 is 날다 with the party's 리, not M11's 날 (a day) · 억울하다's 억 presses; M76's 억 (億) counts to a hundred million · and M88's 전환 carries 환 without a scrap of 환하다's light in it — that one is 轉換, to switch over.

**Calibration, two ladders.** The fear: 무서워요 (M24's 무섭다 — the corridor, the film, said out loud) · 두려워요 (today — the future, the verdict, written down). The fullness: 가득 (M62 — the level the contents reached) · 꽉 (today — the pressure they are under) · 잔뜩 (M62 — the amount somebody brought). A glass is 가득, a rush-hour carriage is 꽉, and a Korean grandmother's table is 잔뜩.

## Part 2 — Building sentences

**Spot the recycling:** every sentence rides one Band 7 pattern and names it — -(으)ㄹ수록 from M89 turns 돌아다니다 into a slope that widens 지식; -더라고요 from M88 reports a friend who is 예전과 다름없다 and nobody else's feeling; -길래 from M93 puts the child's 흉내 in the witnessed clause and the speaker's 척하다 in the main one; -(으)ㄴ/는 데다가 from M92 piles a leak from the 천장 on top of a smell that 배다-ed into the room; -고도 from M85 concedes a debt actually 갚다-ed while the 억울하다 stays; -(으)ㄹ 겸 from M91 gets two errands out of one sitting down, 다듬다 and 꽂다; -다 보니 from M94 keeps you 헤매다-ing until 이윽고 something 환하다 turns up; and 반면에 from M95 balances a brother who 해내다-s against a speaker who is 두렵다. Underneath sit the frames you have held since Ring 1: the badge -(으)ㄴ/-는 from M24 on 만난, 밴, 자는, 환한 and 해내는, the -아/어지다 machine from M35 closing 폭넓어져요, the -게 adverbializer from M33 on 당당하게, the long negation from M28 on 사라지지 않았어요, and M2's 도 and M6's 까지 hiding particles all over the place.

**Gap deck:** the manoeuvre every office runs on (모르는 척하다), the cry with no English word behind it (억울해요), and the money that left through the window (돈을 날리다).

## What's next

The native verb shelf closes here. What you have is a method rather than a list: read a long native verb as two short ones, run the party check on anything ending in 이/히/리/기/우, decode a two-character adjective before you memorize it, and let the Radar patrol keep the homographs apart. Band 8 goes on down the Grade-C list — more of the page's adverbs, the last of the abstract shelf, and the words the newspaper cannot do without. 모처럼 여기까지 왔으니 이곳저곳 돌아다녀 보세요; 계속 읽으세요.
