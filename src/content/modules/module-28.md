# Korean, Word-First — Module 28: In and Out, Up and Down

The sweep's verb module. Korean's most-used motion verbs aren't new words — they're **compounds of verbs you've owned since Band 1**, snapped onto M1's 가다/오다. And the garnish is the third and final negation: **-지 않다**, the long form that's been hiding in plain sight since 괜찮아요.

> **Warm-up (Module 27):** build these out loud — Module 27 blocks, combinations you've never seen written: *I gave a present to the professor* (교수님께 선물을 드렸어요) · *Which direction is east?* (어느 쪽이 동쪽이에요?) · *My wife is Canadian* (아내는 캐나다 사람이에요). If the blocks don't snap together, visit Review first.

**How to use this:** twenty-five verbs sounds like a flood — but ten of them are self-assembling compounds, and the rest are the hands-and-happenings verbs daily life runs on. Learn the assembly rule first; half the checklist becomes free.

---

## Part 1 — Verbs snap together

The rule: **[action] + 가다/오다** — the action, plus which way it travels relative to the speaker. M6's come/go compass, installed inside verbs:

| away from me | toward me | the action |
|---|---|---|
| 들어**가다** go in | 들어**오다** come in | entering |
| 나가다 (M8!) go out | 나**오다** come out | exiting |
| 올라**가다** go up | — | climbing |
| 내려**가다** go down | 내려**오다** come down | descending |
| 걸어**가다** walk (away) | 걸어**오다** walk (here) | M6's 걷다 |

Two specials: **다녀오다** — go-and-come, the errand verb (see the gap deck for the goodbye every Korean child says) — and **가져오다** — have-and-come: *bring* (M24's 가지다 doing the having). M12's 갈아타다 and M21's 갈아입다 were this machine all along; now you hold the assembly line.

## Part 2 — The bricks

The compounds, the hands verbs, and the inside-words.

::vocab::

**The 나다 party:** 나다 — to emerge, sprout, erupt — has been inside your vocabulary for ten modules: 화가 **나**다 (anger erupts, M19), 기억**나**다 (memory surfaces, M19), 소리가 **나**다 (a sound comes off). 나오다 is just 나다 + 오다: emerge toward me. Things HAPPEN to you in Korean by coming out at you.

## Part 3 — The glue

### 1. -지 않다 — the long negation

Ring 1's negation shelf, complete: **안** (M3, quick), **못** (M3, can't), **-지 마세요** (M14, don't!) — and now **-지 않다**, the long form. Stem + -지 않다, tense goes at the end:

- 김치찌개는 별로 맵**지 않아요** — not that spicy (별로's favorite partner)
- 저는 고기를 먹**지 않아요** — M3's 안 먹어요 in its formal suit: same meaning, longer jacket
- Past: 어제는 춥**지 않았어요** — wasn't cold

안 is for speed, -지 않다 for writing and weight. The -지 shelf now holds three: -지 마세요, -지만 (M7), -지 않다 — one stem, three machines.

### 2. States arrive like weather

M11 taught 비가 와요 — rain comes. The pattern is bigger: **잠이 와요** (sleep comes), **문제가 생겼어요** (a problem sprouted — 생기다), 화가 나요 (M19). Korean's inner life is meteorology: states show up on their own; you just report the forecast. The gap deck files 잠이 안 와요 — the 3 a.m. sentence.

## Part 4 — Building sentences

**Spot the recycling:** 해는 동쪽에서 나와요 runs the new 나오다 over M27's compass, 방학 동안 할머니 댁에 다녀왔어요 sends the errand verb to M27's honorific 댁, and 우체국은 은행하고 서점 가운데에 있어요 parks M27's buildings around the new middle-word.

::sentences::

**Formula check (gap deck):** 학교 다녀오겠습니다 — "I'll go to school and come back" — the departure formula, with M18's -겠- inside. The reply is 다녀와.

## Part 5 — Practice

Cover the right column and translate; then flip and build the Korean.

1. The sun comes up in the east. → 해는 동쪽에서 나와요.
2. You can't go in right now. → 지금 들어가면 안 돼요.
3. Kimchi stew isn't that spicy. → 김치찌개는 별로 맵지 않아요.
4. I don't eat meat. → 저는 고기를 먹지 않아요.
5. I put the book in the bag. → 가방 속에 책을 넣었어요.
6. A problem came up! → 문제가 생겼어요!
7. Please bring your passport. → 여권을 가져오세요.
8. During vacation I visited grandma's house. → 방학 동안 할머니 댁에 다녀왔어요.

Assembly drill: take 뛰다 (run) and build both directions — 뛰어가다, 뛰어오다 — then narrate someone entering, climbing, and leaving your building, choosing 가다 or 오다 by where YOU stand.
