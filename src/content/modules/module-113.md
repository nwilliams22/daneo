# Korean, Word-First — Module 113: The Words Without Halves

Every module since Band 7 opened has handed you a decoder. A Sino word splits into two characters you have already met; a long native verb splits into two short ones; an adjective splits into a root and a factory. This module hands you thirty-six words that do none of that. 닥치다 is not 닥 plus 치다; 만만하다 is not 만 plus 만; 개미 is not 개 plus 미. They are native roots — whole, old, and unsplittable — and the arithmetic that has carried you through two hundred Sino words does nothing here at all. So the thesis is about method rather than machinery: **a native word pays in the picture and the collocation.** With no halves to take apart, a note has two currencies left — what the word looks like HAPPENING, and the three or four nouns it always turns up beside — and every note below spends both.

**How to use this:** read the receipts table first, because eleven of today's words were named by earlier notes and carried frozen, and meeting them as payments rather than as new vocabulary is half the work done. Then take the five drawers in order and drill each word against its collocation rather than its English gloss — 눈이 마주치다, 마음에 새기다, 빚을 갚다, 집안의 기둥, 늦기 일쑤예요 — because that is the only handle a word without halves gives you. The Radar patrol at the end is longer than usual and it is the most important thing on the page: native roots collide constantly, and eight of today's words wear a shape you already own for something else.

**Receipts.** Eleven earlier notes wrote an IOU on this shelf. All eleven clear today.

Then the five drawers:

- **THE HAND AND THE BLADE (닥치다 엎드리다 마주치다 베다 메우다 새기다 중얼거리다 긋다 엿보다 잡아먹다 건드리다):** what a body does to the world when there is no hanja to explain it. Three of the eleven are welds you can still see — 마주치다 is M68's 마주 + M17's 치다, 잡아먹다 is M28's 잡다 + M1's 먹다, 엿보다 is a peeking prefix on M1's 보다 — and the other eight are single roots. Two carry NIKL hints worth keeping: 닥치다 is filed under 다다르다 (to arrive at) and 메우다 as '메다'의 사동사. 긋다 is ㅅ-irregular, and the M60 gap deck already wrote the conjugation out: "긋다 (to draw a line, riding frozen) is ㅅ-irregular, seat four in the family M14's 낫다 opened and M40's 젓다 and M54's 짓다 filled: 그어요, 그은."
- **THE ADJECTIVES (아깝다 어리석다 우습다 힘차다 고요하다 날카롭다 만만하다 못되다):** three ㅂ-irregulars (아깝다 우습다 날카롭다 — 아까워요, 우스워요, 날카로워요), two that are really about judging a person (어리석다, 못되다), and three that exist mostly to be sorted against a word you already hold: 고요하다 against M13's 조용하다, 우습다 against M19's 웃기다, 만만하다 against nothing at all, because it lives in the negative.
- **THE MANY AND THE SORTS (여럿 갖가지 이런저런):** three ways to say a lot of assorted things. 여럿 is the lesson in one particle — M29's 여러 needs a noun to lean on, 여럿 stands alone and takes 이. 이런저런 closes the paired-determiner rail: M50's 여기저기 for places, M55's 이것저것 for things, 이런저런 for kinds.
- **THE BODY AND THE BOX (몸통 볼 칸 기둥 뒷모습 괴로움):** four solid objects and two nouns built by a machine — 뒷모습 is M6's 뒤 + M34's 모습 across a linking ㅅ, and 괴로움 is M42's 괴롭다 through the -(으)ㅁ noun freezer from M67, whose md wrote the rule this word obeys: "the ㅂ-family softens first (어렵다 → 어려움, 부끄럽다 → 부끄러움)".
- **THE PEOPLE AND THE NOISE (빚 개미 하느님 차림 그대 아냐 아유 일쑤):** the shelf where the receipts pile up. 차림 is M40's 차리다 through the same -(으)ㅁ freezer; 아냐 is M71's 아니야 with a syllable knocked out; 일쑤 and 그대 are dependent on other words entirely — 일쑤 lives only behind the -기 nominalizer from M13, and 그대 lives only on a page.

::vocab::

**Radar patrol:** 베다 cuts with a blade, and its homograph 베다 rests your head on a pillow — 베개 is the noun this second one built, so 베개를 베다 is not a typo · spaced 못 되다 (M3's 못 + M10's 되다) is failed-to-become; solid 못되다 is nasty, and the space is the whole difference · 볼 is a cheek and also M1's 보다 under the prospective badge (볼 거예요 — the future from M8), and only the cheek takes a particle · 메우다 fills a hole and is not M66's 메다, which carries a bag on the shoulder · the 새 in 새기다 is a verb root, not M13's 새 (new) or M22's 새 (bird) · the 차다 welded inside 힘차다 is the be-full one M37's 채우다 decoded, not M66's 차다, the kick · 그대 has nothing to do with M54's 그대로, which that note decoded as "그 + 대로: as-it-is" · 개미 is one native word and decodes into neither M5's 개 nor M96's 미 · the 치다 in 닥치다 is not M17's 치다 · the 드리다 in 엎드리다 and 건드리다 is not M9's 드리다 · the 건 in 건드리다 is not M99's 건 · the 만 in 만만하다 is neither M9's 만 nor M30's 만 · the 날 in 날카롭다 is the blade-root, not M11's 날 · the 통 in 몸통 is the trunk, not M76's 통 · and 어리석다 has no relation whatever to M21's 어리다.

## Part 2 — Building sentences

**Spot the recycling:** every sentence rides one Band 7 pattern and names it, and each one comes from a different module — -기 일쑤다 from M94 finally gets its dependent noun and puts 중얼거리다 on repeat; -(으)ㄹ까 봐 from M93 keeps a hand away from a 날카로운 칼 before 베다 can happen; -더라고요 from M88 reports a room full of students 엎드려 on their desks; -(으)ㄹ수록 from M89 turns 빚 and M107's 갚다 into a sliding scale; -(으)며 from M85 prints 고요하다 and 그대 in the one register either of them belongs to; -는 김에 from M91 hands 메우다 the 빈칸 that M37's 채우다 has been holding; -기는커녕 from M92 cancels 만만하다 and puts 못되다 in its place; and -기 마련이다 from M87 lets 여럿 gather until 이런저런 talk comes out. Underneath sit the frames you have held since Ring 1: the badges -(으)ㄴ/-는 from M24 on 고요한, 쓰는 and the 빈 inside 빈칸, -아/어서 from M10 carrying the sharp knife, -아/어지다 from M35 closing 가벼워져요, the -기 nominalizer from M13 under two of the eight, and the 합니다체 of M4 on the one written line.

**Gap deck:** the half-second that starts every drama (눈이 마주치다), the promise you carve rather than make (마음에 새기다), and the two syllables to recognize and never say (입 닥쳐).

## What's next

You now have a method for the words that refuse to be decoded: find the picture, learn the three nouns it stands beside, and check the Radar patrol before you trust a familiar-looking syllable. It is the same discipline the Sino shelves taught, run without the arithmetic — and from here Band 8 keeps working down the Grade-C list, where native and Sino sit side by side and only one of them will split. 여럿이 모이면 이런저런 말이 나오기 마련이에요; 계속 읽으세요.
