# Korean, Word-First — Module 55: The Little Words II

Band 6 opens, and the theme is frequency itself. Fifty-four modules of themed shelves kept stepping around these — the little words too load-bearing to belong to any topic: 데 is rank 62 in the entire language and today is its first day on a shelf of its own. This pack collects the dependent-noun engine (줄, 데, 뿐, 터, 듯이), the squeezed pronouns (얘/걔/쟤), the humble and plain plurals (저희/너희), the estimate-welds (한두/두세/서너), and the formal faces of M29's 그렇다 machine. That module taught the little words at beginner height; this is the Ring 2 sequel — smaller syllables, more mileage. The glue is the know-how machine: **-(으)ㄹ 줄 알다/모르다** — including its famous second job, being wrong about things.

**How to use this:** almost every word here is a homograph, a squeeze, or a frame — read the notes before drilling, and drill the radar sets (줄/줄, 간/간/간, 편/편/편, 대/대/대) as sets. Nothing on this shelf is long; everything on it is busy.

## Part 1 — Small words, four factories

This shelf is less a vocabulary list than a toolbox — four little factories, every one of which you've already watched running:

- **The dependent-noun drawer:** Korean keeps nouns that can't stand alone yet hold up half its grammar — and you've been using their output for years. M24's 그런 note confessed that M7's 그런데 was 그런 + 데 all along; M53's -는데 is that same little noun fused into the course's biggest connective. Today the drawer opens: 데, 뿐, 터, 듯이, 식, 외, 간 — each takes M24's badge or a noun and builds a frame.
- **The determiner drawer fills:** M13's 새, M46's 옛, M54's 헌 — and today 온 (whole), 맨 (very-edge), 딴 (other), 단 (single), 각 (each), 몇몇 (several). Everything that stamps a noun from the front is now yours.
- **The 준말 press runs again:** the interlude's law — frequency is pressure — squeezes 이 애 → 얘, 그 애 → 걔, 저 애 → 쟤 (M29's pointer row at kid-height, via M47's 애). And it ran long before you arrived: M29's 그렇다 is 그러하다 pressed flat, the squeeze fossilized so long ago the short form got the factory job.
- **The estimate-welds:** M5's counter-forms fuse into approximations — 한두, 두세, 서너. No word for 'about' required; the weld is the about.

## Part 3 — The glue: -(으)ㄹ 줄 알다 / 모르다

### 1. The know-how machine

Prospective badge + 줄 + 알다/모르다: 운전할 **줄 알아요** = I know how to drive; 매운 것을 먹을 **줄 몰라요** = I don't know how to eat spicy food. Radar vs M10's can-machine: -(으)ㄹ 수 있다 says a way EXISTS; -(으)ㄹ 줄 알다 says you've LEARNED the trick. 수영할 수 있어요 = the pool allows it; 수영할 줄 알아요 = your arms know it.

### 2. Being wrong: 줄 알았어요

Same machine, past tense, and the meaning flips to mistaken belief: 비가 올 **줄 알았어요** = I THOUGHT it would rain — and it didn't. The badge picks the tense of the belief (올 줄 = would come; 온 줄 = had come), and 몰랐어요 works the blind side: 오늘이 월요일인 줄 몰랐어요 = I didn't realize it was Monday. Korean files skills and misjudgments in the same drawer — 줄 is the noun for how you think things go, whether in your hands or your forecasts.

**Calibration:** 몰랐어요 is also the apology machine — 그런 줄 몰랐어요 (M24's 그런) = I didn't know it was like that, the standard I-meant-no-harm. Notice English does the same split with the same verbs: "I can drive" vs "I know how to drive" — Korean just refuses to blur them.

**The frame family:** the other dependent nouns run the same badge-plus-noun grammar — no new machinery: 웃을 **뿐**이에요 (merely), 피곤할 **텐데** (expectation — 터 wearing M53's -ㄴ데), 아는 **듯이** (as if), 그런 **식**으로 (in that manner), 그 **외**에 (besides). One pattern, five frames; the notes carry each.
