# Korean, Word-First — Module 93: The Analyst's Verbs

The report asserts; the mouth hedges. Every word in this module is how print states a finding — 사실이 드러났다, 원인이 밝혀졌다, 방안을 제시했다, 위기를 극복했다 — and every ending in it is how a person, across a table, softens one. Thirty-two Grade-C words in four drawers: FINDING (드러나다 밝혀지다 파악하다 살피다 알아보다 분석 주어지다), STATING (제시하다 지적하다 지적 주장 비판 인정하다 요청하다 반영하다), DOING (다루다 이끌다 실천 실천하다 수행하다 발휘하다 대응 대응하다 대비하다 강화하다 개선 일치하다), and REFUSING AND FORECASTING (극복하다 거부하다 제외하다 전망 예상되다). And the glue is their counterweight — four spoken hedges: **-(으)ㄹ 테니까 / -(으)ㄹ 텐데** (I'll…, so / it'd be…, and given that), **-(으)ㄹ걸요** (I'd guess), **-길래** (seeing that), **-(으)ㄹ까 봐** (for fear that). M85 gave you the written voice and M87 its written hedges; this module gives the verbs that voice is made of, and then the four endings a real person uses to take the edge off.

**How to use this:** decode first — twenty-five of these are two-hanja compounds and you own a half of nearly every one (握 提 示 認 定 反 映 對 應 備 强 化 善 一 服 拒 否 外 展 望) — and read the eleven IOU payoffs as receipts: M86 wrote 파악하다 and 제시하다 frozen with this module's number on them; M81 carried 개선; M87 ran 밝혀지다 and 수행평가; M51 filed 악기를 다루다; M33 promised 알아보다; M72 and M75 said 분석 and 비판 "rides free". Then read Parts 2–5 as one lesson: the sentence deck is spoken, on purpose, so that every report verb arrives wearing a hedge.

## Part 1 — Four drawers of a report

- **Finding (드러나다 밝혀지다 파악하다 살피다 알아보다 분석 주어지다):** two intransitives for what comes to light — 드러나다 exposed by circumstance, 밝혀지다 established by someone's lamp (M56's 밝히다 through -아/어지다 from M35); 파악하다 (把握) gets the measurements; 살피다 is the root M56's 살펴보다 was built on; 알아보다 is the eye-side twin M33's 알아듣다 promised; 분석 is M72's 분석하다 dismounted; 주어지다 is M9's 주다 with no giver.
- **Stating (제시하다 지적하다 지적 주장 비판 인정하다 요청하다 반영하다):** to put forward, to point out, the claim (M56's 주장하다 dismounted), the criticism (M75's 비판하다 dismounted), to admit, to request in writing (M64's 요청 conjugated), and to reflect a view in a decision. 지적 is two words — 指摘 the pointing-out, 知的 the intellectual — and the note holds both.
- **Doing (다루다 이끌다 실천 실천하다 수행하다 발휘하다 대응 대응하다 대비하다 강화하다 개선 일치하다):** the hands (다루다), the front (이끌다 — M51's 끌다 with 이 on), the walk (實踐), the assignment (遂行), the letting-out (發揮), the answer sent back (對應), the bracing (對備), the tightening (强化), the making-better (改善), and the two things arriving at one point (一致).
- **Refusing and forecasting (극복하다 거부하다 제외하다 전망 예상되다):** to subdue a hardship (克服 — the 服 of M83's 운동복, in its other job), to refuse a demand (拒否 — not M33's 거절하다, which declines an offer), to leave out (除外, mostly as 을 제외하고), the outlook (展望), and the forecast's closer, 예상되다 — M34's 예상 with the person removed.

::vocab::

**Radar patrol:** 지적 is 指摘 when you 받다 it and 知的 when it takes 인 · 드러나다 is EXPOSED, M32's 나타나다 SHOWS UP — a scandal 드러나요, a ghost 나타나요 · 거부하다 refuses an order, M33's 거절하다 declines an offer · 대비하다 braces for what might come, M8's 준비하다 gets a thing ready — and 대비 is also 對比, contrast, in the designer's mouth · 이끌다 pulls people forward, M51's 끌다 pulls suitcases and attention · 전망 is the outlook on the economy page and the actual view from the 전망대 · 주장 is the claim (主張), and the team's 주장 is the captain (主將) · and 대응 is a response you decide, M86's 반응 one you can't help.

## Part 2 — The glue: -(으)ㄹ 테니까 / -(으)ㄹ 텐데 — I'll…, so / it'd be…, and given that

M55's 터 was taught as ground, two ways, and its note ran both frames in advance — 금방 갈 테니까, glossed "I'll be there soon, so", and 피곤할 텐데, glossed "you must be tired, given that" — with the sentence 피곤할 텐데 좀 쉬세요 already in the deck. Today the frames get their own part. Build: the future hook -(으)ㄹ from M8 + 터 + 이다, squeezed to 테, then either -(으)니까 from M10 or -는데 from M53. **-(으)ㄹ 테니까** = 'it'll be the case that…, so': 제가 할 테니까 걱정하지 마세요 (M14's 걱정하다) = I'll do it, so don't worry; 이 문제는 제가 다룰 테니까 (today's 다루다). **-(으)ㄹ 텐데** = 'it'll probably be the case that…, and given that': 비가 올 텐데 우산을 가져가세요 (M11's 비, M9's 우산, M52's 가져가다) = it'll probably rain, so take an umbrella; 결과가 곧 밝혀질 텐데 (today's 밝혀지다) = the result will come out soon, and given that…. The person decides the meaning: FIRST person = intention — the same volunteer's will M49's md described ("First person, -겠- pledges") — and THIRD person = supposition, a guess about the world. M55's 터 note put it in one line: "Expectation is intention with the ground shifted." The 니까 frame pushes toward a request or an order; the 텐데 frame sets a scene and lets the listener draw the conclusion — which is why 텐데 can stand alone with nothing after it (피곤할 텐데…), trailing off, the Korean way of saying 'you poor thing'.

## Part 3 — The glue: -(으)ㄹ걸요 — I'd guess

The soft guess: the future hook -(으)ㄹ from M8 + 걸요 (것을 + 요, squeezed until it stopped meaning 'thing'). 아마 집에 있을걸요 (M24's 아마, M1's 집) = I'd guess he's home; 그건 비쌀걸요 (M7's 비싸다) = that'd be expensive, I think; 부장님도 인정할걸요 = the boss will probably admit it, I'd say. It is the guess with a shrug in it — the speaker offers a view and reserves the right to be wrong, the tone rising slightly at the end. Now sort the whole guess-family, because Korean has been building it since M8 and this is the last member:

| form | owner | what it claims | 비 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| -(으)ㄹ 거예요 | M8 | a prediction — I expect it | 비가 올 거예요 |
| 것 같다 | M34 | a hunch — I think, it seems | 비가 올 것 같아요 |
| -나 보다 | M34 | an inference from a clue you can point at | 비가 오나 봐요 |
| -겠- | M49 | a judgment on the spot, from what's in front of you | 비가 오겠어요 |
| -(으)ㄹ 수도 있다 | M84 | a possibility — it might | 비가 올 수도 있어요 |
| 듯하다 | M87 | the written seem | 비가 올 듯하다 |
| -(으)ㄹ걸요 | M93 | the spoken guess with a shrug | 비가 올걸요 |

M34's md drew the first line ("-나 보다 wants a clue you can point at"); M87's 듯하다 note drew the register line ("것 같다 is SAID, 듯하다 is WRITTEN"); -(으)ㄹ걸요 sits at the spoken end of the shelf, one shrug past 것 같아요. M24's 아마 note already knew the culture — "certainty is not the Korean way" — and this ending is that sentence made grammar. **RADAR:** the same form said to yourself, falling, with no 요, is the REGRET -(으)ㄹ걸: 그럴걸 = I should have; 더 공부할걸 (M1's 공부하다) = I should have studied more. Rising and to someone, a guess; falling and to yourself, a sigh. One form, two directions.

## Part 4 — The glue: -길래 — seeing that

The observed reason: stem + 길래, and the cause is something you SAW before you acted. 날씨가 좋길래 산책했어요 (M7's 날씨, M17's 산책하다) = the weather was nice, so I took a walk; 세일하길래 샀어요 (M9's 세일, M2's 사다) = it was on sale, so I bought it; 회사가 제 요청을 거부하길래 다른 방안을 제시했어요 (today's 거부하다 and 제시하다) = the company refused, so I put forward another plan. The rule of persons: the -길래 clause is NOT the speaker — it is the weather, the shop, the company, the other person — and the main clause IS: you watched, then you did. Sort it against the reasons you own: -아/어서 is neutral cause; -(으)니까 from M10 argues (and, that md said, is what the reason takes "if the sentence ends in 주세요/-(으)세요/-(으)ㄹ까요"); -길래 is a cause WITNESSED — which puts it in the family of the -더- endings from M88, the retrospective set whose md called -더니 "the eyewitness's and-then". -길래 is the eyewitness's because. Its question form is the mouth's astonishment: 뭘 했길래 그렇게 피곤해요? (M7's 피곤하다) = what on earth did you do to be that tired?; 무슨 일이길래? (M10's 무슨) = what could it possibly be? — 왜 -길래 asks not for a reason but for a scene.

## Part 5 — The glue: -(으)ㄹ까 봐 — for fear that

The worry clause: the wondering -(으)ㄹ까 (M9 taught -(으)ㄹ까요 as shall-we; here the 요 comes off) + M1's 보다 in its -아 coat, 봐 — literally 'seeing whether…', and in use: in case…, for fear that…. 늦을까 봐 일찍 나왔어요 (M8's 늦다, M8's 일찍, M28's 나오다) = I left early in case I'd be late; 비가 올까 봐 걱정이에요 (M29's 걱정) = I'm worried it might rain; 사람들이 제 실력을 못 알아볼까 봐 걱정했어요 (today's 알아보다) = I was worried people wouldn't recognize what I can do. The main clause is either the precaution you took (일찍 나왔어요, 미리 준비했어요 — M38's 미리, M8's 준비하다) or the worry itself (걱정이에요, 걱정돼요 — M53's 걱정되다). Count 보다's careers — M34's md stopped at six — and this is another: the verb of seeing, used for the thing you are afraid to see. Sort: -(으)ㄹ 수도 있어서 (from M84) states that a thing might happen; -(으)ㄹ까 봐 states that you are afraid it will — and only the second one explains why you brought the umbrella.

## Part 6 — Building sentences

**Spot the recycling:** M55's 터 carries both the 테니까 and the 텐데 sentence, with -지 마세요 from M14 and the try-machine -아/어 보다 from M16 closing them; M24's 아마 leans on the new 걸요; -길래 takes M64's 요청 and M86's 방안 as its scene; the double-subject frame runs 저는 … 사람들이 … under -(으)ㄹ까 봐; the -더- family from M88 (-았/었더니 and -더라고요) carries M72's 분석하다 to today's 일치하다; -잖아요 from M42 puts 반영하다 and 개선 across a table; and the one printed sentence, in the 합니다체 from M4 with -아/어지다 from M35, shows what the deck sounds like with the person taken out.
