# Korean, Word-First — Module 46: It's Been Years

Ring 1 built the clock and the calendar — M5 read the hours, M25 filed the dates, M38 measured the spans. This module is time as a STORY: the era shelf (현대, 90년대, 오늘날), the seasons of a life (시절, 평생, 장래, 언젠가), the ordinal machine (첫 번째, 첫날, 최초), and the pace dial that runs from 지금 당장 to 영원히. The glue is the stopwatch: **-(으)ㄴ 지** — how long it's been SINCE — the machine behind the sentence every learner earns on day 366: 한국어를 배운 지 일 년이 넘었어요.

**How to use this:** almost every brick splits into parts you already own — read the decodes before you drill. The register pairs (지난해/작년, 오늘날/요즘) are one meaning in two dress codes: learn them as pairs, speak the spoken one, and read the news with the other.

---

## Part 1 — The calendar learns to tell a story

- **전/후 go free-standing:** M5's 오전/오후 and M13's -기 전에/-(으)ㄴ 후에 always carried 前 and 後 for you; 이전/이후 hand them over as standalone boundary nouns — 졸업 이후, 그 이전. No verb required; timelines just hinge.
- **The news register arrives:** anchors say 지난해 where the break room says 작년, and essays open with 오늘날 where speech says 요즘 — the same two-dress-codes split M43 filed for the pay words. 이날 completes the kit: dateline anaphora.
- **The determiner shelf seats three:** M13's 새 was the preview, M24's 옛날 note planted 옛, M26's 첫눈 planted 첫 — today both stand alone. 새 집, 옛 친구, 첫 만남: forward, back, and first.
- **The ordinal machine assembles:** M12's counter-번 + -째, driven by M5's native numbers in combining form — 두 번째, 세 번째 — with 첫 claiming first place: 첫 번째. Turns, ranks, and attempts, all counted with parts you own.
- **The initial-sound law keeps paying:** M44's 토론 note decoded 論 → 논/론; the same law bends 年 → 연 up front (연말) and 例 → 예/례 (차례) — one rule, three shelves.

## Part 3 — The glue: the stopwatch noun

### 1. -(으)ㄴ 지 — how long it's been

M24's past badge stamps the verb finished; **지** opens the stopwatch; a duration reads it; one of three finishers closes it: **됐어요 / 지났어요 / 넘었어요**. 한국어를 **배운 지** 일 년이 됐어요 · 여기에서 **산 지** 십 년이 넘었어요 · 못 **만난 지** 오래됐어요 — M35's 오래되다 was built for that last seat. The duration slot takes M25's date units and M38's 기간-vocabulary; the question form asks 얼마나 됐어요?

### 2. The three 지s

Same syllable, three jobs, one tell. The counting-지 is a NOUN and wears a space: 배운 지. The negation -지 glues to the stem, no space: -지 않다 (M28), -지 마세요 (M14). The question-folder -는지 (M33) folds whole questions into sentences. If 지 stands apart after a past badge — someone's counting.

**Calibration:** -(으)ㄴ 지 only counts FROM an event — it needs the past badge and a finisher. For "during," M28's 동안 still serves; for "since then" without counting, 그 이후로 now does. And the greeting 오랜만이에요 (M19) is this machine's frozen fossil — 오래 + 만, a long-gap-since, pre-assembled and handed out at reunions.

## Part 4 — Building sentences

**Spot the recycling:** M36's 넘다 reads the stopwatch past the one-year mark, M12's 지나다 (the 지난- family's parent) closes a decade, M42 delivers both the year-end nudge (-잖아요) and the sunset ache (그립다), M35's 남다 counts what's left of December, and M17's 지다 finally works the sunset job its note promised.

::sentences::

**Gap deck:** the decade that remakes rivers and mountains (십 년이면 강산도 변한다), the calendar as medicine (세월이 약이다), and losing track of time as the highest compliment (시간 가는 줄 몰랐어요).
