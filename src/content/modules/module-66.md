# Korean, Word-First — Module 66: Push, Pour, Run

Band 6 keeps slicing the long tail, and this slice is the body in motion — the verbs that move the hand, the foot, or the whole body through space: point, poke, pick up, hang, pour out, spill over, fire, kick, stomp, knock, burst open, let fall, run to, run back, follow, flee, give back, flip, shake off, lay on top. Five machines carry the load, all on native Korean roots: a **point-and-reach axis** (the hand closing in, the finger leading, the tip going in), a **pour-and-spread axis** (the liquid over the lip, the liquid out), a **burst-and-drop axis** (the two causative make-it-verbs, one that opens one that falls), a **run-and-chase axis** (the 달려/따라 compounds welded onto M1's go and come), and a **turn-and-layer axis** (the over-turn, the shake-off, the on-top). Around them, the earlier-module things do the moving: the finger, the ball, the fish, the river, the cup, the water, the door, the book, the baby, the friend, the hair.

**How to use this:** the five axes are the module — drill the point-and-reach axis as one set (the hand versions of "direct" against the foot versions of "strike"), the pour-and-spread axis as the next (the three verbs that move liquid, in three different directions), the burst-and-drop axis as a pair (one opens, one drops — both with the -뜨리다 make-it suffix), and the run-and-chase axis as the four compounds welded onto M1's 가다/오다. Then spend your reps on the same-sound pairs that are actually different roots: 메다 (carry on the shoulder) vs. M60's 매다 (tie, bind) — one is a shoulder, the other a knot; 붓다 (pour in) vs. 흘리다 (spill out) vs. 쏟다 (dump out) — three liquid verbs in three directions; and the two "sharp-tip" verbs, 찌르다 (the needle-in poke) vs. 쏘다 (the fire — the tip that flies). One hand, many jobs.

## Part 2 — Point, reach, strike

::vocab::

**Radar patrol:** the same-sound-different-root splits are the module's sharpest edge. 메다 (carry on the shoulder) is a different root from M60's 매다 (tie, bind) — same sound, one is a shoulder, the other a knot; the shoulder carries the water, the knot ties the rope. Then the liquid runs three ways: 붓다 pours in over the lip of the cup, 흘리다 spills out along the top, and 쏟다 dumps out in one pour — three verbs, one liquid, three directions, all three in this module. And the two sharp-tip verbs sit side by side without being each other: 찌르다 is the needle-in poke, the tip against skin, while 쏘다 is the fire, the tip that leaves the hand and flies — both "direct," but one stays in place, one runs off. And 가리다 carries two B-senses at once: 시야를 가리다 blocks the view, 시비를 가리다 settles right from wrong — M63's courtroom in one verb. One hand, many jobs.

## Part 3 — The verb machines

### 1. The point-and-reach axis

The hand in every posture of "direct a thing": **가리다** (block the view — the view-blocker, a hand or a leaf set in front of the line of sight), **가리키다** (point at — the pointing-finger verb, the tip leading, the target named by direction), **찌르다** (poke — the needle-in, a point against skin, the tip leading), **집다** (pick up — the fingers-in, thumb and two fingers closing so the thing leaves the surface), **줍다** (gather — the ground-pick, the hand raking the small things up off the floor), **건지다** (lift out — the lift-from-the-water, the thing leaving the liquid), **비키다** (step aside — 비켜!, the body clearing its own spot from the path), and **메다** (carry on — the shoulder verb, the water or the load riding on M14's 어깨). Eight postures of the hand, one axis.

### 2. The pour-and-spread axis

The liquid and the foot, moving out: **붓다** (pour — the liquid over the lip of M18's 컵, the stream catching the glass), **흘리다** (spill — the run-out, the thin spread along the top), **쏟다** (dump out — the overflow, the whole thing leaving the container in one pour), **쏘다** (fire — the laser-gun, the tip that leaves the hand and flies; also "send a look"), **차다** (kick — the foot-swing, M14's 발 leading), **밟다** (stomp on — the ground-tread, the sole coming down), and **두드리다** (knock — the knock-on, the fist flat against M6's 문). The liquid three plus the foot-and-fist four, one axis.

### 3. The burst-and-drop axis

The two causative make-it-verbs, both with the -뜨리다 make-it suffix: **터뜨리다** (make burst — the burst-open, M17's 공 open with its pop) and **떨어뜨리다** (make fall — the make-it-drop, M18's 컵 leaving the hand). One opens, one drops; both are M36 verbs — 터지다 and 떨어지다 — with a hand put behind them.

### 4. The run-and-chase axis

The 달/따라 compounds welded onto M1's 가다 and 오다, plus the two run-away verbs and the return-turn: **달려가다** (run to — 달려 + go), **달려오다** (run here — 달려 + come), **따라가다** (follow to — 따라 + go), **따라오다** (come after — 따라 + come), **달아나다** (flee — the old run-stem + M28's 나다, the bolt), **도망가다** (run away — the 도망 escape family, the run-with-a-plan), and **돌려주다** (give back — M37's 돌리다 bent into 돌려 + M9's 주다). Six compounds on the go/come spine, one on the give spine, one axis.

### 5. The turn-and-layer axis

The over-turn, the shake-off, the on-top, and the crowd-push: **뒤집다** (flip — M6's 뒤 + the 집 root, the front face going over to the back), **털다** (shake off — the throw-off, the dust off the coat, M8's 머리 shaken out), **얹다** (put on top — the layer verb, the hand coming to rest on the book), and **몰다** (drive — 차를 몰다 drives the car, and the herd sense moves the whole flock; M65's 코너 note already showed its passive, 코너에 몰리다). Four turn-and-layer-and-push verbs, one axis.

## Part 4 — Building sentences

**Spot the recycling:** M10's -고 chains every axis's verbs into one breath in all eight sentences — the finger points and the friend points, the water pours and spills, the friend runs to and I follow along, the baby kicks and stomps and knocks, the book flips, the hair shakes out, and the hand comes to rest. M1's 가다 and 오다 take the 달- and 따라-compounds in the run axis. M9's 주다 takes the return-turn in the give-back sentence, with -(으)면 (M20) gating the trade. M28's 손가락, M17's 공, M18's 컵, M22's 강 and 물고기, M25's 아기, M1's 친구, M8's 머리, M6's 문, M1's 책, and M14's 발 and 어깨 are the things every verb lands on.

::sentences::

**Gap deck:** the push-that-carries (밀어붙이다 — S2's 밀다 + M37's 붙이다, the nudge turned into a shove) and the fall-into-water (물에 빠지다 — M1's 물 taken under by M35's 빠지다) — the module's two verbs too useful to leave unspoken.
