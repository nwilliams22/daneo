This module pays two debts the course has carried for nineteen modules: **한테/에게/께** — the to-a-person particles Module 8's 전화하다 note promised was "arriving soon" — and **-들**, the plural marker you've quietly never needed. **How to use this:** the first question every Korean conversation asks a foreigner is 어느 나라에서 왔어요? This module makes sure the question, the answer, and the follow-ups are all yours.

## Part 1 — Country names are tiny poems

Most country names are hanja transliterations, and the syllables mean things:

- **미국** = beautiful country (美 was picked for its sound in "America" — the flattery is accidental)
- **영국** = flower-of-heroes country (英, from "England")
- **중국** = MIDDLE country — not phonetic, a worldview
- **일본** = sun-origin — where the sun rises, seen from the continent
- **독일** (獨逸, via "Deutsch"), **호주** (濠洲, for Australia)
- Phonetic-only latecomers stay Konglish: 프랑스, 러시아, 캐나다

And the machines you already own keep working: [나라] + **사람** = a national (독일 사람), [나라] + **어** = the language (일본어, 중국어 — with one famous exception: 미국 speaks 영어, England's leftover). M23's 외국, decoded country by country.

## Part 3 — The glue

### 1. 한테 / 에게 / 께 — TO a person

Places take 에 (M1); **people take 한테** (speech) or **에게** (writing) — and elders take **께**, the honorific upgrade (M4's pattern, still running). They bolt onto every giving-and-sending verb you own:

- 친구**한테** 선물을 줬어요 — gave a present TO a friend (M9's 주다, completed)
- 부모님**께** 자주 전화해요 — the M8 IOU, paid: the person you call takes the particle
- 아내**한테** 편지를 썼어요 — wrote a letter to my wife

Direction flips with 한테서/에게서 (FROM a person) — meet it in reading; produce it in Ring 2.

### 2. -들 — the optional plural

Korean nouns don't need a plural (책 = book or books; context referees). **-들** appears when people are involved and the many-ness matters: 사람**들**, 친구**들**, 학생**들** — and inside 여러분 (everyone), which is 여러 + 분 politely pluraled. Rule of thumb: use it for people, skip it for things, and never stack it where a number already counts (세 명, not 세 명들).

### 3. The origins kit

All owned, now assembled: **어느 나라에서 왔어요?** (which country did you come from?) → 미국**에서 왔어요** — came-from does the work of "I'm from" → 무슨 일을 하세요? → and the warm one: **고향이 어디예요?** — hometown beats country once they know you're foreign. Answer and return-serve: 저는 서울 사람이에요.

## Part 4 — Building sentences

**Spot the recycling:** 러시아는 정말 크네요 runs M26's noticing ending over a map, 여러분, 한국 문화에 관심이 있어요? aims M26's 문화 and M24's 관심 at a crowd, and 교수님이 한자를 설명해 주셨어요 sends M24's 설명하다 through M9's do-for-me machine wearing M4's honorific.

::sentences::

**Street-title check (gap deck):** every stranger over forty is 아저씨 or 아줌마 — uncle and aunt. Korea extends the family to the whole country; the gap deck explains how to use it without getting glared at.

## Part 5 — Practice

Cover the right column and translate; then flip and build the Korean.

1. Which country are you from? → 어느 나라에서 왔어요?
2. I'm from America. → 저는 미국에서 왔어요.
3. Where is your hometown? → 고향이 어디예요?
4. I gave presents to my friends. → 친구들한테 선물을 줬어요.
5. I call my parents often. → 부모님께 자주 전화해요.
6. My husband is German. → 남편은 독일 사람이에요.
7. Nice to meet you. → 만나서 반갑습니다.
8. Busan is south of Seoul. → 부산은 서울에서 남쪽에 있어요.

Compass drill: stand in your kitchen and name what lies 동쪽/서쪽/남쪽/북쪽 of it — building, city, country, until you run out of geography.
