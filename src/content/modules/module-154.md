# Korean, Word-First — Module 154: Family, Travel and Everyday Places

An uncle's title, a clinic sign, a bus route and a wedding invitation all name a precise relationship. This pack gives you those labels without changing the grammar you already know.

**How to use this:** Start with the word notes. Read each sentence from natural English to Korean-order gloss to Korean, following the matching chunks.

---

## Part 1 — People and their branches

큰아버지 and 작은아버지 mark older and younger brothers of your father. Their spouses are 큰어머니 and 작은어머니. 외할아버지 marks your mother's side. 큰딸 and 작은아들 compare children within a family; neither describes height. 형수 identifies an older brother's wife. Read the full relationship before translating the adjective.

## Part 2 — Signs you meet outside

M52 supplied 입국 and 출입국 for the border, plus 시외버스 and 관광버스 for route and purpose. Today 국제선 crosses a national border; 국내선 stays within it. On a clinic sign, 안과 concerns eyes and 소아과 concerns children. The examination 진찰 and flu label 독감 return from M53; 눈병 now names an eye illness.

팝송 adds the pop-song label beside 클래식 from M51. The rest of the pack names ordinary objects and settings: a heated 온돌 floor, a cooled room, a 색연필, two colors, a 칼국수 meal. The shared endings help: -장 marks a venue in 예식장, 야구장 and 축구장; -색 marks color in 보라색 and 연두색.

## Part 3 — Reuse the machinery

No new glue. The sentences bring M153's 고궁 and 장인 into a new family visit, 삼계탕 into a recovery scene, and 외아들 into a wedding scene. These are new combinations, not lines to recite from the previous module.

- **-는 김에 from M91** attaches a meeting to a palace visit.
- **-았/었더니 from M88** connects a meal and what followed.
- **에 비해 from M86** compares flight routes and their duration.

Eight sentences put the vocabulary into familiar grammar frames.

**Gap deck:** 큰아버지 and 작은어머니 are relationship titles, not measurements. Decode the literal parts, then name the actual relative.
