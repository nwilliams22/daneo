## Part 1 — How to read a Korean map

Three tricks unlock almost everything below.

**Provinces are two-city welds.** 강원도 = **강**릉 + **원**주. 충청도 = **충**주 + **청**주. 전라도 = **전**주 + **나**주. 경상도 = **경**주 + **상**주. Take the first syllable of the region's two chief cities, add 도 (province), done — the 조선 cartographers were compressing, exactly like the 준말 reading said Koreans always do. Only 경기도 works differently: 京 capital + 畿 surroundings — *the province around Seoul*, which is precisely what it is. The big three split north/south with compass syllables you own: 전라북도, 충청남도, 경상북도…

**-주 stamps the old towns, -도 stamps the provinces.** That -주 (州) on 경주, 전주, 광주, 충주 is the old administrative-town suffix — spot it and you know the town predates the map. And a wrinkle worth savoring: 제주도 wears **two hats** — the province (道) and the island (島). Same syllable, same map, two hanja behind it.

**Count your 경s.** 경기도's 경 is 京 *capital*. 경주's and 경상도's 경 is 慶 *celebration*. M22's 경치 carries 景 *scenery*. Three homographs, and the homograph radar S3 handed you sorts them without breaking stride — this is what it was built for.

## Part 3 — The glue: 마다

*Every / each.* One particle, no conjugation, sticks straight onto the noun:

- **날마다** — every day (= M5's 매일: 매 is the Sino "every", 마다 the native one)
- **주말마다 남산에 올라가요** — every weekend, up Namsan
- **지역마다 음식이 달라요** — every region, different food

Like 도 and 만, it **replaces** 이/가 and 을/를 rather than stacking on them. And it distributes: 사람마다 달라요 — *it differs person by person*. That's the whole particle. Ring 2 glue starts gently; Module 32's quoting machine will not be this polite.

## Part 4 — Building sentences

**Spot the recycling:** M28's 올라가다 climbs 남산, M24's badge builds 제일 높은 산, M10's can-machine gives the 경주 tour, and M6's 사이 stands between 북한 and China holding a crater lake.
