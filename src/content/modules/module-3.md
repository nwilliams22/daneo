# Korean, Word-First — Module 3: Food, Eating, and Saying No

Two modules in, you can build real sentences and put them in the past. Module 3 feeds that machine — literally. Food is where Korean daily life happens, so this module is heavy on delicious bricks, and its glue is the stuff you say at every meal: *I want to eat this*, *I don't eat that*, *I can't drink this*.

**How to use this:** Same loop as always. The food words are the easiest vocabulary you'll ever learn — half of them you'll recognize from menus, and several are built from parts you already own.

---

## Part 1 — Food words are honest words

Korean food vocabulary loves to show its work:

- **김밥** = 김 (seaweed) + 밥 (rice) — the name is the recipe
- **불고기** = 불 (fire) + 고기 (meat) — "fire meat"
- **비빔밥** = 비빔 (mixing) + 밥 — "mixing rice"
- **맛있다 / 맛없다** — taste exists / taste doesn't exist
- **배고프다 / 배부르다** — stomach is hungry / stomach is full

Once you see the parts, new menu items start decoding themselves. Even the portmanteaus play this game — 치킨 + 맥주 = **치맥**, the national chicken-and-beer institution.

## Part 3 — The glue: wanting and refusing

### 1. 안 — don't (by choice)

Negation is one syllable placed **right before the verb**:

- 저는 고기를 **안** 먹어요 — I don't eat meat
- 커피를 **안** 마셔요 — I don't drink coffee

No "do-support," no reshuffling. The sentence you already know, plus 안.

### 2. 못 — can't

Same position, different meaning — inability or circumstance, never preference:

- 저는 소주를 **못** 마셔요 — I can't drink soju
- 오늘 **못** 가요 — I can't go today

**안 vs 못 is a real distinction Koreans hear instantly:** 술 안 마셔요 = I choose not to drink. 술 못 마셔요 = I can't. Pick the wrong one and you've said something different.

### 3. -고 싶어요 — want to

Wanting bolts straight onto the verb stem: drop 다, add **고 싶어요**.

| Verb | Want to… |
|------|----------|
| 먹다 | 먹**고 싶어요** — want to eat |
| 마시다 | 마시**고 싶어요** — want to drink |
| 보다 | 보**고 싶어요** — want to see (also: *I miss you*) |
| 가다 | 가**고 싶어요** — want to go |
| 시키다 | 시키**고 싶어요** — want to order in |

One catch worth banking now: **-고 싶어요 is for your own wants.** Reporting someone *else's* desire uses -고 싶어**해**요 — Korean marks whose feeling it is. (This pair lives in the gap deck too.)

### Past tense still applies

Everything stacks: 안 먹**었**어요 (didn't eat), 먹고 싶**었**어요 (wanted to eat).

## Part 5 — Practice

Cover the right column and translate; then flip and build the Korean.

1. I don't eat meat. → 저는 고기를 안 먹어요.
2. I can't drink soju. → 저는 소주를 못 마셔요.
3. I want to eat gimbap. → 김밥을 먹고 싶어요.
4. Kimchi is spicy. → 김치가 매워요.
5. I had ramen for lunch. → 점심에 라면을 먹었어요.
6. I bought fruit and vegetables at the supermarket. → 마트에서 과일하고 채소를 샀어요.
7. I'm hungry now. → 저는 지금 배고파요.
8. I ordered bibimbap at the restaurant. → 식당에서 비빔밥을 주문했어요.

Bonus: say what you actually want to eat right now, out loud, in Korean. There's a decent chance you now can.
