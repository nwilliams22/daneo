## Part 1 — The ladder and the second school day

Korean schools form a clean ladder, and the words build themselves: **초등학교** (elementary) → **중학교** (middle) → **고등학교** (high) → **대학교** (the *big* school). A student is addressed by coordinates: **3학년 2반** — grade 3, class 2. That 반 is M5's *half* with a second job, and **학년** is spelled one way and said another (항년) — the sound interlude's nasalization rule, grading papers.

Then there's the fact of Korean childhood the ladder doesn't show: **학원**. After regular school ends, most kids go to a second one — math 학원, English 학원, piano 학원 — often past dark. The gap deck's 수능 explains what it's all aiming at.

Meanwhile the compounds keep decoding themselves:

- **교실** = teach + room — the 실 of 화장실/침실/거실, now at school
- **공책** = blank + book — M1's 책 finally builds a compound
- **지우개** = erase + the tool-suffix **-개** — M13's 베개 (lean-tool) has a classmate
- **외국어** = outside + country + language — the thing you're doing right now

## Part 3 — The glue

### 1. -기 시작하다 — start to

M13 taught you that **-기** turns a verb into a little noun (자기 전에). Same noun, new machine: hand it to **시작하다** and the verb begins.

- 한국어를 **배우기 시작했어요** — I started learning Korean
- 비가 **오기 시작해요** — it's starting to rain
- 내일부터 운동하기 **시작할 거예요** — I'll start exercising tomorrow

Past, present, future — 시작하다 conjugates like the ordinary M8 verb it is; -기 never changes. One suffix, two machines now (전에, 시작하다), more coming.

### 2. Right and wrong

The feedback pair every learner needs: **맞아요** (correct!) and **틀렸어요** (wrong — try again). You've heard both from this app's own quizzes; now they're officially yours. With them: **질문이 있어요** (questions exist before they're asked) and **물어보다** (ask-and-see — the 보다 softens it).

### 3. Exams are watched

Korean doesn't *take* a test — it **sees** one: 시험을 **봐요**. Do well and you *saw it well*: 시험을 **잘 봤어요**. The gap deck files the full story; M1's 보다 just adds its strangest job to the résumé.

## Part 5 — Practice

Cover the right column and translate; then flip and build the Korean.

1. I started learning Korean. → 한국어를 배우기 시작했어요.
2. It's starting to rain. → 비가 오기 시작해요.
3. Don't forget the homework. → 숙제를 잊지 마세요.
4. What grade are you in? → 몇 학년이에요?
5. I did well on the test. → 시험을 잘 봤어요.
6. The answer is wrong. Do it again. → 답이 틀렸어요. 다시 하세요.
7. Math is hard, but it's fun. → 수학은 어렵지만 재미있어요.
8. Starting tomorrow, I'll start exercising. → 내일부터 운동하기 시작할 거예요.

Autobiography drill: three true sentences about your own studying — what you started (시작했어요), what's hard (힘들어요/어려워요), what you'll do tomorrow (내일부터…). The module is about you anyway.
