# Korean, Word-First — Module 74: Two Words, One Verb

M28 taught you that Korean's busiest motion verbs are welds — 들어가다, 나오다, an action snapped onto 가다/오다 — and M66 ran the rail again with 달려가다 and 따라오다. This module opens the whole factory. Twenty-eight words, and you already own nearly every part: verbs welded to verbs (생겨나다, 찾아내다, 흘러가다), nouns welded to verbs (소문나다, 잠들다), adverbs welded to verbs (잘살다, 밤늦다), a -치다 tail that means with force (넘치다, 합치다), the one-off causative 없애다, and three loners the pool owed you (들르다, 졸다, 빨다). Beside the welds, two smaller shelves: the 편 family finally splits three ways (편리하다 · 편안하다 · 불편하다) with four more adjectives behind it, and the native nouns of true and false — 멋, 거짓, 가짜, 공짜, 남, 볼일, 밥맛 — where 진짜 gets its family and the sign on every restaurant gets decoded. No new grammar; the grammar is that Korean welds.

**How to use this:** read each weld as arithmetic before you drill it — A + B, both owned — and the word is half-learned on sight. Spend your reps on the rails (which door a verb uses: 나다, 내다, 들다, 가다, -치다), on the spacing pairs (잘 살아요 / 잘살아요), and on the look-alike trio 들다 / 들르다 / 들리다, which English keeps miles apart and Korean keeps one vowel apart.

## Part 1 — The rails

Every verb on the first shelf runs on a rail you already own:

- **The 나다-door:** M28's 나다 — 'Feelings and happenings COME OUT in Korean; 나다 is the door they use.' M67 sent 기억나다 and 생각나다 through it; today 생겨나다, 자라나다, 소문나다 follow.
- **The 내다-door:** M28's 내다, the put-it-out verb — 찾아내다 forces the find out into the open; 알아내다 rides free on the same rail.
- **The 들다-door:** M28's 잠 note said '잠이 들다 — you fall IN'; 잠들다 welds the door shut behind you.
- **The 가다-rail:** the [action] + 가다 table M28 hung gains 흘러가다 — flow, then away; 흘러 is the 르-family shape from the table in M7.
- **The -치다 tail:** NOT M17's 치다. On 넘치다 and 합치다 it is a suffix meaning with force: over-hard = overflow, fit-hard = merge.
- **The spacing law:** 잘 살아요 lives well; 잘살아요 is rich — the rule M63's 잘못 filed (잘 못 해요 / 잘못했어요), and M41's 잘생기다 ran on faces.

## Part 3 — Three shelves, no new grammar

### 1. Verb + verb: the -아/어 coat and the rail

The first verb conjugates into its -아/어 shape, then the second welds on and takes every ending: 생기 + 어 + 나다 → 생겨나다 → 생겨났어요. That is the whole machine — the same coat M66 gave 달리다 inside 달려가다. Which rail a verb takes is the meaning: 나다 makes it erupt (생겨나다, 자라나다), 내다 forces it out (찾아내다), 가다 sends it away (흘러가다). The radar pairs are the payoff: 생기다 arises casually, 생겨나다 is chronicled; 찾다 searches or finds, 찾아내다 only finds, with the effort showing.

### 2. Noun + verb, adverb + verb — and the space that means something

A bare noun or adverb welds too, and fusing it changes the word's class or its sense: 소문 + 나다 → 소문나다, a verb you can badge (소문난 맛집); 잠 + 들다 → 잠들다; 밤 + 늦다 → 밤늦다, an adjective that lives as the adverb 밤늦게. With adverbs the space IS the meaning: 잘 살아요 / 잘살아요. And one weld is a suffix, not a word: 없다 + 애 → 없애다, the sixth causative the party in M37 (이/히/리/기/우) never invited — make-not-exist, get rid of.

**Calibration:** the -치다 tail (넘치다, 합치다) is force, not striking; the 나다-door narrates, so 생겨나다 and 자라나다 belong to news and biography where 생기다 and 자라다 belong to speech. When in doubt, say the spaced version — 잠이 들었어요, 소문이 났어요 — it is never wrong, only less fused.

### 3. The 편 family and the -짜 family

편하다 (M12) has held two jobs since Ring 1; M42's 편안하다 took at-ease, and today 편리하다 (便利 — the benefit-利 of M56's 이용하다) takes convenient: 교통이 편리해요, 마음이 편안해요, and 불편하다 (不便, from the 불- ledger M42's 불만 note told you to keep) negates both — plus the social sense, 불편한 자리. On the noun shelf the -짜 tail names its family once: 진짜 (M11, 眞) / 가짜 (假) / 공짜 (空, the sky-空 of 공항) — real, fake, free. 멋 and 거짓 were pre-split by M21's 멋있다 and M19's 거짓말; 볼일 is 보다 + the -(으)ㄹ badge + 일; and 밥맛 is appetite — and, in 반말 aimed at a person, the insult its note warns you about.

## Part 4 — Building sentences

**Spot the recycling:** the causative 재우다 from M37 puts the baby down and -아/어 버리다 from M52 finishes off the parent, M66's 붓다 overfills the cup for 넘치다, M67's 기뻐하다 watches the plucky answer land, 처럼 (M41) and M36's 보이다 size up the fake bag, -(으)려고 하다 from M50 and -지 마세요 from M14 refuse the freebie, the -게 adverbializer (M33) rides 밤늦게 and 씩씩하게, and the -(으)ㄴ/-는 badge from M24 hangs 소문난 on the restaurant sign.

**Gap deck:** the neighbor's bigger rice cake (남의 떡이 더 커 보인다), the baldness that follows loving free things (공짜 좋아하면 대머리 된다), and the errand that is also the restroom (볼일 보다).

## What's next

The long tail keeps unspooling, and the welds keep paying: every -아/어 + 가다/오다/내다/나다 you meet from here is two words you own. Say the pairs until the space is audible — 잘 살아요, 잘살아요 — and when you need to leave the room, you now have the line: 볼일이 있어서 먼저 갈게요.
