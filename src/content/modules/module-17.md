# Korean, Word-First — Module 17: Hobbies and Sports

취미가 뭐예요? — after your job and your age, Korea's third question. This module is the answer kit: the sports, the instruments, the -방 economy — and the biggest grammar prize left in Band 2, **-는 것**, the machine that turns any verb into a *thing* you can like, own, and be bad at.

> **Warm-up (Module 16):** build these out loud — Module 16 blocks, combinations you've never seen written: *Try sending the email* (이메일을 보내 보세요) · *I go to work at eight* (여덟 시에 출근해요) · *That (thing over there) is important* (저거는 중요해요). If the blocks don't snap together, visit Review first.

**How to use this:** you already own more of this module than you think — 노래, 영화, 게임, 여행, 산, 자전거, 수영하다 have been accumulating since Band 1. Today they get teammates and a grammar engine.

## Part 1 — The suffixes run the leisure industry

Three little syllables organize Korean fun:

- **-구** (ball): 축**구**, 야**구**, 농**구** — kick-ball, field-ball, basket-ball. Learn one syllable, get every sport.
- **-방** (room): 노래**방**, PC**방**, 찜질**방** — the rented-room economy (gap deck). M8's bedroom 방 moonlights as an industry.
- **-치** (hopeless-at): 음**치**, 몸**치**, 길**치** — tone-deaf, dance-clumsy, direction-blind (gap deck). Cheerful incompetence, taxonomized.

And the verbs sort by physics: struck things take **치다** (피아노, 기타, 테니스), stamped things take **찍다** (사진), danced things dance themselves (춤을 **추다** — like 그림을 그리다).

## Part 2 — The bricks

Sports, instruments, screens, and the good-at/bad-at pair.

## Part 3 — The glue

### 1. -는 것 — the verb-to-thing machine

You met 것 in disguise last module: 이거 is just 이 + 거, *this thing*. Now the thing-word goes to work. Put **-는** on a verb stem, add **것**, and the verb becomes a noun phrase:

- 요리하다 → **요리하는 것** — cooking (the activity)
- 저는 요리하는 것을 **좋아해요** — I like cooking
- 제 취미는 사진을 찍는 **것이에요** — my hobby is taking photos
- 운동하는 것을 **별로 안 좋아해요** — I don't really like exercising

This is the answer machine for 취미가 뭐예요? — and far beyond: anything a verb does, -는 것 lets you talk *about*. It's the most consequential pattern in Band 2; let the sentences drill it in.

### 2. 잘하다 / 못하다 — good at, bad at

M10's 잘 and 못, fused into verbs: 노래를 **잘해요** (good at singing), 요리를 **못해요** (bad at cooking). With instruments, keep 치다: 피아노를 잘 쳐요. The gap deck's 잘 못 해요 modesty rule still applies — Koreans deflect compliments; now you can deflect precisely.

### 3. 별로 — the polite shrug

Always with a negative: **별로 안 좋아해요** — don't *really* like it. Softer than 싫어해요, which is for enemies and cilantro.

## Part 4 — Building sentences

**Spot the recycling:** 회식 후에 노래방에 가요 chains M16's dinner into M13's 후에, and 기타를 쳐 보세요 hands this module's instrument to last module's try-machine.

**Fan-culture bingo:** 대박! (gap deck) · 우리 팀 · 화이팅! — you can now survive a stadium, a 노래방, and a group chat about last night's 드라마.

## Part 5 — Practice

Cover the right column and translate; then flip and build the Korean.

1. What's your hobby? → 취미가 뭐예요?
2. I like cooking. → 저는 요리하는 것을 좋아해요.
3. My hobby is taking photos. → 제 취미는 사진을 찍는 것이에요.
4. I'm learning to swim. → 수영하는 것을 배우고 있어요.
5. Try playing the guitar. → 기타를 쳐 보세요.
6. Yesterday we lost, but today we won. → 어제는 졌지만 오늘은 이겼어요.
7. I don't really like exercising. → 운동하는 것을 별로 안 좋아해요.
8. Korean dramas are popular. → 한국 드라마는 인기가 많아요.

Confession drill: three true -는 것 sentences about yourself — one 좋아해요, one 잘해요, one 못해요. The nominalizer only sticks when it's carrying your own life.
