# Korean, Word-First — Module 18: Restaurants and Ordering

Module 3 taught you to eat; Module 9 taught you to shop. This module puts you at the table properly: the metal chopsticks, the bubbling 찌개, the tabletop grill, the delivery app — and the **meal formulas**, the fixed sentences Korean says around food that turn out to be grammar lessons in disguise.

> **Warm-up (Module 17):** build these out loud — Module 17 blocks, combinations you've never seen written: *I like reading comics* (만화를 읽는 것을 좋아해요) · *I'm bad at dancing* (춤을 못 춰요) · *I take photos every week* (매주 사진을 찍어요). If the blocks don't snap together, visit Review first.

**How to use this:** the eating verbs sort by cooking physics — 굽다 (grill), 볶다 (stir-fry), 끓이다 (boil), 비비다 (mix) — and each one names dishes you already know. Learn the verb, decode the menu.

---

## Part 1 — The menu decodes itself

Korean dishes are named by method + material, and you now own both halves:

- **떡볶이** = 떡 (rice cake) + 볶다 (stir-fry) — stir-fried rice cakes
- **비빔밥** = 비비다 (mix) + 밥 — mixed rice
- **삼겹살** = 삼 (three) + 겹 (layer) + 살 (flesh) — M2's Sino three, counting pork layers
- **돼지고기 / 소고기** = pig-meat / cow-meat — animal + M3's 고기, no separate words needed
- **된장 / 고추장** — the 장 (paste) family: fermented soybean, red chili
- **맛집** = taste + house — the restaurant worth the line

At the table: **수저** (spoon + chopsticks, fused), the 반찬 that arrive free and refill forever, and the 그릇 that stays on the table — Korean bowls don't lift.

## Part 3 — The glue

### 1. The meal formulas — grammar in culture

Fixed sentences, memorized whole, said at every meal:

- **잘 먹겠습니다** — before eating: "I will eat well" — thanks to the cook or payer
- **잘 먹었습니다** — after eating: "I ate well"
- **맛있게 드세요** — the server's blessing: "eat deliciously" (M4's 드시다)
- **많이 드세요** — the host's command: "eat a lot" (M17's 많이)

Look closely at the first pair: **먹겠습니다 / 먹었습니다**. One syllable swaps — **-겠-** (will, intention) for **-었-** (did, past) — and gratitude changes tense. You've just met -겠- for the first time, inside a formula you'd have memorized anyway. That's this module's grammar, and it arrived free.

### 2. The ordering arc

All owned machinery, assembled: 여기요! (M9) → 뭐가 맛있어요? **추천해 주세요** → [dish] 주세요 → 포장해 주세요 or here-to-stay → **계산해 주세요** (M9). One new verb — 추천하다 — completes a script you've been building since Band 1.

### 3. Table etiquette, three rules

Wait for the eldest's spoon. Don't lift your 그릇. **건배** clinks lower than your senior's glass, and juniors turn slightly away to drink — M4's politeness ladder runs straight through the table.

## Part 4 — Building sentences

**Spot the recycling:** 축구 경기 후에 치킨을 시켜요 chains M17's match through M13's 후에 into M3's 시키다 (the 치맥 supply chain), and 혼자 먹는 것도 괜찮아요 runs M17's nominalizer into M7's kindest verb.

## Part 5 — Practice

Cover the right column and translate; then flip and build the Korean.

1. (Before eating) → 잘 먹겠습니다!
2. (After eating) → 잘 먹었습니다!
3. What's good here? Please recommend. → 뭐가 맛있어요? 추천해 주세요.
4. After the soccer match, we order chicken. → 축구 경기 후에 치킨을 시켜요.
5. On weekends I grill pork belly with my family. → 주말에 가족하고 삼겹살을 구워요.
6. I was hungry, so I boiled ramyeon. → 배고파서 라면을 끓였어요.
7. The bowl is hot — be careful. → 그릇이 뜨거워요. 조심하세요.
8. Eating alone is fine too. → 혼자 먹는 것도 괜찮아요.

Restaurant run: next Korean meal, run the whole arc out loud — greeting, recommendation request, order, 잘 먹겠습니다, 잘 먹었습니다, 계산해 주세요. Six modules of machinery, one dinner.
