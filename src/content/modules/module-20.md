# Korean, Word-First — Module 20: Phones, Internet, and Messages

The 핸드폰 has been in your pocket since Module 6 — now you learn to actually run it: calls, texts, wi-fi passwords, the upload verb, and the delete verb that's been hiding inside your eraser since Module 15. The glue is the last big hinge of Band 2: **-(으)면** — *if / when* — which turns any two facts into a plan, a warning, or a law of nature.

> **Warm-up (Module 19):** build these out loud — Module 19 blocks, combinations you've never seen written: *Are you close (with them)?* (친해?) · *Let's take a photo!* (사진 찍자!) · *Don't fight* (싸우지 마). If the blocks don't snap together, visit Review first.

**How to use this:** most of this vocabulary is Konglish or self-assembling compounds — 비밀번호 is M19's secret + a number, 영상통화 is video + call, 셀카 is self + camera. Korea built its digital vocabulary from parts you largely own.

## Part 1 — The internet speaks in your old words

The digital verbs are physical metaphors, all recycled:

- **올리다** — to *raise*: you LIFT content onto the internet (사진을 올려요)
- **지우다** — to erase: M15's 지우개 was this verb + the tool suffix all along
- **걸다** — to *hang* a call, same verb as hanging a picture — and M12's 걸리다 was its passive cousin the whole time
- **끊다** — to *cut* a call (and M14's cigarettes: 담배를 끊다)
- **저장하다** — to store: the 장 of 냉장고, refrigerating your data

And the compounds self-assemble: 전화 + 번호, 비밀 + 번호, 영상 + 통화. Read them once and they're yours.

## Part 3 — The glue

### 1. -(으)면 — if / when

The hinge. Consonant stems take **으면**, vowel and ㄹ stems take **면**:

- 시간이 **있으면** 전화해 주세요 — if you have time, call me
- **모르면** 검색해 보세요 — if you don't know, search it
- 비가 **오면** 집에 있을 거예요 — if it rains, I'm staying home

Korean doesn't strongly split *if* from *when* — 도착하면 is both "if you arrive" and "when you arrive." Context carries the certainty.

### 2. The hinge composes

Everything you own plugs into it:

- **-(으)면 안 돼요** — must not: 누르면 안 돼요 (M10's 안 돼요, conditioned)
- **-(으)면 + can**: 연락하면 같이 놀 수 있어요 (M19 + M10)
- **-(으)면 + future**: 알람을 끄면 다시 자요 — a law of nature

One suffix, and half your machines gain an if-switch.

### 3. Phone etiquette, quickly

여보세요? opens. 누구세요? screens. The junior waits for the senior to 끊다 first. And texts answered instantly are 정; texts read and ignored are 읽씹 (gap deck) — provable, timestamped, and litigated between friends.

## Part 4 — Building sentences

**Spot the recycling:** 문자 봤어? 답장해! runs last module's 반말 at texting speed, and 비밀번호를 잊었어요 forgets M19's secret with M15's verb — the compounds keep paying.

::sentences::

**카톡 note (gap deck):** Korea's social life runs on one yellow app — 카톡해! replaced 전화해! for a generation. The 단톡방 (group chat) is where plans actually form; the 1 that vanishes beside your message is how 읽씹 becomes evidence.

## Part 5 — Practice

Cover the right column and translate; then flip and build the Korean.

1. Hello? Who is this? → 여보세요? 누구세요?
2. If you have time, please call me. → 시간이 있으면 전화해 주세요.
3. If you don't know, try searching. → 모르면 검색해 보세요.
4. I lost my phone! → 핸드폰을 잃어버렸어요!
5. What's your phone number? → 전화번호가 뭐예요?
6. Did you see my text? Reply! → 문자 봤어? 답장해!
7. I forgot the password. → 비밀번호를 잊었어요.
8. You must not press this. → 이거 누르면 안 돼요.

Conditional drill: three true -(으)면 sentences about your own phone habits — one plan, one excuse, one law of nature (알람을 끄면…). The hinge only sticks when it's carrying your life.
