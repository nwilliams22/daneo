# Korean, Word-First — Module 5: Counting Things and Telling Time

Module 2 gave you the Sino-Korean numbers (일, 이, 삼…) and promised their sibling system later. This is later. Korean runs **two complete sets of numbers** with a strict division of labor, and today you learn the native set, the counter words that go with it, and — finally — how to tell the time. Which, delightfully, uses *both systems at once*.

**How to use this:** The counters look like bureaucracy but they're just English's "two *cups* of coffee, three *sheets* of paper" made mandatory. Learn the frame once; every counter drops into it.

---

## Part 1 — Two number systems, one division of labor

| | Sino-Korean (Module 2) | Native Korean (now) |
|---|---|---|
| Numbers | 일, 이, 삼, 사, 오… | 하나, 둘, 셋, 넷, 다섯… |
| Runs | dates, minutes, money, phone numbers, floors, math | counting things & people, hours, your age |
| Feel | reading digits | counting on fingers |

**The native set, 1–10:** 하나 · 둘 · 셋 · 넷 · 다섯 · 여섯 · 일곱 · 여덟 · 아홉 · 열 — then it stacks just like Sino: 열하나 (11), 열둘 (12)… and 스물 is 20. Past the 20s–30s, real speech drifts back to Sino numbers, so ten native numbers plus 스물 genuinely covers you.

**Five of them shapeshift before a counter:**

| Counting | Before a counter |
|----------|------------------|
| 하나 | **한** — 한 잔 |
| 둘 | **두** — 두 명 |
| 셋 | **세** — 세 시 |
| 넷 | **네** — 네 개 |
| 스물 | **스무** — 스무 살 |

## Part 3 — The glue

### 1. The counting frame

**[thing] [number] [counter]** — the thing first, then the amount:

- 커피 **두 잔** — two cups of coffee (never 두 커피)
- 사과 **세 개** — three apples
- 생선 **두 마리** — two fish
- 우리 가족은 **네 명**이에요 — my family is four people

개 is the emergency counter — when you don't know the right one, 개 is always understood.

### 2. Telling time — the hybrid clock

**Hours native, minutes Sino.** No one knows a tidy reason; everyone does it:

- 세 시 — 3 o'clock (native 세)
- 세 시 **십 분** — 3:10 (Sino 십)
- 두 시 **반** — 2:30 (반 = half)
- **오전** 아홉 시 / **오후** 세 시 — 9 a.m. / 3 p.m. (the a.m./p.m. word goes first)

### 3. Asking with 몇

몇 slots into the number's seat of any counting frame:

- **몇 시**예요? — what time is it?
- **몇 살**이에요? — how old are you?
- **몇 명**이에요? — how many people?

(Asking someone's age early isn't rude in Korea — Module 4 explained why: the answer decides how you speak to each other. For elders, the noun upgrades: 연세.)

### 4. Age

Native number + **살**: 열 살 (ten years old), 스무 살 (twenty). Note the contraction chain: 스물 → 스무 살.

## Part 4 — Building sentences

**Counted phrases usually shed their object particle in speech** — 커피 두 잔 주문했어요, not 커피를 두 잔을. The counter itself tells you its role.

::sentences::

**Days take time-에** like every time noun: 월요일**에** 학교에 가요 — two 에s, two jobs (when, where-to), zero confusion once you stop translating word-for-word.

## Part 5 — Practice

Cover the right column and translate; then flip and build the Korean.

1. What time is it now? → 지금 몇 시예요?
2. It's 2:30 in the afternoon. → 오후 두 시 반이에요.
3. I ordered two cups of coffee. → 커피 두 잔 주문했어요.
4. How old are you? → 몇 살이에요?
5. My family is four people. → 우리 가족은 네 명이에요.
6. I read three books. → 책 세 권 읽었어요.
7. I go to school on Mondays. → 월요일에 학교에 가요.
8. I have plans on Saturday. → 토요일에 약속이 있어요.

Clock check: read your actual current time out loud, Korean-style — native hour, Sino minute. That's the skill.
