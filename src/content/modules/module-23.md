# Korean, Word-First — Module 23: Travel and Asking the Way

Twenty-two modules of machinery, and now somewhere to take it: the airport, the palace, the beach, the four-way intersection where you finally admit 길을 잃어버렸어요. The garnish is **-(으)면 돼요** — *just do X* — which isn't even new: it's M20's hinge plus M10's 되다, and you already know its evil twin.

> **Warm-up (Module 22):** build these out loud — Module 22 blocks, combinations you've never seen written: *The forest is deeper than the park* (숲이 공원보다 더 깊어요) · *Stars look beautiful* (별이 아름다워 보여요) · *This alley is the most complicated* (골목이 제일 복잡해요). If the blocks don't snap together, visit Review first.

**How to use this:** travel Korean is mostly five sentences said bravely — where is it, is it far, which way, just-do-X, and I'm lost. This module drills all five, then spends the rest on the vocabulary of being somewhere new.

---

## Part 1 — The suffixes pack your bags

The compound machine does tourism now:

- **여권** = travel + 권 (M5's book-counter, promoted to documents) — the passport
- **관광객 / 관광지** = sightseeing + guest / + place — the tourist and where they cluster
- **-관** builds public buildings: 박물**관**, 미술**관** — and 영화관, when you get there
- **바닷가** = M11's sea + edge · **온천** = 온돌's warm + spring · **사거리** = four + streets
- **안내소** = guidance + place — the ⓘ booth this module aims to make optional

And 지내다 — the verb inside M1's 잘 지냈어요? — finally surfaces, twenty-two modules after you first said it.

## Part 3 — The glue

### 1. -(으)면 돼요 — just do X

M20 taught the prohibition: 누르면 **안 돼요**. Drop the 안 and the same machine grants permission — "if you do X, it's fine," which is how Korean says *just*:

- 여기서 지하철을 **타면 돼요** — just take the subway from here
- 저기서 왼쪽으로 **가면 돼요** — just turn left over there
- 두 시간 전에 공항에 **도착하면 돼요** — just arrive two hours early

Every direction you'll ever receive in Korea arrives in this shape, chained.

### 2. The asking-the-way kit

All owned, now assembled: 여기요 (M9) → [place]이/가 **어디에 있어요?** (M6) → **여기서 멀어요?** → listen for 쪽, 사거리, 횡단보도, 건너편 → **감사합니다**. When it fails: **길을 잃어버렸어요**, said cheerfully, restarts the process.

### 3. 쪽 — the direction-maker

M6 gave you 왼쪽/오른쪽 as sealed units; here's the raw part: **쪽** makes any noun directional. 이쪽 (this way), 저쪽 (that way), 바다 쪽 (toward the sea). 어느 쪽이에요? — which way? — is the follow-up question that makes any answer usable.

## Part 4 — Building sentences

**Spot the recycling:** 비행기표가 기차표보다 더 비싸요 runs M22's comparison machine over M12's tickets, 서울에서 궁을 구경했어요 sends M22's 구경 to the palace, and 부산은 바닷가가 제일 유명해요 lets 제일 do the travel brochure's job.

::sentences::

**Proverb check (gap deck):** 금강산도 식후경 — even the legendary mountain ranks below lunch. Plan your 일정 accordingly; Korea already has.

## Part 5 — Practice

Cover the right column and translate; then flip and build the Korean.

1. Excuse me, where is the museum? → 여기요, 박물관이 어디에 있어요?
2. Is it far from here? → 여기서 멀어요?
3. Just turn left over there. → 저기서 왼쪽으로 가면 돼요.
4. Just take the subway from here. → 여기서 지하철을 타면 돼요.
5. Don't forget your passport! → 여권을 잊지 마세요!
6. I'm lost. → 길을 잃어버렸어요.
7. Plane tickets are more expensive than train tickets. → 비행기표가 기차표보다 더 비싸요.
8. Coming home after a trip is the best. → 여행 후에 집에 돌아오면 제일 좋아요.

Route drill: give directions from your front door to your nearest station, entirely in -(으)면 돼요 chains. If a lost tourist could follow it, Band 2 has done its job.
