# Korean, Word-First — Module 67: The Inner Weather

M7's 기분 note called it your inner weather; this module is the full forecast. Nothing here debuts — both headline machines were taught modules ago, and today they ship at scale: the -아/어하다 factory (M42's 미워하다 filed its report) turns four feeling-adjectives into watchable verbs, the -(으)ㅁ freezer (named a machine in M33's 웃음 note) turns six verbs into nouns, and M28's 나다 — "the door feelings always use," per M42's 울음 note — admits 기억나다 and 생각나다. Around the machines, the inner life stocks its shelves: the felt worth of effort (보람), the day's charge (기운), the mind's own science (심리), and the two alarms Korean actually shouts (큰일 났어요! · 웬일이에요?).

**How to use this:** the two factories are the module — learn each rule once and eight of the words come nearly free; spend your reps on the radar pairs (흥미/관심, 기운/힘, 염려하다/걱정하다, 피로하다/피곤하다) and on the recall triangle, which English collapses into one word and Korean splits into three.

## Part 1 — Two factories and a door

- **The -아/어하다 factory (M42):** adjective = my inside; -아/어하다 verb = someone ELSE's outside, watchable. M1's 좋아하다 was this all along; M42's 미워하다 named the machine. Today's shipment: 기뻐하다, 슬퍼하다, 부러워하다, 즐거워하다.
- **The -(으)ㅁ freezer:** previewed the day M32's 느끼다 promised 느낌, named in M33's 웃음 note, load-bearing by M42's 울음. You already own 잠 (M28), 꿈 (M8), 웃음 (M33), 걸음 (M38), 느낌 (M34), 얼음 (M40), 울음 (M42); today six more go in.
- **The 나다-door:** M19's 화나다 was the first customer; M34's 기억 note ran the frame (기억이 나요). Feelings, memories, and crises ERUPT in Korean — they aren't retrieved.
- **The recall triangle:** 기억하다 (M19) = you reach for it · 기억나다/생각나다 = it comes to you · 떠올리다 = you summon it. Effort, arrival, invocation.

## Part 3 — The machines at full production

### 1. -아/어하다 — feelings, worn on the outside

Korean grammar is strict about other minds: 슬퍼요 reports YOUR inside; to narrate someone else's, point at what shows — 슬퍼해요. The factory conjugates first, then welds 하다 on: 기쁘다 → 기뻐하다, 부럽다 → 부러워하다 (ㅂ→워). And the product is a true verb: it takes 을/를 — M1's 좋아하다 has been demonstrating this since week one — and runs the progressive: 아직도 슬퍼하고 있어요.

### 2. -(으)ㅁ — the noun freezer

Vowel stems take bare ㅁ (만나다 → 만남, 싸우다 → 싸움); batchim stems take -음 (묻다 → 물음, where the ㄷ-irregular's ㄹ survives in the noun — 걸음's rule, per M38); the ㅂ-family softens first (어렵다 → 어려움, 부끄럽다 → 부끄러움). One product ships pre-shrunk: 어둡다 → 어둠, never 어두움. The output is a THING — countable, ownable, transferable: 만남이 있었어요.

**Calibration:** -(으)ㅁ freezes the event solid and formal; -기 (M13) keeps the doing warm and in progress. 물음 sits in a textbook heading; 묻기 sits on a to-do list. In casual speech Korean usually reaches for -는 것 (M17) and avoids choosing at all.

### 3. The recall triangle — and the door it uses

기억하다 (M19) is a hand reaching into the archive. 기억나다 and 생각나다 are the archive delivering unbidden — X가 나다, the eruption door of M28's 나다, the one M19's 화나다 opened first. 떠올리다 is the deliberate summons: 떠오르다 (M49) with the causative party (M37) behind it. English 'remember' flattens all three; Korean bills you for the difference in agency — and 큰일 났어요 is the same door at crisis volume.
