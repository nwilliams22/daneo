# Korean, Word-First — Module 13: Home and Rooms

You've been coming 집에 since Module 1 — now you finally get to walk around inside. Rooms, furniture, the appliance fleet, and the small verbs that run a household: turn on, turn off, open, close, tidy, throw away. The grammar garnish is the **before/after pair** — **-기 전에** and **-(으)ㄴ 후에** — which turns any two sentences into a sequence.

> **Warm-up (Module 12):** build these out loud — Module 12 blocks, combinations you've never seen written, which is the point: *I'm going to buy a transit card* (교통카드를 사러 가요) · *How long does it take to Busan?* (부산까지 얼마나 걸려요?) · *Please stop at the bus stop* (정류장에서 세워 주세요). If the blocks don't snap together, visit Review first.

**How to use this:** the vocabulary is the payload — Band 2's standing rule — and most of it is honest compounding you can decode on sight. Read Part 1 before the checklist and half the words will feel pre-learned.

## Part 1 — The Korean home, in words that explain themselves

First, the setting: urban Korea overwhelmingly lives in the **아파트** — numbered towers, numbered floors (**층**), and an **엘리베이터** doing the vertical commuting. Which floor? **몇 층?** — M5's counter machinery never stopped working.

Inside, the words are job descriptions:

- **침실** = 침 (sleep — the one in 침대) + 실 (room) — the sleeping room
- **화장실** — you've had it since Module 6; notice the same 실 now
- **냉장고** = cold + storage + warehouse — the refrigerator, described
- **세탁기** = laundry + 기 (machine) — the same 기 that flies in 비행기
- **옷장** = clothes + 장 (storage) — the 장 also working in 냉장고 and M12's 주차장
- **열쇠** = 열다 (open) + 쇠 (metal) — a key is "open-metal"

Two culture anchors, both in the gap deck: the **현관**, where every shoe in Korea stops, and the **온돌** floor — heating lives *under* the **바닥**, which is why the floor is furniture: you sit on it, eat at low tables on it, and an **이불** on a warm floor beats a cold bed. That's also why the street's shoes never come past the entryway.

## Part 2 — The bricks

The rooms, the objects, the household verbs, and an adjective set that slots straight into your irregular families.

::vocab::

**Family check-in, new members only:** ㅂ-irregulars gain 어둡다, 더럽다, 시끄럽다 (어두워요, 더러워요, 시끄러워요) — while 좁다 stays regular like 입다. The ㅡ-drop family gains tiny 끄다 (꺼요). And 밝다/넓다 bring the double-batchim story from the sound interlude: 박따/널따 alone, both letters waking before a vowel — 밝아요, 넓어요.

## Part 3 — The glue

### 1. -기 전에 — before doing

**-기** turns a verb into a little noun; **전에** means "before it." Stem + 기, no exceptions, no sound rules:

- **자기 전에** 핸드폰을 꺼요 — before sleeping, I turn off my phone
- 친구가 **오기 전에** 집을 청소해요 — before my friend comes, I clean the house

Note the second one swallowed a whole clause, subject included — 전에 doesn't mind.

### 2. -(으)ㄴ 후에 — after doing

**후에** means "after it," but it wants the verb stamped as finished first: consonant stem + **은**, vowel stem + **ㄴ**:

- 손님이 **간 후에** 정리했어요 — after the guests left, I tidied up
- **먹은 후에** — after eating

### 3. Plain nouns need no surgery

Both words take a bare noun directly — **수업 전에** (before class), **수업 후에** (after class). You already say 오전/오후 — morning and afternoon are just "noon-before" and "noon-after." This machine was hiding in Module 5 all along.

## Part 4 — Building sentences

**Spot the recycling:** 새 집을 보러 가요 runs Module 12's -(으)러 가다 with brand-new cargo, and 지하철역까지 오 분 걸려요 is M12's 걸리다 measuring the walk from your new front door.

::sentences::

**Listing-Korean bingo:** 방은 좁지만 깨끗해요 · 엘리베이터가 있어요 · 지하철역까지 오 분 — you can now read half of every apartment ad in Seoul.

## Part 5 — Practice

Cover the right column and translate; then flip and build the Korean.

1. Before sleeping, I turn off my phone. → 자기 전에 핸드폰을 꺼요.
2. After class, I rest at home. → 수업 후에 집에서 쉬어요.
3. What floor do you live on? → 몇 층에 살아요?
4. Next week I'm moving to a new apartment. → 다음 주에 새 아파트로 이사해요.
5. Please turn on the light. → 불을 켜 주세요.
6. Before my friend comes, I clean the house. → 친구가 오기 전에 집을 청소해요.
7. The room is cramped but clean. → 방은 좁지만 깨끗해요.
8. It was noisy outside, so I closed the window. → 밖이 시끄러워서 창문을 닫았어요.

Room-tour drill: stand in your own home and narrate a room per sentence — what it is, one thing in it, one adjective. 거실이에요. 소파가 있어요. 아주 편해요. Pure recycled machinery, new address.
