# Korean, Word-First — Module 48: The Makers

Ring 1 built you a consumption habit — 책 (M1), 영화 (M2), 신문 (M8), the 드라마/만화/독서 shelf (M17), 뉴스 (M24), and M32's 방송/광고/프로그램; M44 hung 문학 over it all. This module walks behind the page and the camera: the MAKERS (작가, 배우, 시인, and the -가 suffix that houses them), the WORKS (작품, 장면, 일기, the bedtime shelf), the APPARATUS (방송국, 촬영, 언론), and the AUDIENCE, sorted by medium (관객, 시청자, 독자). The glue is the decision machine: **-기로 하다** — M13's -기 plus M6's (으)로 plus 하다: a decision is a direction taken. 매일 일기를 쓰기로 했어요 — today you learn to put resolutions on record.

**How to use this:** the shelf is hanja arithmetic and you own nearly every part — read the decodes first and let 家, 記, and 者 do the memorizing. The radar patrol is the densest in Band 5 (연기 alone runs three ways); trust the notes, and keep the maker/audience pairs together in your reps.

---

## Part 1 — The house a skill lives in

M45 taught you to think in factories; this shelf runs its own:

- **The -가 (家) maker-suffix:** 家 is the house hanja — the one inside 가족 (M4) — and hung on a field it means the person so practiced they LIVE there: 작가, 소설가, 화가, 음악가, 예술가 — one suffix, five careers. Radar before it bites: 가수 (M4) wears song-歌, a different 가 entirely.
- **The person-suffixes keep paying:** M43's 소비자 taught the person-者 — today it files 저자, 시청자, and garnish 독자; M23's 외국인 anchors 시인; and 관객 is 관광객 (M23) minus the 광: the guest, reseated.
- **The audience sorts by medium:** 관객 fills seats, 시청자 fills living rooms, 독자 turns pages — three words English flattens into 'audience.'
- **The writers sort by register:** 작가 is the career, 소설가 the genre specialist, 저자 the colophon entry, 시인 the one the subway doors quote — four job titles, one desk.
- **The apparatus decodes too:** -가 houses a skill, but -국 (약국 M6, 우체국 M27) houses an institution — 방송국 is M32's 방송 given a bureau.

## Part 2 — The bricks

**Radar patrol:** this shelf collapses everywhere, all mapped: 연기 is acting (演技), smoke (연기가 나다), AND a postponement (공연 연기 — read the poster carefully) · 일기 is the diary (日記) but 일기 예보 is the weather (日氣) · 기사 the article (記事) vs M12's 기사님 the driver (技士) · 화가 the painter vs M19's 화가 나다 — 화가가 화가 나면 is legal Korean · 배우 the actor vs 배우다 (M2), though actors do learn lines · and M31's 시청 note settles its own bill: city hall is 市廳, but 시청자 watch-and-listen (視聽).

## Part 3 — The glue: putting a decision on record

### -기로 하다 — decide-to

M13's -기 turns any verb into a noun, M6's (으)로 points a direction at it, and 하다 takes it: to decide is to choose a heading. Its natural habitat is the past — 매일 운동하기로 **했어요** = I've decided to exercise daily — because 했어요 doesn't mean the deciding ended; it means the decision STANDS. Break it and Korean notices: 담배를 끊기로 했잖아요.

### The appointment reading

Between two people the same machine books calendars: 내일 만나**기로 했어요** = we agreed to meet tomorrow; 공연을 보기로 했어요 = it's planned, both of us know. An agreement, not a hope.

**Calibration:** M34's -(으)ㄹ 것 같아요 guesses, M8's -(으)ㄹ 거예요 states your own plan, and -기로 했어요 is a commitment on record — signed either with yourself (the resolution) or with someone else (the appointment). It's the New Year's ending, the quit-smoking ending, and the reason flaking on plans requires an apology.

## Part 4 — Building sentences

**Spot the recycling:** M25's -(으)ㄹ 때 and M31's 마다 sit at grandmother's bedside, M43's (이)나 counts a hundred channels in disbelief, M42's -잖아요 praises the all-rounder 배우, M24's badge signs the novel and hunts the TV schedule, and the written register's busy-sign 중 guards the film set.
