# Korean, Word-First — Module 61: The Verb Sweep

Band 6 makes its verb pass: thirty-four verbs, zero new machinery. Everything here is assembled from parts you already own — 찾다 rides M6's come/go compass into three directions, M28's 내다 and 놓다 weld into the hands-verbs, and the happen-machines of M35 and M36 graduate their whole tail class at once (없어지다, 불리다, 발견되다…). Plus two double agents the dictionary insists you take whole: 미치다 (reach — and crazy) and 취하다 (take — and drunk).

**How to use this:** read every seam before you drill — almost every word splits into taught parts, and the split IS the memory. Spend your reps on the doubles (미치다, 취하다) and the active/happens pairs (쌓다/쌓이다, 벌이다/벌어지다): Korean grammar lives in exactly that gap.

## Part 3 — The happen-machine, graduating class

### 1. The -아/어지다 row (M35's machine)

느껴지다, 없어지다, 만들어지다, 커지다, 벌어지다 — the world doing things while you watch. M35 built the machine on adjectives (추워지다); today it runs on the big verbs: feelings arrive (느껴져요), wallets vanish (없어졌어요), problems grow (커졌어요).

### 2. The passive-party row (M36's 이/히/리/기)

쌓이다 (이), 불리다 (리), 담기다 (기) — each one an earlier verb turned around: 쌓다, 부르다 (M19), 담다 (M40). The star frame is naming: **X라고 불려요** = is called X, M32's quote-라고 doing the pinning.

### 3. The 되다-row (the swap-the-하다 move, M36)

발견되다, 진행되다, 사용되다, 생각되다, 관련되다 — M36 taught the trick on 시작되다; here is the register that lives on it: announcements, headlines, reports. 회의가 진행되고 있습니다 is how institutions breathe.

**Calibration:** the 되다-row is formal furniture — natural in print and on microphones, stiff at dinner. In speech, Korean keeps the active voice or reaches for the 것 같다 hedge (M34). Learn to READ this row everywhere and to say it sparingly.
