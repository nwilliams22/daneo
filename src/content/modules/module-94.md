# Korean, Word-First — Module 94: Motion and Mishap

Nine modules of Band 7 have been the essay's — nouns of state, structure, existence, and the endings that weigh them. This one goes back to the body and the native verb shelf, because the endings that are left on the checklist are the ones a STORY needs: how you were standing when it happened, what you were in the middle of, what you kept doing until you noticed, and how close it came. Thirty-four Grade-C verbs — every one a weld or a party product of something you own (the passive party of M36, the causative party of M37, the -지다 wing from M35, the 뛰다 chassis of M68) — and the dependent noun 채. The thesis for the glue: **Korean narrates accidents with auxiliaries.** The verb says what you were doing; the ending says how it went wrong — **-(으)ㄴ 채** (in the state of), **-다가** (in the middle of, and then), **-다 보니 / -다 보면** (kept at it and found / if you keep at it), and the four endings of mishap: **-(으)ㄹ 뻔하다** (almost), **-고 말다** (ended up), **-아/어 대다** (keeps on, annoyingly), **-기 일쑤다** (is always doing).

**How to use this:** decode every verb into its parts before you drill it — 넘기다 is M36's 넘다 through the causative party of M37, 실리다 is M61's 싣다 through the passive party of M36, 둘러싸다 is 두르다 in its 르-coat plus a wrap-싸다 you have not met — and let the parts carry the meaning. Then read Parts 2–5 as one lesson on ONE sentence shape: [what I was doing] + [how it went]. The table at the end of Part 5 is the module on four lines, and the sentence deck is eight small disasters; read it for the endings, not the damage.

## Part 1 — The bricks: four drawers of the body in motion

Three earlier notes left the door open: M86's 위기 note ran 위기를 넘기다 with the verb frozen and said outright that it belongs to M94; M90's 범죄 note did the same with 범죄를 저지르다; the M86 gap deck did it with 사정없이 때리다. This is the module they were pointing at, and the four drawers are the four things a body does to the world:

- **PUT AND WEAR (걸치다 두르다 대다 삼다 감추다 둘러싸다 덧붙이다 매달리다 들이다 익히다 거두다 넘기다):** drape, wrap, press against, take-as, hide, surround, add on, hang from, let in, cook through, gather in, pass over — twelve ways of placing a thing, and five of them are party products: 들이다 and 익히다 and 넘기다 through the causative 이/히/기 of M37, 매달리다 through the passive 리 of M36, 덧붙이다 on M37's 붙이다.
- **GO WRONG (저지르다 때리다 치르다 쓰러지다 흩어지다 빠져나가다 퍼지다 외치다 일어서다 앞장서다):** commit, hit, pay-and-endure, collapse, scatter, slip out, spread, shout, stand up again, take the lead — the verbs of the incident and its aftermath. Three ride the -지다 wing from M35 (쓰러지다 흩어지다 퍼지다); two are welds on M14's 서다 (일어서다 앞장서다); two are 르-verbs from the 르-family table of M7 (저지르다 치르다: 저질러요, 치러요).
- **LIGHT AND WEIGHT (비치다 비추다 띄다 빛나다 실리다 기울이다 다지다 맺다 아끼다 더불다):** light landing (비치다), light aimed (비추다), a thing lifted into the eye (띄다), a thing giving light (빛나다) — M49's 빛 shelf gets its verbs; then weight — carried (실리다), tipped (기울이다), pressed firm (다지다), tied (맺다), spared (아끼다), and one fossil, 더불어, that only lives in its -아/어 coat.
- **THE DO-VERBS (이러다 그러다):** M33's 이렇게/그렇게 + 하다, contracted. 이러다가 늦겠어요 = at this rate we'll be late; 그러다가 어느 날 = and then one day. They exist to carry Part 3's ending, and they open half the stories told in this language.

::vocab::

**Radar patrol:** 두르다 wraps, M38's 서두르다 hurries, M42's 서투르다 fumbles — three -두르다 shapes, one wrap · 대다 puts a thing against another; M55's 대 counts machines and scores · 삼다 says 삼따 exactly as M40's 삶다 does — one adopts, one boils — and M2's 삼 is three · 매달리다's 달리다 is the hang-달다 of 매달다, NOT M17's 달리다 (run) · the 싸다 inside 둘러싸다 wraps; M7's 싸다 is cheap · 비치다 has M49's 빛 in it, not M11's 비 · 익히다 is two NIKL rows, 고기를 (cook through) and 기술을 (master), one causative · 때리다 beats a body, M17's 치다 strikes a thing or plays it · 쓰러지다 is the hospital, M36's 넘어지다 the pavement, M88's 무너지다 the building · 아끼다 is not 아 + M21's 끼다 · and 채 is three rows too: the state-noun of Part 2, the counter of 집 한 채, and the adverb not-yet — none of them the 채 of M18's 야채.

## Part 2 — The glue: -(으)ㄴ 채(로) — in the state of

The past badge -(으)ㄴ from M24 + **채** (the dependent noun on today's shelf) + an optional 로: one action is FROZEN, and the main clause happens inside it. 옷을 입은 **채로** 잤어요 (M8's 입다, M7's 옷, M1's 자다) = slept with my clothes on; 신발을 신은 **채** 들어갔어요 (M21's 신다, M9's 신발, M28's 들어가다) = went in with shoes still on — the sentence every Korean floor has heard; 눈을 감은 **채** (M14's 눈, M41's 감다) = with eyes closed; 창문을 열어 놓은 채 (-아/어 놓다 from M40 inside it) = with the window left open. Always the PAST badge, because the state was already made when the main clause began; and nearly always a state that should have been undone — 채 carries a faint shrug. Sort it against its two neighbours: -아/어 놓다/두다 from M40 (that md: "the work is banked") makes a state ON PURPOSE and leaves it for later; -(으)면서 from M38 (that md: "it's ONE body multitasking") runs two actions at the same time; 채 is one action frozen and forgotten while another happens.

**Calibration:** 창문을 열었어요 (I opened it) · 창문을 열어 놓았어요 (I opened it and left it — banked) · 창문을 연 채 잤어요 (I slept with it open — and should not have). The third is the one with the cold in it.

## Part 3 — The glue: -다가 — in the middle of, and then

Verb stem + **다가**, no vowel fuss: the first action was UNDER WAY when the second interrupted it. 공부하다가 잠들었어요 (M1's 공부하다, M74's 잠들다) = fell asleep while studying; 가다가 넘어졌어요 (M1's 가다, M36's 넘어지다) = fell on the way; 비가 오다가 그쳤어요 (M11's 비 and 그치다) = it rained, then stopped. The do-verbs live here: **이러다가** 늦겠어요 = at this rate we'll be late (the -겠- of M49), **그러다가** 어느 날 (M27's 어느, M11's 날) = and then one day — the storyteller's hinge. Then the tense trick: put -았/었 INSIDE it and the first action was COMPLETED, so the second reverses it — 문을 **열었다가** 닫았어요 (M13's 열다 and 닫다) = opened the door, then closed it; 샀다가 환불했어요 (M2's 사다; 환불 — a refund, riding frozen) = bought it, then returned it. 열다가 닫았어요 would mean the door was half open when you shut it; 열었다가 means it was open. Sort: -(으)면서 from M38 holds two actions together; -자마자 from M38 has no gap between them; -다가 is a switch — the first stops, or is undone, when the second arrives. In speech it clips to -다 (이러다 큰일 나요 — the gap deck).

**Calibration:** 가면서 전화했어요 (called while walking) · 가자마자 전화했어요 (called the moment I arrived) · 가다가 전화했어요 (was on my way, stopped, and called). Three clocks; only the third interrupts.

## Part 4 — The glue: -다 보니 / -다 보면 — kept at it and found / if you keep at it

-다가 clipped to -다, + M1's 보다 in two coats. **-다 보니** (보다 + the -(으)니 of -(으)니까 from M10, shortened): you kept doing the first thing, looked up, and FOUND the second — 계속 하다 보니 익숙해졌어요 (M28's 계속, M70's 익숙해지다) = I kept at it and got used to it; 그러다 보니 = and doing that, I found; 한국에 살다 보니 매운 음식을 좋아하게 됐어요 (M4's 살다, M3's 맵다 in the badge, M1's 음식, -게 되다 from M35) — the expat's whole biography. M61's 어쩌다 note ran the frame ahead of time ("어쩌다 보니 = one thing led to another (gap deck)") and this is the grammar inside it. **-다 보면** (보다 + -(으)면 from M20): if you keep at it, you WILL find — 살다 보면 그런 날도 있어요 (M24's 그런, M11's 날) = if you live long enough there are days like that, the national consolation; 하다 보면 돼요 = keep at it and it works, the national encouragement. Sort: -(으)니까 from M10 gives a REASON you are asserting; -(으)면 from M20 gives a CONDITION; 보니 and 보면 both insist on DURATION — the result came from keeping on, not from one act — and 보니 says it already happened to me, 보면 that it will happen to you.

**Calibration:** 살아서 알아요 (I know because I live here — a reason) · 살면 알아요 (if you live here you'll know — a condition) · 살다 보면 알아요 (live here a while and you'll come to know — duration). Only the third has time inside it.

## Part 5 — The glue: the four endings of mishap

Four auxiliaries, one job each — the verb says what you did, the ending says how it went wrong. **-(으)ㄹ 뻔하다:** prospective badge + 뻔 (a dependent noun, frozen) + 하다, ALWAYS past — 넘어질 뻔했어요 (M36's 넘어지다) = I almost fell; 죽을 뻔했어요 (M14's 죽다; the gap deck) = I nearly died. A near-miss, known afterwards. **-고 말다:** verb--고 from M10 + 말다, the 말 of -지 마세요 from M14 as an auxiliary — 결국 울고 말았어요 (M32's 결국, M7's 울다) = ended up crying; 잠들고 말았어요 (M74's 잠들다). A completion the speaker regrets — the cousin of -아/어 버리다 from M52 (that md: "finished, unrecoverable, and you have feelings about it"): 버리다 is the mouth's version with the feelings audible; 말다 is the narrator's, the one a novel writes. **-아/어 대다:** today's 대다 as an auxiliary — 떠들어 대다 (M51's 떠들다) = keep on making a racket, 먹어 대다 (M1's 먹다) = keep stuffing yourself, 울어 대다 = cry and cry. Repetition with the speaker's eyebrows up; never a compliment. **-기 일쑤다:** the -기 nominalizer from M13 + 일쑤 (a dependent noun, frozen: the habitual mishap) + 이다 — 지각하기 일쑤예요 (M16's 지각하다) = is forever late; 잊기 일쑤예요 (M15's 잊다) = always forgetting. A verdict on a pattern, always a bad one.

| Ending | Meaning | Example | The ending it resembles |
|---|---|---|---|
| -(으)ㄹ 뻔하다 | almost — a near-miss, told afterwards | 넘어질 뻔했어요 — I almost fell | -(으)ㄹ 것 같다 from M34, but only in the past, and it did NOT happen |
| -고 말다 | ended up — a regretted completion | 울고 말았어요 — I ended up crying | -아/어 버리다 from M52, with the feelings written rather than spoken |
| -아/어 대다 | keeps on — repetition that annoys | 떠들어 대요 — they keep making noise | -아/어 놓다 from M40, but stacking actions instead of results |
| -기 일쑤다 | is always doing — a bad habit as a verdict | 지각하기 일쑤예요 — is forever late | 자주 from M8, with a frown attached and the copula at the end |

**Calibration:** 넘어졌어요 (I fell) · 넘어질 뻔했어요 (I almost fell) · 넘어지고 말았어요 (I ended up falling — and here comes the story) · 넘어지기 일쑤예요 (I am always falling). Same verb, four distances from the ground.

## Part 6 — Building sentences

**Spot the recycling:** the past badge -(으)ㄴ from M24 freezes 감정을 감춘 under 채 and colours 이상한 소문; -아/어서 from M10 gives the reason in 너무 더워서 and hands the fans' shouting to the song in 외쳐 대서; -(으)면 from M20 closes 연습하다 보면 and opens 급하게 일하면; -게 되다 from M35 finishes 익히게 돼요; the long negation from M28 empties 아무 말도 하지 않았어요 and M3's 안 and 못 sit before 들렸어요 and 자다가; the passive party of M36 supplies 들리다; the -게 adverbializer from M33 makes 급하게; M30's only-만 narrows 회사 일에만; and M70's 멀어지다, M74's 잠들다 and M32's 결국 keep the jobs their notes gave them.

**Gap deck:** the warning to the child on the chair (이러다 큰일 나요), the near-death that happens weekly (죽을 뻔했어요), and the ear tipped toward the speaker (귀를 기울이다).
