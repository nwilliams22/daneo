# Korean, Word-First — Module 47: The Second Ladder

Ring 1's M4 handed you the nuclear family — and, almost in passing, a starter kit it never finished: a swap table (자다 → 주무시다, 나이 → 연세, 집 → 댁), the marker -(으)시-, and a promise that Korean honors the person you talk ABOUT, not just the person you talk to. Forty-three modules later, this module pays that table off in full. The family map grows outward — in-laws (the 시- prefix), mom's side (the 외- prefix), grandchildren, the generation words — and the grammar grows upward: **께서**, the subject particle for people who outrank you, plus **만큼**, the particle that measures one family member against another. Korean runs TWO politeness ladders; today you climb the second.

**How to use this:** learn the honorific shelf as a SYSTEM — every entry names its plain twin (자다/주무시다, 나이/연세, 생일/생신), so drill them as pairs, the way M4's swap table always intended. The kinship nouns are cheap by comparison: two prefixes (시-, 외-) and one arithmetic trick (촌수) generate most of the map.

---

## Part 1 — The map is drawn in prefixes

Korean kinship is a coordinate system, not a word list:

- **The 시- axis (husband's side):** 시- stamps his whole family — 시어머니, 시아버지, 시집. A woman traditionally married INTO that house, hence 시집가다 = she marries; a man 장가가다-s — he historically moved in with the in-laws instead. The asymmetric verb pair is the culture lesson.
- **The 외- axis (mother's side):** the outside-外 of 외국 (M23) files your mother's entire family as OUTSIDE — 외할머니, 외삼촌, 외가. A patriarchy fossil, preserved in a prefix; the 외할머니 doing school pickup runs the inside of everything anyway.
- **The 촌수 arithmetic:** Korea measures kinship in 촌 (degrees): parents are 1촌 away, siblings 2촌 — so dad's brother is literally NAMED his distance: 삼촌, 'three degrees.' Your cousin is a 사촌, one step further.
- **The 님-upgrade:** M16's 님-ladder reaches home — 아버님, 어머님: the forms for in-laws, other people's parents, and formal occasions.

## Part 3 — The glue: the second ladder

### 1. 께서 — the honored subject

Politeness endings (M4's -요, 합니다) honor the LISTENER. The second ladder honors the SUBJECT — and it moves as a set: 할아버지**께서** 신문을 읽**으세요**. The particle replaces 이/가 when the doer outranks you; the verb agrees with -(으)시- (M4's marker, finally meeting its particle); and a small vocabulary swaps in whole — 주무시다, 드시다 (M4), 잡수시다, 계시다 (M4). It stacks like its plain twins (께서는, 께서도), and M27's 께 is its dative sibling: 께 sends things TO the honored; 께서 marks the honored DOING.

M4's two rules of thumb still govern: never honor yourself (no 저께서, ever), and when in doubt about someone's rank — honor them.

### 2. 만큼 — the equative

M41's 처럼 note promised 'the measuring cousin'; here it is: noun + **만큼** = as much as that noun. 막내가 형**만큼** 커요 — the youngest is exactly brother-sized. S3's proverb (아는 만큼 보인다) and M42's 그만큼 were both running on credit; the debt clears today.

**Calibration:** three particles now compare — 보다 (M22) RANKS (형보다 커요: taller, someone loses), 만큼 EQUATES (형만큼 커요: a tie, measured), 처럼 (M41) PAINTS (형처럼 커요: big in his manner). Ranking, measuring, resembling — pick your instrument.
