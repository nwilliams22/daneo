# Korean, Word-First — Module 145: Exhibitions, Advice and Everyday Care

You have the application form. Now send it, visit the exhibition, and find out who is hiring. This pack moves between public errands and the smaller work of looking after someone.

**How to use this:** Learn the words first. Then read each sentence as three views of the same scene: natural English, Korean-order gloss, and Korean.

---

## Part 1 — Follow the noun to the scene

**A place grows out of an action.** 전시하다 from M144 means exhibit. 전시장 is the venue: the same display idea with a place attached. 모집하다 belongs on its noticeboard when the venue needs people; 신청서 can now travel 우편으로, by post.

**Two clocks, one day.** 정오 is twelve at midday. 한낮 is a stretch of bright daytime. You can miss an appointment at 정오 and still arrive 한낮에. 차차 works on a different scale: a change happens gradually, without a clock deadline.

**The kitchen shelf.** 도마 is where you cut; 식기 is what you serve with. 수입품 tells you the goods came from abroad, while 싼값 tells you about the price. Origin, purpose and price are three separate claims.

**Eyes can do more than move.** You already know the eye noun 눈 and the verbs 감다 and 뜨다. Their compounds 눈감다 and 눈뜨다 keep the physical actions and open figurative readings. Today's medal sentence is literal; the gap card's overlooking is deliberate.

**Earlier vocabulary:** 눈감다 → M68; 눈뜨다 → M68. These words return in the examples as review; the 34 entries below are this pack’s new checklist.

## Part 2 — Building sentences

No new glue. The familiar patterns let these nouns do useful work immediately.

**Spot the recycling:** M144's 앨범 and 전시하다 share an exhibition trip; 신청서 receives a postal reply; 일자리 belongs to a spouse's remembered search. These are new combinations of familiar blocks.

Watch three connections:

- **-는 김에 from M91:** attach an extra action to a trip already happening.
- **-았/었더니 / -던 from M88:** report what followed an action, or look back at an ongoing situation.
- **-고서 / -고도 from M85:** distinguish after doing something from even after doing it.

Eight sentences put the vocabulary into familiar grammar frames.

::sentences::

The understood speaker need not appear as 저는. Keep the Korean subject where Korean puts it: 답장이 왔어요 literally gives the reply its own arrival.

**Gap deck:** 눈감아 주다 lets a fault slide; 도마 위에 오르다 puts an issue under scrutiny. The words are concrete, but the scenes need not be.
