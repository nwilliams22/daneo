# Korean, Word-First — Module 8: Routines and the Future

You can describe the world; now you get to move through it on schedule — and past it, into tomorrow. This module delivers the daily-routine verbs (get up, wash, head out, rest) and the two tenses that complete your kit: **what you're doing right now** (-고 있어요) and **what you'll do next** (-(으)ㄹ 거예요). After this, you can narrate a whole day in three tenses.

**How to use this:** Part 1 is your day, told in order, using every new verb once. Read it aloud twice and half the vocab list is pre-installed.

---

## Part 1 — A day, in Korean

Follow the timeline — every bold word is in this module's checklist:

> 아침에 **일찍** **일어나요**. 얼굴을 **씻고** 이를 **닦아요**. **샤워하고** 옷을 **입어요**. **준비하고** **나가요**.
> 회사에서 일을 **시작해요** … 여섯 시에 **끝나요**.
> **가끔** 공원에서 **운동해요**. **보통** 집에서 **쉬어요** — 티비를 보고, **게임**을 하고, **가끔** **빨래하고** 방을 **청소해요**.
> **밤**에 **전화하고**, **신문**을 읽고, 자요. **하루**가 끝나요.

*(That sneaky -고 linking verbs inside one sentence — 씻고, 샤워하고 — is the same -고 from -고 싶어요 and -고 있어요, quietly doing "and" between verbs. Module 10 makes it official; for now just absorb the rhythm.)*

## Part 3 — The glue

### 1. Right now: -고 있어요

Stem + **고 있어요** — you're in the middle of it:

- 밥을 먹**고 있어요** — I'm eating
- 친구를 기다리**고 있어요** — I'm waiting for my friend

있다 moonlights yet again: exists → have → is-located → *is-in-the-middle-of*. Four jobs, one verb, and you knew it in Module 1.

### 2. The future: -(으)ㄹ 거예요

Drop 다, then: vowel stem → hook on **ㄹ 거예요**; consonant stem → add **을 거예요**:

| Verb | Future |
|------|--------|
| 쉬다 | 쉴 거예요 — will rest |
| 가다 | 갈 거예요 — will go |
| 먹다 | 먹**을** 거예요 — will eat |
| 청소하다 | 청소할 거예요 — will clean |
| 바쁘다 | 바쁠 거예요 — will be busy |

One nuance to bank now: about **yourself**, it's a plan; about **others**, it shades into "probably" — 친구는 지금 바쁠 거예요 (she's probably busy). The gap deck holds that thought.

### 3. The frequency ladder

**항상** (always) → **보통** (usually) → **자주** (often) → **가끔** (sometimes) — parked before the place and verb: 저는 **보통** 지하철을 타요. Plus the day's bookends: **일찍** (early), 늦게 (late, from 늦다), **먼저** (first), **나중에** (later).

### 4. Contrast-은/는, officially

You've used 은/는 as "as for" since Module 1. Its second job is **contrast**: 보통 지하철을 타요. 그런데 오늘**은** 걸어요 — "but *today* (unlike other days) I'm walking." Same particle, now doing what English does with vocal stress.

## Part 4 — Building sentences

**Three tenses, one skeleton.** 먹었어요 → 먹고 있어요 → 먹을 거예요 — the frame you learned in Module 1 now travels through time.

::sentences::

**The universal check-in:** 뭐 해요? ("whatcha doing?") — answerable, as of today, with any -고 있어요 sentence you can build. (뭐 = what; it gets formal papers in Module 9.)

## Part 5 — Practice

Cover the right column and translate; then flip and build the Korean.

1. I get up at 7 every day. → 저는 매일 일곱 시에 일어나요.
2. I'm eating right now. → 지금 밥을 먹고 있어요.
3. I'm waiting for my friend. → 친구를 기다리고 있어요.
4. Tomorrow I'll rest at home. → 내일은 집에서 쉴 거예요.
5. I'll clean my room this weekend. → 주말에 방을 청소할 거예요.
6. I usually take the subway. But today I'm walking. → 보통 지하철을 타요. 그런데 오늘은 걸어요.
7. The movie ends at 8. → 영화가 여덟 시에 끝나요.
8. I'll be busy next week. → 다음 주에 바쁠 거예요.

Timeline check: say one thing you did yesterday, one thing you're doing now, one thing you'll do tomorrow. Three tenses, out loud — that's the whole module in three breaths.
