# Korean, Word-First — Module 40: The Make-Ahead Kitchen

Band 5 opens at the cutting board. Ring 1 taught you to ORDER food — M3's flavors, M18's tools and pastes; this module hands you the knife: the recipe verbs (썰다, 섞다, 젓다, 삶다, 부치다), the seasoning shelf (간장 completes the family M18 promised), Korea's aromatics by the armful, and the low table (상) it all lands on. The glue is the ending that explains the whole cuisine: **-아/어 놓다 / 두다** — do it now, leave the result standing for later. Korea batch-cooks its meals into the future; today you learn the grammar it does it with.

**How to use this:** the verbs are the module. The nouns will stick from their stories; spend your reps on the sibling pairs (담다/담그다, 썰다/자르다, 삶다/끓이다) — Korean splits cooking verbs where English splits utensils.

---

## Part 1 — The shelf decodes itself

Almost nothing on this shelf is a stranger:

- **The 장-family completes:** M18 taught 된장 and 고추장 and its note promised 간장. Here it is — 간 (saltiness: 간을 보다 = taste-check) + 장 (paste). Three jars, one morpheme.
- **The -맛 rack:** 달다 + M24's badge + 맛 = 단맛. Same mold: 짠맛, 매운맛 — flavors become nouns by wearing the badge.
- **The 양- prefix:** 서양's ocean-洋 (M31) stamps imports — 양파 is literally the 'Western scallion', shelved beside 양말 (M21).
- **The 간 radar:** 간장's 간 is salt; 간식's 간 is 시간's between-間 — a between-meal. Same syllable, different pantries.

## Part 2 — The bricks

**Homophone patrol:** 부치다 (pan-fry; mail) sounds exactly like M37's 붙이다 — the note that flagged the mail-verb now pays out in 파전. And 젓다 (stir) sounds like M36's 젖다 (get wet): stir the soup, don't soak in it. Meanwhile M14's ㅅ-irregular family (낫다 → 나아요) finally gains its second member: 젓다 → 저어요.

## Part 3 — The glue: cook now, eat later

### 1. -아/어 놓다 — do-and-set-down

M28's 놓다 (put down) welds onto any verb: do X and LEAVE THE RESULT STANDING. 반찬을 **만들어 놓았어요** = I made the side dishes — and they're sitting there, ready. 썰어 놓다, 차려 놓다, 창문을 열어 놓다 (M6's window, left open on purpose). The ending's whole promise: the work is banked. In speech it clips by the 준말 law: 만들어 **놨**어요.

### 2. -아/어 두다 — do-and-store

두다's sibling ending, with a longer horizon: 놓다 sets it down for tonight; 두다 files it away — **얼려 두세요** (freeze it for whenever — 얼다 + M37's causative 리), 김치를 **담가 두다** (a season's insurance). The two overlap constantly; the feel is 놓다 = counter, 두다 = cupboard.

**Calibration:** tonight's counter → 놓다 · the deep cupboard of the future → 두다. Native speakers swap them freely; the horizon is the feel, not a law.

**This is a food culture written in grammar.** 밑반찬 — M28's 밑 + M3's 반찬: the base side-dishes made ahead so any table fills in minutes — are 만들어 놓은 반찬 by name and nature. And M26's 김장 is -아/어 두다 at national scale: a November of 담그기 so that winter arrives pre-cooked. Korea eats in the present tense off work done in the past.

## Part 4 — Building sentences

**Spot the recycling:** M38's 미리 preps the weekend, M31's 마다 puts kimjang on the calendar, M35's 남다 fills the freezer through M37's causative (얼리다), M34's 것 같다 tastes the soup, and M39's -아/어도 delivers the caretaking credo.

::sentences::

**Gap deck:** the flavor of grandmother's hands (손맛), broth denied as total denial (국물도 없다), and vinegar as sabotage (초를 치다).
