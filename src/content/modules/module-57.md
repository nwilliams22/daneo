# Korean, Word-First — Module 57: The Measuring Words

Band 6 is the long tail — the words that resisted every theme because they run ALL the themes. This module is the abstract-noun toolkit: the words that measure the world (크기, 길이, 높이, 무게, 속도), slice it into fractions (전체, 일부, 나머지), analyze it (차이, 효과, 가능성), and file it (종류, 일반, 전문, 기본, 기초). Almost every entry decomposes into hanja you already own — S3 called the decoder a cheat code, and this shelf is where the compound interest pays out. The glue is the frame Korea schedules itself with: **~(으)ㄹ 예정이다** — is scheduled to.

**How to use this:** read every decode before you drill — nearly nothing here is a stranger, and the notes name the parts. Spend your reps on the precision pairs instead (기본/기초, 주요/중요, 일반/보통, 업무/일): Band 6 words earn their frequency rank by NOT being interchangeable.

## Part 3 — Two machines

### 1. The measure factory — adjectives become rulers

Korean builds its rulers from its adjectives: 크다 + M13's -기 = **크기**; 길다 and 높다 + the -이 that turned 놀다 into 놀이 (M26) = **길이**, **높이**; 무겁다 melts irregularly into **무게** — the weld M35's 몸무게 note already exposed. The lone Sino member is **속도** (고속도로's 速 + 정도's 度). Measures ride the possession chassis you know from 인기가 많아요: 높이가 백 미터예요, 무게가 나가요 — the thing OWNS its number.

### 2. ~(으)ㄹ 예정이다 — the itinerary frame

The prospective -(으)ㄹ you've used since -(으)ㄹ 때 (M25), aimed at 예정: 출발할 **예정입니다** = scheduled to depart. This is how stations announce, companies plan, and wedding invitations commit — the future, but with paperwork: 다음 달에 이사할 예정이에요.

**Calibration:** -(으)ㄹ 거예요 predicts, -(으)려고 해요 (M50) intends, ~(으)ㄹ 예정이에요 is BOOKED — the plan exists outside your head. The more institutional the speaker, the more 예정 you'll hear; it is the airport's favorite noun.
