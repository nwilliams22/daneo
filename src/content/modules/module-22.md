# Korean, Word-First — Module 22: City and Nature

The 도시 you commute through and the 자연 you escape to on weekends — plus the grammar that ranks them: **더** (more) and **제일** (the most), the comparison machine. With it comes the course's best running joke reaching its finale: the particle **보다** (*than*) — the syllable 보다's fifth job, and this one isn't even a verb.

> **Warm-up (Module 21):** build these out loud — Module 21 blocks, combinations you've never seen written: *These pants look comfortable* (바지가 편해 보여요) · *Take off your coat* (코트를 벗으세요) · *The wallet is in my pocket* (지갑이 주머니 안에 있어요). If the blocks don't snap together, visit Review first.

**How to use this:** the comparison machine is Band 2's last BIG grammar — what remains on the map (M23–M26) is garnish: a convenience pattern, the noun-modifiers, dates, and one noticing-ending for the capstone. From here, it's mostly vocabulary and victory laps.

## Part 1 — The last homograph roundup

Band 2's homograph radar retires with honors:

- **새** — M13's *new*, now also *bird*. 새 새 — a new bird — is legal Korean and a tiny poem.
- **보다** — see (M1), watch-an-exam (M15), try (M16), seem (M21)… and now *than*, as a particle. Fully unionized.
- **풀** — grass, and also the glue stick in M15's pencil case.
- **야경** — the night-야 of 야근, not the field-야 of 야구, plus 경치's 경.
- **물고기** — 물 + 고기: *water-meat*. Korean names the fish by its destiny.

And 고양이 — promised by the sound interlude as the batchim-ㅇ example — finally lands in the checklist, ng staying seated as advertised.

## Part 3 — The glue

### 1. 더 / 보다 — the comparison machine

M9's 더 (more) reveals its grammar career. Full form:

- [loser]**보다** [winner]가 **더** [adjective] — 서울이 부산**보다 더** 커요
- Preferences too: 여름 옷**보다** 겨울 옷을 **더** 좋아해요

The 보다 attaches to the thing being outdone. Drop it when context is obvious — 더 커요 alone works fine.

### 2. 제일 — the most

제 + 일 — literally "number one" (M2's Sino 일). Park it before any adjective and the contest is over:

- 제주도가 **제일** 아름다워요
- **세상에서 제일** 맛있어요 — the most in the world: the superlative's biggest stage

### 3. 구경 — looking as leisure

**구경하다** turns viewing into an activity: 시장 구경, 야경 구경 — and the seasonal pilgrimages 꽃구경 and 단풍구경 (gap deck), where the whole country follows bloom fronts like weather. 구경만 할게요 — just browsing — is fitting-room-adjacent survival Korean.

## Part 4 — Building sentences

**Spot the recycling:** 산에 오르면 운동화를 신으세요 chains M20's hinge into M21's shoes and foot-verb, 가을에는 하늘이 높아 보여요 puts M21's looks-machine on M11's sky, and 비가 그친 후에 무지개를 봤어요 resolves M11's 그치다 through M13's 후에 with 보다's original job — three bands of the course in one sky.

::sentences::

**Ranking bingo:** 더 좋아요 · 제일 맛있어요 · 세상에서 제일 — you can now argue about restaurants, neighborhoods, and seasons, which is to say: you can now make small talk in Korean.

## Part 5 — Practice

Cover the right column and translate; then flip and build the Korean.

1. Seoul is bigger than Busan. → 서울이 부산보다 더 커요.
2. The subway is faster than the bus. → 지하철이 버스보다 더 빨라요.
3. Jeju Island is the most beautiful. → 제주도가 제일 아름다워요.
4. Cats are quieter than dogs. → 고양이가 강아지보다 더 조용해요.
5. In spring, the flowers bloom. → 봄에 꽃이 피어요.
6. In autumn, the sky looks high. → 가을에는 하늘이 높아 보여요.
7. My mom's cooking is the best in the world. → 우리 엄마 요리가 세상에서 제일 맛있어요.
8. After the rain stopped, I saw a rainbow. → 비가 그친 후에 무지개를 봤어요.

Ranking drill: three honest superlatives about your own life, predicate-side — [thing]이 제일 [adjective]예요. Your 동네, your 맛집, your season. Argue with a Korean about any of them and you're conversing.
