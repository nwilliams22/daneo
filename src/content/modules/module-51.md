# Korean, Word-First — Module 51: Turn It Up

M48 walked you behind the page and the camera; this module is its loud sibling — the speaker and the stage. You already own 음악 (M2), 노래 (M7), 소리 (M19), and M17's hobby shelf; today Korea makes noise with them: the racket verbs (소리치다, 떠들다), the verbs sound travels on (들려오다, 흘러나오다), the play button (틀다), the music shelf (악기, 가요, 무대), the fame machine (스타, 팬, 프로), and the stadium — Korea's loudest room, where 2002 taught a whole country one chant. The glue is the ending the 준말 reading promised: **-죠** — the we-both-know tag that completes M42's listener-management set.

**How to use this:** the compounds are free — 들려오다, 흘러나오다, and 노랫소리 are all welded from verbs and nouns you own, so read the decodes before you drill. Spend your reps on the two calibration pairs (틀다/켜다, 우승/이기다) and on the five radar words the notes flag hardest: 북, 팬, 프로, 가사, 탤런트 — each is two words wearing one spelling.

## Part 1 — Nothing here is new; it's just louder

This shelf is almost entirely machinery you own, turned up:

- **Sound gets direction:** M36's 들리다 + M1's 오다 = 들려오다 (sound arrives); M35's 흐르다 + M28's 나오다 = 흘러나오다 (music escapes doorways); M37's causative 들리- + M9's -아/어 주다 = 들려주다 (play it FOR me). Three compounds, zero new parts.
- **치다's empire expands:** M17 filed pianos and rackets under the strike-play verb; today it collects 소리치다 (strike sound into the air), 박수를 치다 (applause), 북을 치다 (drums), and 큰소리치다 (talk big). One verb, an entire percussion section.
- **The converter shelf:** 리듬, 바이올린, 콘서트, 스타, 팬, 쇼, 시리즈 — the Konglish reading's rules, running live (v→ㅂ, z→ㅈ, ㅡ-cushions). And its false-friend table gains a member: 탤런트 is a drama actor, not a gift.
- **The -장/-회 economy keeps building:** M17's 경기 + place-場 = 경기장; 대학교's great-大 + the gather-會 of 회사/회의/사회 = 대회. The stadium and the tournament, both prefab.

## Part 3 — The glue: -죠 — what we both know

### 1. The tag — 알죠?

Bolt -죠 onto any verb and a statement turns to the listener for a nod: 이 노래 **알죠**? (you know it, right?), 내일 시험이**죠**? (the exam's tomorrow, right?). It claims the ground is shared and asks only for confirmation — softer than a real question, warmer than a statement.

### 2. The of-course answer — 좋죠!

Same ending, answering: 좋**죠**! / 그렇**죠**! / 맛있**죠**! — 'obviously, yes.' Where 좋아요 agrees, 좋죠 co-signs: what you said was already true for both of us. This is the sound of enthusiastic Korean agreement.

### 3. The polite nudge — 가시죠

With M4's honorific -(으)시-, -죠 turns suggestions velvet: 같이 **가시죠** (shall we?), 시작**하시죠** (let's begin) — the meeting-room's gentlest imperative. Full form -지요 survives in careful speech; 반말 keeps just -지 (알지?).

**The knowledge triangle — M42's set, complete:** -잖아요 = YOU know this, I'm pointing at it (reminding) · -거든요 = I know something you DON'T (informing) · **-죠** = we BOTH know (confirming). Three endings, three states of shared knowledge — aim by asking who holds the information. Point -죠 at genuinely shared ground and you sound native; point it at news and you sound like you're assuming.
