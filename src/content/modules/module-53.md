# Korean, Word-First — Module 53: The Waiting Room

Ring 1's M14 taught first-aid Korean — 아프다, 감기, 열, and the -지 마세요 to stop touching it; M41 leaned in close enough to name eyebrows. This module is the patient's register: naming symptoms precisely (증상, 두통, 몸살 — the illness English can't name), the treatment arc from 입원 to 퇴원, the joints Korean builds by finding necks, the 禁-family on the walls, and the trajectory verbs that plot the chart. In Band 1 you could say it hurts; today you can give a history. The glue is the course's biggest holdout: **-는데/-(으)ㄴ데** — THE spoken connective, the one Korean conversation actually runs on — taught properly at last, in its natural home: the doctor's office.

**How to use this:** the registers ARE the module — 이 vs 이빨, 몸 vs 신체, 죽다 vs 돌아가시다: health Korean is register Korean, and picking the wrong rung is the real malpractice. Drill the trajectory trio (좋아지다/나빠지다/심해지다) as one machine, and give the glue Part double reps: -는데 will appear in more of your future sentences than any word on this shelf.

## Part 3 — The glue: -는데 / -(으)ㄴ데 — the situation-setter

Fifty-two modules of Korean and its most-used connective still hasn't appeared — until now. **-는데** hangs on a clause and hands the listener a SITUATION; whatever follows lands inside it. Three jobs, one machine:

### 1. Background-setting — here's the scene, here's my move

머리가 아픈데 약 좀 주세요 — my head hurts, (so, given that) please give me some medicine. 감기에 걸렸는데 병원에 가야 돼요? — I've caught a cold; do I need the hospital? The -는데 clause isn't the point — it's the STAGE for the point. Korean sets the scene before acting on it, and this is the ending it does it with.

### 2. Soft contrast — it happened, BUT

약을 먹었는데 안 나았어요 — took the medicine; didn't get better. M7's -지만 argues the mismatch; -는데 just notes it and lets you draw the conclusion. Most spoken "but"s in Korean are this one.

### 3. Trailing off — the -는데요… landing

좀 아픈데요… — the sentence that politely never ends. Stop on -는데요 and the situation is delivered, the request unspoken, the listener invited to finish: the pharmacist is already reaching for the shelf. Refusals soften the same way — 지금은 좀 바쁜데요… — a no said without saying no.

**The forms:** verbs and 있다/없다 take **-는데** (가는데, 먹는데, 있는데); adjectives take **-(으)ㄴ데** (아픈데, 큰데); nouns take **-인데** (몸살인데); anything past takes **-았/었는데**. ㄹ drops as usual (살다 → 사는데). If you can badge it (M24's tables), you can -ㄴ데 it.

**The origin story:** M24 closed the cold case — 그런데 = 그런 + 데, "that kind of situation, and…" — and today the machinery inside comes out: every -(으)ㄴ데 clause does what 그런데 does, mid-sentence, with YOUR situation standing in for 그렇다. And this module's own 근데 (rank 563!) is 그런데 through the 준말 press. You've been saying the glue since M7; now you can aim it.
