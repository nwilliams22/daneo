# Korean, Word-First — Module 85: The Written Voice

Ring 3 opens here, and the first thing to know is that you already own the grammar. The NIKL Grade-C list is the essay, the newspaper column, the lecture and the novel — and what changes at that altitude is not the engine but the REGISTER. Every ending in this module is one you have used since Ring 1, wearing a formal coat: **-(으)며** is verb--고 from M10 and -(으)면서 from M38 in print, **-(으)므로** is the fourth row of the because-family M84 just closed, **-(으)나** is -지만 from M7 with the seams welded shut, and **-고서/-고도** are two riders on verb--고 that make the sequence, or the concession, explicit. The thirty-five words are the adverbs that steer an argument (따라서, 다만, 아울러, 결코, 과연, 비로소), the degree words of prose (다소, 상당히, 대체로, 대개, 사실상, 그야말로), and the pronouns fiction runs on — 그녀, 그이, 자네 — plus the one it insults with. Thesis for the whole ring: **written Korean is spoken Korean with the seams welded shut**, and every word here has a spoken twin you already know.

**How to use this:** read each adverb's note for its TWIN — the mouth's word it replaces — and drill the pair, not the word: 그래서/따라서, 역시/과연, 꽤/상당히, 갑자기/문득, 가끔/때때로/때로. Then spend your reps on the four glue parts, each a one-line swap against an ending you own. Calibration lines say when the mouth may use the form (rarely) and when the page must (always).

## Part 1 — The adverbs of the page

S2 opened the written register (the M56 md: "S2 taught you to READ the written register") and M29 gave you its first citizen — 그러나, "The WRITTEN but — books, news, exams. Say it aloud and you sound like an editorial". This shelf is the rest of that citizenry, in three rows:

- **The steering adverbs** — the words a column turns its corners on: 따라서 concludes (M7's 그래서 in print), 그리하여 narrates (the folktale's 그래서), 다만 opens the caveat (M30's 만 wearing 다), 아울러 adds and binds (the ministry's 그리고), 일단 suspends (for now), 결코 and 아예 cancel (with a negative — M39's 절대 and 전혀 in a suit), 과연 nods or doubts (M32's 역시, sat down), 비로소 marks the threshold (M35's 마침내 with a reason attached), 앞서 points back up the page, 때로 sings, and 어찌/도대체 ask the question nobody can answer.
- **The degree adverbs** — how much, in print: 더욱, 다소, 상당히, 대체로, 대개, 사실상, 실제로, 실로, 참으로, 그야말로, 워낙, 널리, 달리, 서서히, 끊임없이, 그저, 그나마, 문득. Most decode on sight — 多少, 相當, 大體, 大槪, 事實上, 徐徐 — and three are pure assembly: 끊임없이 — M20's 끊다 + the -(으)ㅁ freezer from M67 + M32's 없이, 참으로 and 실로 (the -로 tail M62's 정말로 note counted), 달리 and 널리 (the -리 press that made M62's 멀리).
- **The pronouns of fiction** — 그녀 (she — a twentieth-century coinage, gap deck), 그이 (he, affectionately — a wife's word), 자네 (you, said down and warmly — the father-in-law's word), and 놈 (guy; bastard — the drama's villain, and a dependent noun that needs a pointer in front).

::vocab::

**Radar patrol:** 따라서 the conjunction (comma after it, at the head of a sentence) versus 따라서 the verb form (상황에 따라서 — M32's 따르다, mid-sentence) · 결코 is 決 + 코, not M14's 코 the nose · 대개 is 大槪, not M5's 개 the counter · 달리 is M24's 다르다 pressed flat, not M17's 달리다 the runner · the 도 of 도대체 is not M2's 도, the 대 not M55's 대 · 그저 has no kin in the 그저께 inside M52's 엊그제 · 실로 (truly) is not the 사무실로 of M61's 찾아가다 note · and -(으)나 the ending (Part 4) is not the (이)나 particle from M43 — 먹으나 is a verb, 밥이나 is a noun with a shrug.

## Part 2 — The glue: -(으)며 — the written and/while

Vowel and ㄹ stems take **며**, consonant stems **으며**: 가며, 살며, 먹으며, 웃으며. It does BOTH jobs its spoken twins split between them. As **while** it is -(으)면서 from M38 — that md's rule, "One subject does both things", holds unchanged: 그녀는 **웃으며** 말했다 (she said, smiling — the novel's line; 웃으면서 aloud). As **and** it is verb--고 from M10 listing two states: 가격이 **싸며** 방도 넓다 (the price is low, and the rooms are spacious — the advertisement's line; 싸고 aloud). Context sorts them: one body doing two things = while; two facts side by side = and. Around it the page runs the plain style — the -다/-ㄴ다 closer the M32 md called "the plain form" ("M19's 반말 dictionary style"), which the quote machine taught you to read; you may keep 요/합니다 closers in your own writing and still use -(으)며 inside the sentence. The past does not enter it: 먹었으며 exists but is rare — the tense rides on the final verb, exactly as with -(으)면서.

**Calibration:** 웃으면서 말했어요 is the mouth · 웃으며 말했다 is the page · if you HEAR -(으)며, someone is reading aloud — a newsreader, a wedding officiant, a student with a textbook. Mouths never generate it.

## Part 3 — The glue: -(으)므로 — the written because

Vowel and ㄹ stems take **므로**, consonant stems **으므로**, and the past welds in: 비싸므로, 살므로, 늦었으므로, 학생이므로 (the copula takes 이므로). M84 closed the because-family at three rows — -아/어서 (spoken, neutral), -(으)니까 from M10 (spoken, before commands — the M10 md: "Rule of thumb: if the sentence ends in 주세요/-(으)세요/-(으)ㄹ까요, the reason takes **니까**"), and -기 때문에 from M84 ("written, formal, objective — the newspaper's, the essay's, the announcement's"). **-(으)므로** is the fourth row and the most formal of all: the notice board's, the ruling's, the textbook proof's — 공사 중**이므로** 우회하시기 바랍니다 (S2's 공사 and 중; 우회 — a detour, riding frozen) — and it CAN precede a request, which -아/어서 and -기 때문에 never can. That is why every sign uses it: the reason plus the polite order, in one sentence, from no one in particular. Its sentence-initial twin is S2's 그러므로 ("in a judge's robe") — but the word prose actually reaches for at the head of a sentence is this module's 따라서. The closer it loves: **-(으)시기 바랍니다** (M34's 바라다, formal — the notice board's please), as s_m85_elevator_stairs shows.

**Calibration:** 늦어서 죄송해요 is the mouth · 늦었으니까 먼저 드세요 is the mouth giving an instruction · 늦었기 때문에 is the report · 늦었으므로 is the report giving an instruction. One reason, four coats; the listener and the order pick the coat.

## Part 4 — The glue: -(으)나 — the written but

Vowel and ㄹ stems take **나**, consonant stems **으나**, the past welds in: 노력했**으나** 실패했다 (tried, but failed), 가격은 싸**나** 방은 어둡다, 학생이나 (a student, but…). It is -지만 from M7 in print — the M7 md rule "Only the final verb carries the 요; the welded one goes bare" holds here too, and every -지만 sentence you own converts by swapping the ending: 바쁘지만 행복해요 → 바쁘나 행복하다. Its sentence-initial twin is M29's 그러나 (but; however — written), the way 따라서 is the twin of -(으)므로. RADAR, the important one: this is a VERB ending — not the (이)나 particle from M43 (or; a whole), which sits on nouns, and not M1's 나 (I). 먹으나 = eats, but; 밥이나 = rice or something. The 으 is the tell: a particle never takes one.

**Calibration:** 비싸지만 맛있어요 is the mouth · 비싸나 맛있다 is the page · 그러나 opens the next sentence where 하지만 (M7) would in speech. If you would not write 그러나, do not write -(으)나 either — they travel together.

## Part 5 — The glue: -고서 and -고도 — after doing, even having done

Two riders on verb--고 from M10, both straight onto the stem, no tense inside. **-고서** = and only then, after: M10's 약: 밥을 **먹고서** 약을 드세요 (eat first, THEN the medicine) — bare 먹고 would list the two; 먹고서 orders them, which is why the pharmacist says it. It is a tighter -(으)ㄴ 후에 (the 후에 from M13) and the mouth uses it too, mostly with instructions. **-고도** = even having done: 돈을 **받고도** 일을 안 했다 (took the money and STILL did no work) — -아/어도 from M39 ("concede and proceed") for a completed act: 받아도 concedes a hypothetical, 받고도 concedes a fact. Both spoken and written; both drop the 요 on the welded verb like every -고 you own.

**Calibration:** 먹고 = and · 먹고서 = and only after that · 먹고도 = and even so · 먹으며 = and (in print). Four -고-shaped joints; the second syllable is the meaning.

## Part 6 — Building sentences

**Spot the recycling:** -(으)면서 from M38 is heard under 웃으며, verb--고 from M10 under 넓으며 and 받고도, -지 마세요 from M14 closes the snow notice behind -(으)므로, -기 때문에 from M84 and -(으)니까 from M10 are set beside -(으)므로 in the elevator note so the four becauses can be compared, 께서 from M47 and -(으)ㄹ 때 from M25 carry the grandfather, and -지 않다 from M28 does the long negation the guy with the money deserves.

**Gap deck:** the 'she' that print invented (그녀), the ladder of you's from 너 to a name (너 · 자네 · 당신 · 그쪽 · ○○ 씨), and the three ways to say 'person' after a number — one counts, one respects, one judges (놈 · 명 · 분).
