# Korean, Word-First — Appendix S3: Hanja, the Cheat Code

Here is the course's last secret: **you already know about sixty hanja roots.** Nobody told you, because nobody needed to — every time a note said "the 학 of 학교" or "식당's 식," you were learning a Chinese character's meaning without ever seeing the character. This appendix lines them all up, because the trick scales: Ring 2's vocabulary is even MORE Sino-Korean than Ring 1's, and a learner who reads roots meets every new word half-decoded.

**The rules of the cheat code:** recognition only. You will never write 學 — Sejong's alphabet (Reading 1) made that optional, and 한글날 celebrates the fact. A root here is just a SYLLABLE with a stable meaning that recurs across words. Spot the syllable, guess the word, be right embarrassingly often.

## The radar was hanja all along

Thirty modules of "homograph radar" — 눈/눈, 배/배, the five 이s — and here's what it was actually detecting: **different hanja colliding on the same Korean syllable.** The final exhibits:

- **화**: 話 speech (전화, 대화) · 火 fire (화요일) · anger (화나다) — three roots, one syllable
- **문**: 文 writing (문화, 문제, 신문) · 門 door (문, 남대문, 창문)
- **미**: 美 beautiful (미국, 미술관) · 未 not-yet (미래, 미안하다 — "un-settled feeling")
- **남**: 南 south (남쪽, 남대문) · 男 male (남자, 남편, 남녀)
- **교**: 校 school (학교) · 敎 teaching (교회, 교수, 교과서)
- **일**: 一 one · 日 day/sun (일요일 holds BOTH: day-of-sun) · plus native work-일
- **말**: 末 end (주말) · native speech-말 — one Sino, one homegrown

When two words share a syllable and the meanings don't rhyme, suspect two roots wearing one Hangul coat. That suspicion — which you've practiced since 눈 met 눈 — is exactly how educated Koreans parse new vocabulary.

## Using the cheat code

Meet an unknown word; scan for roots; guess before you look up:

- 수학 = number(수)-learning(학) → mathematics ✓
- 대학생 = big-school-person → university student ✓
- 외국어 = outside-country-language → foreign language ✓ (you did this one in M15)
- 음식점 = food-shop → restaurant ✓

You'll be wrong sometimes (roots collide, native words interlope) — that's what the Explore tab is for. But Ring 2's ~2,100 words are mostly two-root compounds of syllables on this page. The cheat code isn't a shortcut around the vocabulary. It IS the vocabulary, seen from above.
