# Korean, Word-First — Module 151: Everyday Care and Shared Spaces

A bulb needs changing, a kettle disappears in the dark, and a friend needs someone to listen. This pack puts practical care beside the words for school, neighborhoods and shared culture.

**How to use this:** Learn the words and their notes first. Then read each sentence from natural English through the Korean-order gloss to Korean. Trace matching chunks instead of translating one word at a time.

---

## Part 1 — The object tells you the job

전구 is the replaceable bulb, 주전자 is the vessel, and 내용물 is what sits inside a container. Korean keeps those jobs separate. 페인트 needs an action such as 칠하다; the English habit of using paint as both noun and verb does not transfer automatically.

The cost shelf grows too: 교육비 covers education expenses, while 학비 names tuition or school fees. 전문점 ends in shop-店, and 토론자 ends in person-者. Read the last piece before guessing what sort of thing the whole word names.

## Part 2 — Attention has its own vocabulary

관람 is audience attention; 기억되다 is what survives in memory. 들어주다 adds something warmer: hearing a person out, or granting a request. 가만 supplies the quiet. These are not interchangeable ways of saying see or hear.

Keep the radar on for 재수: the reserved word is luck. 교외 takes you outside the central city. Neither spelling tells its whole story without context.

## Part 3 — Building sentences

No new glue. Familiar frames carry the new vocabulary; notice which noun is the subject and which action closes each sentence.

**Spot the recycling:** 사립 and 속마음 return from M150 in a cost comparison and a supportive conversation; 음반 from M51 supplies the recording choice. Each scene is new.

- **-더라도 from M95 concedes a circumstance.**
- **-는 김에 from M91 adds an opportunity to an activity.**
- **-았/었더니 from M88 connects a personal action to the response observed.**

Eight sentences put the vocabulary into familiar grammar frames.
