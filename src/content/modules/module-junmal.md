# Korean, Word-First — 준말: Korean Shrinks

You've been collecting these one at a time for thirty modules: 조금 became 좀, 것 became 거, 그러면 became 그럼. Each arrived as a footnote. This reading assembles the footnotes into what they actually are: **a system**. Korean shrinks what it says often — on purpose, by rule, and so consistently that the shrunken forms have their own dictionary entries and their own name: **준말**, "shortened words" (준 from 줄다, *to shrink* — the word for shrinking is itself ready to shrink).

---

## The shelf you already own

Every one of these arrived somewhere in the course as a one-off. Look at them together:

| Full | Shrunk | Where you met it |
|---|---|---|
| 조금 | 좀 | M9 — the softener |
| 것 | 거 | M17 — 이 것 → 이거 |
| 무엇 | 뭐 | M10 taught you the CLIP first; M29 delivered the full suit |
| 그러면 | 그럼 | M29 — the social two syllables |
| 이야기 | 얘기 | M24 — stories at speed |
| 요즈음 | 요즘 | M26 — these days, shortened these days |
| 오래간만 | 오랜만 | M19 — long time no see, less long |
| 아주머니 | 아줌마 | M27 — the honorific clipped into the street title |
| 마음 | 맘 | 맘에 들어요 — casual liking |
| 가지다 | 갖다 | M28 — having, gripped tighter |

One rule explains all of them: **frequency is pressure**. Say a word often enough and Korean squeezes the air out of it. English does this lawlessly (going to → gonna); Korean does it so cleanly the results get standardized.

## The fusion table — pronouns + particles

The biggest 준말 payoff is one you've half-met: pronouns FUSE with their particles. This is why spoken Korean sounds faster than the Korean you type:

| Full | Fused | |
|---|---|---|
| 나는 / 저는 | **난 / 전** | as-for-me |
| 나를 | **날** | me-[object] |
| 나의 / 저의 | **내 / 제** | my — M17's 제 was a 준말 all along! |
| 이것이 | **이게** | this-[subject] |
| 그것은 | **그건** | that-[topic] |
| 그것을 | **그걸** | that-[object] |
| 무엇을 | **뭘** | what-[object] |

Read the left column in books; hear the right column everywhere. The machinery is M1's particles doing M19's casual register at M12's 빨리빨리 speed.

The same pressure produces 뭐예요? (from 무엇이에요), 여기서 (여기에서 — M23's asking-the-way kit used it), and — previewed for Ring 2 — the ending -지요 collapsing into **-죠**.

## Reading the shrink direction

A useful habit: when you meet an unfamiliar short word, try inflating it. 담에 → 다음에 (next time). 어케 → 어떻게 (texting). 맨날 → 만날 → every day. Most "slang" your textbook doesn't know is just a full form you DO know, with the air let out. Deflation runs on schedule; your dictionary is bigger than it looks.

The end of the line is texting, where whole syllables collapse to bare jamo: ㅋㅋ (laughter), ㅇㅇ (yeah — 응 twice), ㄱㅅ (감사), ㄴㄴ (no no). That territory belongs to the slang appendix — but notice it's the SAME rule at maximum pressure: what gets said most shrinks most, until only consonants remain.

## App tie-ins

- **Typing drill:** the fused forms are composition practice — 이게 is four jamo doing the work of six. The composer handles both; your fingers learn why Korea texts fast.
- **Explore:** paste any suspicious short form and ask — the translator inflates 준말 on sight.
- **Review:** both forms count. Recognizing 뭘 IS knowing 무엇을; the schedule doesn't double-charge you.
