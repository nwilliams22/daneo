# Korean, Word-First — Module 7: Describing Things and Feelings

You can locate anything in the city; now you get to have opinions about it. This module delivers the adjective flood — sizes, prices, difficulty, moods, colors — and the connectives (그리고, 그런데, 하지만, 그래서) that chain sentences into actual talk. By the end you'll produce your first multi-sentence Korean: *I'm tired. So I'm going home.*

**How to use this:** Adjectives are where the conjugation families you've been collecting finally line up. Part 1 assembles the full map — after that, no new adjective will ever surprise you.

---

## Part 1 — The irregular families, assembled

Korean adjectives conjugate like verbs (you've known this since 맛있어요). Five patterns cover everything you've met, and this module completes the set:

| Family | Rule | Members so far |
|--------|------|----------------|
| Regular | 아요/어요 by vowel | 좋다→좋아요, 많다→많아요, 길다→길어요 |
| ㅂ-irregular | ㅂ → 워 | 맵다→매워요, 가깝다→가까워요, **쉽다→쉬워요, 어렵다→어려워요** |
| ㅡ-drop | ㅡ falls before 아/어 | 바쁘다→바빠요, 배고프다→배고파요, 예쁘다→예뻐요, **슬프다→슬퍼요** |
| 르-irregular | 르 → ㄹ라/ㄹ러 | 배부르다→배불러요, 모르다→몰라요 |
| ㄷ-irregular *(verbs)* | ㄷ → ㄹ | 듣다→들어요, 걷다→걸어요 |

The 하다-adjectives (따뜻하다, 행복하다, 피곤하다…) are the easiest of all: 하다 → 해요, every time.

And the **있다/없다 factory** keeps producing: 재미**있다** (fun exists = interesting), 재미**없다** (boring), joining 맛있다/맛없다 — the gap deck has the full assembly line.

## Part 3 — The glue

### 1. Connectives — sentence-starters

Where 하고 joins *nouns*, these join *sentences*. Full stop, connective, continue:

| Word | Job | Feel |
|------|-----|------|
| 그리고 | and | plain addition |
| 그런데 | but / by the way | soft pivot (근데 in speech) |
| 하지만 | but / however | firm contrast |
| 그래서 | so / therefore | cause → result |

- 피곤해요. **그래서** 집에 가요. — I'm tired. So I'm going home.
- 한국어는 재미있어요. **그런데** 어려워요. — Korean is fun. But it's hard.

### 2. -지만 — "but" welded on

When both halves fit one sentence, weld them: drop 다, add **지만**:

- 비싸**지만** 맛있어요 — expensive but delicious
- 바쁘**지만** 행복해요 — busy but happy

Only the final verb carries the 요; the welded one goes bare. (And note 바쁘**지만** keeps its ㅡ — the drop only happens before 아/어.) Past tense welds too: 갔**지만** (went, but…).

### 3. Colors

Every color is a noun ending in 색, so they run on Module 4's copula:

- 꽃이 **빨간색이에요** — the flower is red
- 가방은 **검은색이에요** — the bag is black

(Why is the green traffic light called the *blue* light? 파란색's gap-deck entry explains.)

## Part 4 — Building sentences

**Two-sentence units are the new skill.** Watch the timeline stay consistent across 그래서 (both halves past, or both present), and watch -지만 pack a contrast into one breath.

::sentences::

**Reacting to anything is now free:** 맛있어요! 비싸요! 재미없어요… 괜찮아요. You have opinions; deploy them.

## Part 5 — Practice

Cover the right column and translate; then flip and build the Korean.

1. The weather is warm. → 날씨가 따뜻해요.
2. The coffee is expensive but delicious. → 커피는 비싸지만 맛있어요.
3. I'm tired. So I'm going home. → 피곤해요. 그래서 집에 가요.
4. Korean is fun. But it's hard. → 한국어는 재미있어요. 그런데 어려워요.
5. I'm busy but happy. → 바쁘지만 행복해요.
6. The flower is red. → 꽃이 빨간색이에요.
7. I feel good today. → 오늘 기분이 좋아요.
8. I love you. → 사랑해요.

Mood check, out loud: describe today in two connected sentences — [something about your day]. 그래서/그런데 [how you feel]. That's conversation.
