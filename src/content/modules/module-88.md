# Korean, Word-First — Module 88: The Retrospective

English has a past tense; Korean has a past tense AND a witness stand. The syllable **-더-** slips into an ending and changes what the sentence claims: not *this happened* but *I was there, and this is what I saw*. 맛있었어요 is a fact about the restaurant; 맛있더라고요 is a report from someone who ate there. 먹은 밥 is rice that got eaten; 먹던 밥 is rice I was in the middle of. Four endings carry the syllable — the badge **-던**, the connective **-더니** (with its first-person twin **-았/었더니**), the closer **-더라고요**, and the situation-setter **-던데(요)** — and every one of them reports something the speaker witnessed or lived, not something they merely know. This is the one piece of tense Korean has that English lacks. Around it sit the words of a life witnessed: sixteen nouns from 삶 to 운명 and seventeen verbs of going through, from 겪다 to 무너지다. Thirty-three Grade-C words, four glue parts, and the proverb about sleeves.

**How to use this:** learn the rule before the forms — -더- is the eyewitness marker, and it cannot report your own present feeling, only what you watched (in the world, in others, or in your own past self). Then take the four endings as one family with four sockets: badge, connective, closer, scene-setter. Part 1's nouns are mostly hanja arithmetic on characters you own (過程, 段階, 時期, 轉換, 運命); the verbs are welded from verbs you own (살아오다, 지켜보다, 흔들리다) or arrive with an irregular you already run (깨닫다, 잇다).

## Part 1 — The life nouns and the verbs of going through

Three drawers, sorted by what they are made of:

- **Frozen natives:** 삶, 흐름 and 움직임 are M4's 살다, M35's 흐르다 and M50's 움직이다 through the -(으)ㅁ noun freezer from M67 — the living, the flowing, the moving, as things. 짝 is the other half of any pair; 한마디 is M5's 하나 in its counter shape on a frozen 마디 (a joint, a word).
- **Sino time-nouns, all decodable:** 과정 (過程 — the 過 of M35's 과거, the 程 of M32's 정도), 단계 (段階 — M13's 계단 with its characters reversed), 시기 (時期 and 時機, both NIKL rows), 시점 (時 + M32's 점), 초기 (the 初 of M46's 최초), 직후 (the 直 of M56's 직접 + the 後 of M13's 후에), 이래 (以來 — M32's 이상's 以 + M2's 내일's 來), 전환 (the turn-轉 of M50's 자동차 note + the 換 of M64's 교환), 회복 (M53's 회복하다, dismounted), 운명 (M36's 운 + the 命 of M64's 명령), 인연 (the 因 of M86's 인하다 + a first 緣).
- **The verbs:** welded pairs — 살아오다/살아가다 (M4's 살다 + M1's 오다/가다: life up to now, life from here), 지켜보다 (M26's 지키다 + M1's 보다), 나아가다 (M28's 나다 + 가다), 애쓰다 (frozen 애 + M10's 쓰다); the passage verbs 겪다 (suffer through), 거치다 (pass through), 깨닫다 (wake up to — ㄷ-irregular like M2's 듣다); the holding verbs 지니다, 갖추다 (kin of M56's 갖다), 유지하다 (維持, two firsts), 머물다 (M52's 머무르다 clipped, as that note promised); the joining pair 잇다 (ㅅ-irregular, fourth after M14's 낫다, M40's 젓다, M54's 짓다) and 이어지다; the grand 이룩하다 (M35's 이루다 for nations); and the two that fall — 흔들리다 (M41's 흔들다 through the passive party of M36) and 무너지다.

**Radar patrol:** 삶 (life) ≠ M40's 삶다 (to boil) — same stem-spelling, different word, both say 삼 · 과정 is 過程 (a process) AND 課程 (a curriculum — M44's 과제's 課) · 시기 is 時期 (a period), 時機 (the moment) and, frozen, 猜忌 (jealousy) · 초기's 初 is not M25's 초 (the candle) · 이래 (since) ≠ the 이래 of S4's 왜 이래 (이렇게 해) ≠ the quoting -(이)래 in M82's 코끼리 song · 단계 = 계단 flipped: stairs you climb, stages you reach · 깨닫다 flips its ㄷ (깨달아요) where M13's 닫다 keeps it · 잇다 sounds exactly like M1's 있다 until a vowel arrives (이어요 / 있어요) · 나아가다's 나아 is M28's 나다, not the 나아요 of M14's 낫다 · 애쓰다's 애 is effort, not M47's 애 (a kid) · and 거치다 has no 치다 in it.

## Part 2 — The glue: -던, the fourth badge

M24 built the badge family in three headings — "### 1. Adjectives: -(으)ㄴ", "### 2. Verbs, present: -는", "### 3. Verbs, past: -(으)ㄴ" — and M25 added the future -(으)ㄹ. Today the family gets its retrospective member. Stem + **-던** (no ㄹ-drop, no ㄷ-flip — 던 starts with a consonant: 살던, 듣던), or past stem + **-았/었던** for a thing wholly finished and looked back on:

| Badge | Owner | 먹다 | 살다 | Says |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| -는 (verb, present) | M24 | 먹는 밥 | 사는 집 | the rice I'm eating · the house I live in — now |
| -(으)ㄴ (verb, past) | M24 | 먹은 밥 | 산 집 | the rice I ate · the house I lived in — done, a fact |
| -던 (retrospective) | this module | 먹던 밥 | 살던 집 | the rice I was eating (interrupted) · the house I used to live in (habitual) |
| -았/었던 (finished retrospective) | this module | 먹었던 밥 | 살았던 집 | the rice I once ate · the house I once lived in — over, and remembered |

Two readings, one badge: **interrupted** (먹던 밥 — I was eating it and stopped; 하던 일 = the work I was in the middle of) and **habitual** (어릴 때 듣던 노래 — the song I used to hear, with -(으)ㄹ 때 from M25 in front; 내가 살던 집 = the house I used to live in). Adjectives take it too — 어렵던 시기 = the hard time, as I lived it — and nouns take -이던 (학생이던 시절 — my student days, M46's 시절). The M49 gap deck's 호랑이 담배 피우던 시절 said "the -던 is a looking-back badge from a later module — today it stays frozen in the formula": this is the module, and the formula unfreezes. RADAR against -(으)ㄴ 지 from M46 (that md: the past badge stamps the verb finished and 지 "opens the stopwatch"): 살던 집 looks back at the living; 산 지 십 년 counts the time since. One looks, one counts. **Calibration:** 먹는 밥 (now) · 먹은 밥 (done) · 먹던 밥 (I was in the middle of it) · 먹었던 밥 (once, and it is over) · 먹을 밥 (still to come — M25). Five badges, one bowl.

## Part 3 — The glue: -더니 and -았/었더니 — I saw X, then Y

**-더니** joins two clauses with a witnessed turn between them. The subject is usually NOT the speaker — you watched it happen to someone or something else: 아침에는 맑더니 오후에 비가 왔어요 (M11's 맑다, M5's 오후, M11's 비) = it was clear in the morning, then it rained; 열심히 하더니 합격했어요 (M10's 열심히, M44's 합격) = he worked hard and, sure enough, passed; 어릴 때는 작더니 이제 저보다 커요 (M1's 작다, M26's 이제) = she was small as a child, and now she's taller than me — the before-and-after -더니, the aunt's sentence at every holiday.

**-았/었더니** is the first-person twin: past stem + 더니 = I DID X, and found Y. 약을 먹었더니 나았어요 (M10's 약, M14's 낫다) = I took the medicine and got better; 문을 열었더니 아무도 없었어요 (M13's 열다, M29's 아무 with 도 — that note's "with negatives it empties rooms (아무도 없어요 — nobody)") = I opened the door and nobody was there; 물어봤더니 모른대요 (M4's 모르다, -대요, the quoting -다고 하다 from M32 squeezed) = I asked, and they say they don't know. The speaker acts; the world answers; -았/었더니 reports the answer.

**Calibration** — the sort against the connectives you own: 비가 오니까 (-(으)니까 from M10 — a REASON) · 비가 오자마자 (-자마자 from M38 — that md's "as soon as (zero gap)", a SEQUENCE with no gap) · 비가 오는데 (-는데 from M53 — a SITUATION) · 비가 오더니 (a sequence I WATCHED, with a turn in it: 비가 오더니 갑자기 그쳤어요 — M28's 갑자기; 그치다, to stop raining, riding frozen). -더니 is neither cause nor clock: it is the eyewitness's and-then.

## Part 4 — The glue: -더라고요 — I found that, I saw that

The spoken retrospective closer: stem + **-더라고요** (past: -았/었더라고요; casual: **-더라**). 그 식당 정말 맛있더라고요 (M2's 식당, M11's 정말, M1's 맛있다) = that restaurant turned out to be really good — I ate there; 생각보다 어렵더라고요 (the bare noun of M10's 생각하다 with the than-보다 from M22, M7's 어렵다) = it was harder than I expected, I found; 벌써 끝났더라고요 (M23's 벌써, M8's 끝나다) = it had already ended, I discovered. It sits on adjectives, verbs and the copula alike (인연이더라고요), and always after the fact: you watched, and now you tell.

**The eyewitness rule:** -더라고요 cannot report your own present feeling — no *내가 슬프더라고요 (M7's 슬프다), because you do not WITNESS your own sadness, you have it. 슬프더라고요 about someone you watched is fine; 슬펐더라고요 about your past self, looked at from now, is fine too. The line is the same one -나 보다 from M34 draws on the other side: -나 보다 infers from a clue, -더라고요 reports from the scene. **Calibration:** 맛있었어요 (a fact) · 맛있잖아요 (-잖아요 from M42, that md's "you know this already") · 맛있네요 (-네요 from M26 — noticing it right now) · 맛있더라고요 (I was there, you were not, here is what I saw) · 맛있대요 (-대요 from M32 — someone else says). Five closers, five distances from the plate.

## Part 5 — The glue: -던데(요) — the retrospective scene-setter

Take -는데 from M53 (that md's "the situation-setter") and put -더- inside: [observation]**-던데** = here is what I saw — and now my move, or my soft contrast. 사람이 많던데 다른 데로 갈까요? (M1's 사람, M7's 많다, M24's 다르다, M55's 데) = there were a lot of people (I saw); shall we go somewhere else? 그 영화 재미있던데 같이 볼래요? (M2's 영화, M7's 재미있다, M9's 같이) = that film looked good — want to see it together? 조건을 다 갖췄던데 왜 안 뽑았어요? = he had all the qualifications, as I saw it — so why wasn't he picked? Past inside: -았/었던데. And the trailing form does what the heading "Trailing off — the -는데요… landing" from M53 did, with a witness attached: 사람이 많던데요… = there were a lot of people, is all I'm saying. One shape for every stem — verbs, adjectives, 있다/없다, nouns with 이 — because -더- flattens the badge distinctions: 가던데, 좋던데, 학생이던데. **Calibration:** 재미있는데 (it's fun — a situation, now) · 재미있던데 (it was fun when I saw it — and here comes my point) · 재미있더라고요 (it was fun, I found — full stop). -던데 opens a door; -더라고요 closes one.

## Part 6 — Building sentences

**Spot the recycling:** -(으)ㄹ 때 from M25 and -(으)면 from M20 frame the childhood song, and 생각이 나다 (M28's 나다) closes it; -(으)면서 from M38 carries 거치다 through the hard years; the past badge from M24 wears 이래 (졸업한 이래), 직후 (무너진 직후) and the fully finished -았/었던 (갔던 식당); -아/어 보다 from M16 and -는데 from M53 set the alumni scene; -아/어지다 from M35 rides inside M61's 없어지다; M28's 동안 and M32's 결국 pace the sibling's striving under -더니; M14's 푹 and the 르-family from M7 finish the recovery under -았/었더니; -(으)니까 from M10 earns the right to say -더라고요; M9's 다 and M61's 뽑다 bracket -던데; and the can-machine -(으)ㄹ 수 없다 from M10 with M2's 도 carry 한마디 through the day the company fell.
