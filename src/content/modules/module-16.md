# Korean, Word-First — Module 16: Work and the Office

You've had 회사 since Module 2 and 일하다 nearly as long — now you get the life around them: the commute verbs, the meeting room, the salary, and the company dinner. The garnish is **-아/어 보다** — *try doing* — which is 보다's third career, and the kindest way Korean has of asking anything.

> **Warm-up (Module 15):** build these out loud — Module 15 blocks, combinations you've never seen written: *I started studying* (공부하기 시작했어요) · *The last question is hard* (마지막 문제는 어려워요) · *Don't forget the answer* (답을 잊지 마세요). If the blocks don't snap together, visit Review first.

**How to use this:** the office runs on a small set of syllables — 근 (duty), 실 (room), 님 (respect), 사 (job) — and this module mostly teaches you to read their combinations. Also: the pointing words 이거/그거/저거 finally arrive; you've needed them since Module 9.

---

## Part 1 — The 근-family tells the whole day

Three verbs, one syllable of duty (근):

- **출근하다** — out to duty: the morning verb
- **퇴근하다** — retreat from duty: the day's sweetest verb
- **야근하다** — night duty: the verb the gap deck's 월화수목금금금 is about

Around them, the familiar machinery keeps compounding: **사무실** and **회의실** join the room-실 family (교실, 침실…); **변호사** joins the job-사 crew (요리사, 간호사); **부장님** climbs the 님-ladder below M9's 사장님; **월급** is month-pay; **회식** = gather + eat — the team dinner that's officially optional and practically not.

And the trio you've been owed since 주세요 arrived: **이거 / 그거 / 저거** — this, that-by-you, that-over-there. M6 gave you the place-trio (여기/거기/저기); this is the same geometry for things.

## Part 2 — The bricks

Commute verbs, office rooms, jobs, paperwork, and the pointing set.

::vocab::

**Note the pairs:** 끝나다 (it ends) vs **끝내다** (I end it) — one vowel of agency. 늦다 (late, generally) vs **지각하다** (late, officially). 틀리다 (wrong answer) vs **실수하다** (wrong action).

## Part 3 — The glue

### 1. -아/어 보다 — try doing

Conjugate the verb as if for -아/어요, then hand it to **보다**: do it *and see*.

- 이거 **먹어 보세요** — try eating this (every host, every table)
- 한국어로 **말해 보세요** — try saying it in Korean
- 새 단어를 **외워 보세요** — try memorizing new words

This is 보다's third job — see (M1), see-an-exam (M15), and now *attempt*. The try-flavor is what makes requests gentle: no stakes, just see how it goes. Past tense works too: 먹어 봤어요 — I've tried it.

### 2. Needing, backwards

**필요하다** runs on the 있어요 chassis: 돈이 **필요해요** — 'money is needed (to me).' The needed thing is the subject; you're just the venue. Same with favors: 부탁하다's noun — 부탁이 있어요 — a favor exists, and it's headed your way.

### 3. Office survival phrases

**잘 부탁드립니다** (I'm in your hands — every first meeting) · **수고하셨습니다** (the leaving ritual — gap deck) · and the 눈치 to know when to deploy each (also gap deck; unteachable, but nameable).

## Part 4 — Building sentences

**Spot the recycling:** 새 단어를 외워 보세요 sends M15's 외우다 through the new try-machine wearing M13's 새, and 동생은 회사에서 일하기 시작했어요 runs M15's start-machine on M2's oldest verb.

::sentences::

**Office bingo:** 출근했어요? · 퇴근 안 해요? · 수고하셨습니다 — the three bells of the white-collar day.

## Part 5 — Practice

Cover the right column and translate; then flip and build the Korean.

1. Try eating this. → 이거 먹어 보세요.
2. Try speaking in Korean. → 한국어로 말해 보세요.
3. I work at a company. → 저는 회사에 다녀요.
4. I get off work at six. → 여섯 시에 퇴근해요.
5. I worked late yesterday, so I'm tired. → 어제 야근해서 피곤해요.
6. I sent the documents by email. → 서류를 이메일로 보냈어요.
7. I made a mistake. But it's okay. → 실수했어요. 하지만 괜찮아요.
8. From next week, I'm on vacation. → 다음 주부터 휴가예요.

Business-card drill: introduce yourself as if handing over a 명함 — name (M4's copula), job (직업), where you 다녀요. Two hands, slight bow, 잘 부탁드립니다.
