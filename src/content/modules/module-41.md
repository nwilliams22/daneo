# Korean, Word-First — Module 41: The Body, Up Close

Band 5 walks back to Module 14's body — and leans in. Ring 1 named the big parts; this module does the detail work: the face top-to-bottom (이마 to 혀), the hands in action (grip, touch, wave, bow), the material body (피부, 뼈, 땀, 털), and the body that acts **without you** — breaths, sighs, yawns, sneezes, trembling hands. The glue is **처럼** (like) — the simile particle — because once you can name baby skin, Korean expects you to compare things to it.

**How to use this:** watch the particles more than the nouns. The involuntary shelf runs on 이/가 + a happen-verb (하품이 나와요, 손이 떨려요) — the body as weather, reported rather than commanded. And let the compounds self-assemble: half this module is parts you already own, welded.

---

## Part 1 — The body builds itself

Korean body vocabulary is a compound machine running on Ring 1 parts: 손 + 바닥 (M13's floor) = the palm; 발 + 가락 = toes (M28's 손가락-note promised them); 눈 + 빛 = the look in someone's eyes; 배 + 꼽 = the navel a formal bow folds its hands over. Learn one new root here (등 — the body's back, inside 발등) and Korean hands you 손등 free.

The other half is the **involuntary body** — what it does without a permission slip. Each event picks its own escape verb, and none of them is "do":

- **나다** (M28's eruption door) — 땀이 나요, 냄새가 나요, 쥐가 나요 (a cramp!)
- **나오다** (M28) — 하품이 나와요, 재채기가 나와요, 한숨이 나와요
- **쉬다** — 숨을/한숨을 쉬어요 (yes, spelled like M8's rest-쉬다 — the radar's best catch today)
- **떨리다** — 손이 떨려요, 목소리가 떨려요: M36's machine reporting your own nerves back to you

Grammar of humility: the body acts, 이/가 marks it, and you just file the report.

**The passive party pays a feeling:** 떨다 + 리 = 떨리다 — you don't tremble your hands; they tremble at you (M35's do/happen split, aimed inward). And a retro-decode: M36's 떨어지다 was 떨다 + -아/어지다 all along — shaken until it lets go.

## Part 3 — The glue: 처럼

*Like / as.* A particle, straight onto the noun, no conjugation — the simile in one syllable:

- **아기 피부처럼 부드러워요** — soft like baby skin
- **고양이처럼 하품을 해요** — yawns like a cat
- **아빠처럼 한숨을 쉬어요** — sighs just like his father

You've had likeness since M24 — but 같다 is a **verdict**, parked at the end: 아기 피부 같아요 (it's like baby skin; case closed). 처럼 rides mid-sentence and lets the real adjective finish the thought — that's the whole upgrade. (M9's 같이 moonlights the same way in casual speech — 너같이 = 너처럼 — now you know what it was doing.) The measuring cousin 만큼 (*as much as*) waits for a later module.

Like 마다 (M31), that's the entire particle. Band 5's glue is light on purpose.

## Part 4 — Building sentences

**Spot the recycling:** M38's -자마자 closes the eyes it once opened, -(으)면서 sighs through M10's homework, M36's 젖다 puts on M24's badge for a warning label, and M28's 나다 finally delivers the bread smell its note promised.

::sentences::

**Gap deck:** suspense as a fistful of sweat (손에 땀을 쥐다), laughter as navel loss (배꼽 빠지게 웃다), and the deadline as fire on the instep (발등에 불이 떨어지다).
