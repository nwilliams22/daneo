# Korean, Word-First — Module 92: Existence and Matter

M22 walked you into 자연 and 세상; M49 lifted your eyes to 지구 and 태양; M41 gave you 숨 and 체중 and M34 the 정신 that runs the body. This module names what IS underneath all of them: life as a force (생명, 존재, 목숨, 영혼), the world as stuff (우주, 물질, 자원, 대기, 기후), the body as an organism (육체, 심장, 호흡, 신경, 감각), and four native things the essay never mentions but the mouth always does (둥지, 참새, 꼬리, 틈). Thirty-four Grade-C words, and a thesis for the glue: **an essay states a fact and then weighs it.** Korean has four endings for the weighing, and each one takes a fact and does something different with it — *only that* (**-(으)ㄹ 따름이다**), *true, but* (**-기는 하다**), *not even that* (**-기는커녕**), *and moreover* (**-(으)ㄴ/는 데다가**). Four ways to concede a fact and move past it, taught on the nouns facts are made of.

**How to use this:** the nouns are two-character hanja sums and you own a half of almost every one (生 命 現 實 人 物 體 氣 表 面 心 神 感 子 女 出) — decode each once, then let the drawer hold them. Read Parts 2–5 as one lesson with four dials: every ending below sits on a fact, and the table at the end of Part 5 is the whole module on one line. The sentence deck is eight facts weighed; read it for the weighing, not the facts.

## Part 1 — The bricks: four drawers of what is

M86's 의존하다 note carried 存 with a promissory note — "존재, existence, rides frozen; it belongs to M92" — and M45's 정신적 note split exhaustion in two and shelved half of it — "정신적 vs 육체적 (bodily; a later shelf)". This is the later shelf, and the four drawers are the four things an essay can say exist:

- **BEING (생명 존재 존재하다 목숨 영혼 현실 현실적 현상):** three lives — M25's 인생 the span, M88's 삶 the living of it, 생명 the force, and 목숨 the one you can lose — plus 존재 (what is), 영혼 (what remains), 현실 (what is the case), and 현상 (what appears). 존재하다 is the verb M1's 있다 turns into when a sentence needs a thing to BE rather than to be somewhere.
- **MATTER (우주 인류 물질 물체 핵 자원 대기 기후 표면 하천 계곡 물기):** from the largest container to the smallest wet — the universe, the species in it, matter and the objects it makes, the nucleus, the resources, the atmosphere and its long-run mood (기후 against M7's 날씨), a surface, the report's river (하천 against M22's 강), the summer valley, and the moisture you wipe off a counter.
- **THE ORGANISM (육체 심장 호흡 신경 감각 시선 눈길 태아 출산 자녀):** the body as flesh (육체 against M14's 몸), its pump, its breath in the nurse's count (호흡 against M41's 숨), its wiring, its senses, the two lines an eye draws (시선 for the novel, 눈길 for the kitchen), and the three words of a birth — the child before, the event, the children after.
- **THE NATIVE THINGS (둥지 참새 꼬리 틈):** a nest, the bird that is simply there, a tail, and a crack — in a wall or in a day.

::vocab::

**Radar patrol:** 생명's 명 is 命, not the counter-名 of M5's 명 (네 명) · 대기 is 大氣 the atmosphere here, 待機 waiting in 대기실 and 대기 번호, and neither is the 대기 inside M76's 꼭대기 · 눈길 is the eye's path with M14's 눈 and a snowy road with M11's — one spelling, sky and face · 물질 and 물체 carry the thing-物, not M1's 물 · 자원's 원 is 源 the spring, not M9's 원 (₩) nor the 原 of M86's 원인 · 현상 is 現象; NIKL lists no prize-懸賞 row · 육체's 육 is 肉, not M2's 육 · and the four lives sort in one line: 인생 is the span, 삶 is the living, 생명 is the force, 목숨 is the stake.

## Part 2 — The glue: -(으)ㄹ 따름이다 — nothing but

Prospective badge + **따름** (a dependent noun, frozen) + 이다: 감사할 **따름입니다** = I can only be grateful — M1's 감사합니다 taken apart and rebuilt as the award speech; 최선을 다할 **따름이에요** (M39's 최선, M61's 다하다) = all one can do is one's best; 기다릴 따름이에요 (M8's 기다리다) = there is nothing to do but wait. On nouns through the copula: 작은 존재일 따름이다 = is merely a small being. It is the formal twin of the 뿐 frame — M89 gave you -(으)ㄹ 뿐이다 with the rule M55's 뿐 note set, "뿐 declares the menu has one line" — and 따름 declares the same menu from a podium. The 뿐 sentence can end in 이야 or 이에요; 따름 wants 입니다 or the page's 이다, and a speaker who says 따름이야 is being funny.

**Calibration:** 기다려요 (I wait) · 기다릴 뿐이에요 (I merely wait) · 기다릴 따름입니다 (I can do nothing but wait — the press conference). Same fact, three heights.

## Part 3 — The glue: -기는 하다 — it is true that… (but)

The -기 nominalizer from M13 + 는 + 하다: the verb is frozen into a noun, topicalized, and handed to 하다 to carry its tense — 비싸**기는 해요** (M7's 비싸다) = it IS expensive, said with the *but* already in the voice; 가기는 갈 거예요 = I'll go, technically. The doubled form repeats the verb instead of using 하다: 먹**기는 먹었어요** (M1's 먹다) = eat I did; 닦기는 닦았는데 = wipe it I did, but. Casual it clips to **-긴 하다**: 좋긴 한데 = it's good, but. Sort it against the two *but*s you own: -지만 from M7 and -(으)나 from M85 (that md: "-지만 from M7 with the seams welded shut") both join two clauses and put the contrast BETWEEN them; -기는 하다 concedes INSIDE the first verb, before the second clause arrives — and very often no second clause comes at all, because the 는 has already said it.

**Calibration:** 비싸요 (it's expensive) · 비싸지만 맛있어요 (expensive, but tasty) · 비싸기는 해요 (it IS expensive… — and the listener supplies the *but*). The third grants the fact and reserves the verdict.

## Part 4 — The glue: -기는커녕 — far from, let alone

The same -기 + 는 + **커녕** (a particle, frozen): 돈을 벌**기는커녕** 다 썼어요 (M9's 돈, M16's 벌다, M9's 다, M10's 쓰다) = far from earning money, I spent it all. On a noun it drops the -기: 밥**은커녕** 물도 못 마셨어요 (M1's 밥, 물 and 마시다, M3's 못) = never mind a meal, I couldn't even drink water. The anatomy never changes: [the expected, bigger thing]은/는커녕 [the lesser thing]도 + a negative — the second half wants 도 from M2 or, in print, the 조차 of M89 (that md: 조차 "names the bottom of the scale"), and the sentence must end badly. It is always a step DOWN: 커녕 says the big thing did not happen and then reaches for the small thing that did not happen either. Put the bigger thing after 도 and the sentence breaks.

**Calibration:** 밥을 못 먹었어요 (I couldn't eat) · 밥은커녕 물도 못 마셨어요 (never mind eating — not even water) · 밥은커녕 물조차 못 마셨어요 (the same, on the page). Each step lowers the floor.

## Part 5 — The glue: -(으)ㄴ/는 데다가 — on top of

M55's 데 — the situation-noun whose note confessed that 그런데 was 그런 + 데 all along — wears the badge -(으)ㄴ/-는 from M24 and takes **다가**: 비가 오**는 데다가** 바람까지 불어요 (M11's 비, 바람 and 불다, M1's 오다, 까지 from M6) = it's raining, and on top of that the wind is blowing. Adjectives take -(으)ㄴ (맑은 데다가, 약한 데다가), verbs -는, the past -(으)ㄴ (먹은 데다가). The rule is DIRECTION: the second fact piles on the first in the same sense — two good things, or two bad ones — never one of each; and the second clause likes 도 or 까지 on its noun to mark the pile. Sort it against -(으)며 from M85, which lists two facts flat, and against -(으)ㄹ 뿐만 아니라 from M89 (that md: "= not only… (but also)"), which is the same pile in a suit — 데다가 is the mouth's *and what's more*, 뿐만 아니라 the page's.

**Calibration:** 비가 오고 바람이 불어요 (rain and wind, listed) · 비가 오며 바람이 분다 (the same, on the page) · 비가 오는 데다가 바람까지 불어요 (rain — AND wind, piled). Only the third makes the weather worse as it goes.

| Ending | Takes a fact and… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| -(으)ㄹ 따름이다 | shrinks it to only-that | 기다릴 따름입니다 — I can only wait |
| -기는 하다 | grants it, reserves the verdict | 비싸기는 해요 — it IS expensive (but) |
| -기는커녕 | denies it and the smaller one under it | 밥은커녕 물도 못 마셨어요 — not even water |
| -(으)ㄴ/는 데다가 | piles a second one on top | 비가 오는 데다가 바람까지 — and wind, too |

## Part 6 — Building sentences

**Spot the recycling:** the copula rides the prospective badge under 존재일 따름이에요; -지만 from M7 follows the concession in 변하기는 했지만 and the -는데 of M53 follows the doubled 닦기는 닦았는데; the long negation from M28 closes 심하지 않아요 and M3's 안 and 못 close the two 커녕 sentences; the double-subject frame M24 first showed carries both 데다가 sentences (물이 맑은 데다가 공기도, 심장이 약한 데다가 호흡도); -(으)면서 from M38 puts the dodging and the answering in one body; and the badge -(으)ㄴ from M24 sits under 넓은, 작은, 맑은 and 약한.

::sentences::

**Gap deck:** the sentence that releases you from a thing already on the table (신경 쓰지 마세요), the reply with nobody home behind it (영혼 없는 대답), and the sparrow that cannot pass the mill (참새가 방앗간을 그냥 지나치랴).
