# Korean, Word-First — Module 50: The Moving City

Ring 1 assembled your city a piece at a time: M6 handed you the rides (버스, 지하철, 택시), M12 the whole transit system and the streets under it, M22 split 도시 from 시골, and M23 taught you to ask the way. This module is the city as a SYSTEM: the built fabric (빌딩, 건축, 시설, 공간), the road network from 골목길 to 고속도로, the fleet one register deeper (전철, 열차, 승용차), and a twelve-verb motion engine to run it all. The glue is intention, spoken: **-(으)려고 (하다)** — in order to, planning to, about to — the ending M12's errand-form has been holding a seat for. You already survive this city; today you narrate it.

**How to use this:** watch the registers. This shelf keeps two sets of books — the spoken city and the written one — and the difference is never meaning, only paperwork. Learn those words as pairs. Then spend your reps on the twelve motion verbs; they are the module.

## Part 1 — Two sets of books

Korean's city vocabulary splits by register, and today the ledger balances:

- **The car column:** 차 (M2) is what you say, 자동차 what forms say, 승용차 what statistics say — one vehicle, three registers, and a grade-A debt paid with a hanja poem (self-moving-cart).
- **The rail column:** 기차 (M12) vs 열차 (the PA system's word); 지하철 (M6) vs 전철 (the network's word — much of the 'underground' runs in daylight).
- **The everywhere column:** 여기저기 in the mouth, 곳곳 in the headline.
- **The verb column:** 가다 and 있다 in speech; 향하다 and 위치하다 in print.
- **Debt-payment day:** M38's 도로 note promised 고속도로, M36's 사고 note pre-built 교통사고, M48's 대중 note pre-assembled 대중교통, and 주차장 (M37) finally hands over its 주차.

## Part 3 — The glue: intention, out loud

### 1. -(으)려고 — in order to

Vowel and ㄹ stems take **려고**, consonant stems **으려고** — then say what you did about it: 시장에 가**려고** 일찍 나왔어요 = I left early to go to the market. And the radar M12 loaded long ago: **-(으)러 rides only the 가다/오다 family** and names an errand — M12's own fine print said other purposes 'wait for later modules.' This is that module. -(으)려고 rides ANY verb and carries intent: 사러 가요 is an errand; 사려고 돈을 모았어요 is a campaign.

### 2. -(으)려고 하다 — planning to / about to

Add 하다 and the intention stands on its own: 지금 나가**려고 해요** = I'm about to head out; 내년에 이사하려고 해요 = we're planning to move. Then the on-the-verge job, granted even to things with no will: 버스가 출발**하려고 해요** — the bus 'intends' to leave; 비가 오**려고 해요** — the sky is about to rain. Korean hands out intention generously; learn to read the forecast in it.

**Calibration:** register, again. -기 위해(서) (M44) is purpose in its Sunday clothes — essays, plans, vision boards. -(으)려고 is what mouths actually say: 합격하기 위해서 공부합니다, writes the application; 합격하려고 공부해요, says everyone in the study room. Same meaning, different paperwork — the month-pay/wage split, applied to grammar.
