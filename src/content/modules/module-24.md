# Korean, Word-First — Module 24: Opinions and Modifiers

Twenty-three modules in, there's still a hole in your Korean: you can say the movie IS fun (영화가 재미있어요), but not *a fun movie*. The garnish is **-(으)ㄴ/-는** — the turn-around badge that parks any adjective, then any whole clause, in front of a noun. You've been wearing it unknowingly since Module 13; today it gets named. The theme is opinions — the kind people, strange rumors, and best cafes that modifiers exist to argue about.

> **Warm-up (Module 23):** build these out loud — Module 23 blocks, combinations you've never seen written: *Just exchange money at the bank* (은행에서 환전하면 돼요) · *When did you come back home?* (집에 언제 돌아왔어요?) · *Is the information center that way?* (안내소가 저쪽에 있어요?). If the blocks don't snap together, visit Review first.

**How to use this:** opinions in Korean are short — 최고예요, 별로예요, 글쎄요 — but the *reasons* need modifiers. Learn the badge first, then spend the vocabulary disagreeing politely.

## Part 1 — The adjective turns around

Korean adjectives have been verbs in disguise since Module 1 — they conjugate (무서워요, 비쌌어요), so until now they could only live at the END of a sentence: 이야기가 무서워요. To stand in FRONT of a noun, the dictionary form trades its -다 for a badge:

- 무섭다 → **무서운** 이야기 — a scary story
- 좋다 → **좋은** 생각 — a good idea

English turns "the movie is fun" into "a fun movie" by shuffling word order. Korean shuffles nothing — it stamps the ending. (M13's 새 was the preview: it came pre-turned, 새 집 with no badge needed. Every other adjective catches up today.)

The real unlock: **whole clauses wear the badge.** 마음이 착한 사람 — "[heart-is-kind] person," someone kind-hearted. Korean has no *who*, *that*, or *which*; the badge does their entire job. This is how Korean builds relative clauses, and it's the biggest syntax gap left between you and real sentences.

And here's the twist — you've used this machinery for eleven modules: 그런데 (M7), -(으)ㄴ 후에 (M13), -는 것 (M17). All badges in disguise. Today, the paternity tests.

## Part 2 — The bricks

Talk nouns, judgment adjectives, and the little words opinions hide behind.

**The compound radar:** 오해 = 오 (wrong) + 해 (untangle — M10's 이해's own 해): understanding, done wrong. 대화 = 대 (facing) + 화 (speech — 전화's 화): the face-to-face version of a phone call. 반대 flips 대화's facing-대 with 반 (flip — not M19's half-반). And 진심/관심 share 심, the heart-syllable — 진짜's true-진 in front of one, and whatever catches yours in front of the other.

## Part 3 — The glue

### 1. Adjectives: -(으)ㄴ

Batchim → **-은**: 좋은, 많은, 젊은. No batchim → **-ㄴ**: 예쁜, 비싼, 유명한, 이상한. ㅂ-family → **-운**: 무서운, 매운, 아름다운, 부러운 (the ㅂ waves goodbye exactly as it did in 무서워요). One exception squad: adjectives built on 있다/없다 keep their verb card and take **-는** — 맛있는, 재미있는, 맛없는.

### 2. Verbs, present: -는

Straight onto the stem, batchim or not: 좋아하는 노래 (the song I like), 자주 가는 카페 (the cafe I often go to). ㄹ-stems drop the ㄹ first: 알다 → **아는** 사람 (someone I know), 살다 → **사는** 곳 (where I live). The decode: M17's -는 것 was THIS badge plus the thing-word — 요리하는 것, "the cooking thing," all along.

### 3. Verbs, past: -(으)ㄴ

The adjective badge, borrowed for finished actions: 어제 **본** 영화 (the movie I saw yesterday), 여행에서 **산** 기념품 (the souvenir I bought), 엄마가 **만든** 음식 (mom-made food — ㄹ drops again), **들은** 이야기 (a story I heard — M2's ㄷ still flips). The decode: M13's -(으)ㄴ 후에 was this badge plus 후 — 먹은 후에, "the after of the eaten," all along.

### 4. The kind-of words

The ㅎ-adjectives wear the badge and become the course's most useful question: **어떻다** (to be how) gives 어때요? (how is it?), 어땠어요? (how was it?), and badge-form **어떤** — what KIND of. Its siblings 이렇다/그렇다/저렇다 give **이런/그런/저런** — this-kind, that-kind, that-kind-over-there. And two cold cases close: M7's 그런데 = 그런 + 데 ("that kind of situation, and…"), M7's 그래서 = 그렇다 + -아서 ("being that way, so…"). M10's 어떻게 was 어떻다's adverb the whole time.

> **The third badge (teaser):** there's a future one — **-(으)ㄹ** — and you've said it since Band 1: 먹**을** 거예요 is "[to-eat] thing is," 할 수 있어요 is "[to-do] way exists." It gets its full debut when -(으)ㄹ 때 lands in Module 25.

## Part 4 — Building sentences

**Spot the recycling:** 관광객이 많은 곳은 항상 복잡해요 points the new badge at M23's tourists, 여행에서 산 기념품이에요 gives the past badge M23's souvenir, and 모르면 물어보면 돼요 runs M23's just-do-X machine at double -면.

::sentences::

**Proverb check (gap deck):** 아는 만큼 보인다 — you see as much as you know. Built on this module's own badge (아는 = 알다 + -는), and truer every module.

## Part 5 — Practice

Cover the right column and translate; then flip and build the Korean.

1. That's a good idea! → 좋은 생각이에요!
2. How was the movie you saw yesterday? → 어제 본 영화 어땠어요?
3. Don't believe that kind of story. → 그런 이야기를 믿지 마세요.
4. It's the song I like the most. → 제가 제일 좋아하는 노래예요.
5. Actually, it's a little different. → 사실은 조금 달라요.
6. I have something I'm curious about. → 궁금한 것이 있어요.
7. If you don't know, just ask. → 모르면 물어보면 돼요.
8. Kind-hearted people are the best. → 마음이 착한 사람이 최고예요.

Badge patrol: pick five adjectives you've owned since Band 1 and park each in front of a noun it's never met — 매운 것, 바쁜 날, 따뜻한 마음. If it sounds like a K-drama episode title, you're doing it right.
