# Korean, Word-First — Module 14: Body and Health

The body from head to 발, the pharmacy-and-hospital kit, and the machine every parent, doctor, and warning sign runs on: **-지 마세요** — *don't.* Along the way, half your old syllables pick up second jobs — this is the module where the homographs earn their keep.

> **Warm-up (Module 13):** build these out loud — Module 13 blocks, combinations you've never seen written: *Before eating, I wash my hands* (먹기 전에 손을 씻어요) · *The living room is spacious* (거실이 넓어요) · *Please open the window* (창문을 열어 주세요). If the blocks don't snap together, visit Review first.

**How to use this:** the body words are short, ancient, and native — no compounding tricks to lean on, so this checklist is more raw memorization than M13's. The payback: body words power more idioms per syllable than any other set in the language (gap deck has the evidence).

## Part 1 — Old syllables, second jobs

This module is a homograph festival, and that's on purpose — meet them here, in context, before they ambush you in the wild:

- **눈** — M11's snow, now also *eye*. 눈이 와요 vs 눈이 아파요.
- **열** — M5's ten, M13's 열다/열쇠, now also *fever*: heat that 있어요.
- **병** — M5's bottle-counter, now also *illness* — and 병원 finally decomposes: illness + house.
- **팔** — M2's eight, now also *arm*. Two of them, not eight.
- **다리** — *leg*, and also every bridge in Korea: a river's legs.
- **배** — M3 already warned you: stomach, boat, and pear share one syllable.

And two compounds you can decode on sight: **코피** (nose + blood = the K-drama overwork nosebleed — one vowel from 커피) and **눈물** (eye + water = tears).

## Part 2 — The bricks

The body, the clinic, and the verbs of getting sick and getting better.

::vocab::

**Family check-in:** 아프다 — the ㅡ-drop patriarch whose shape you've said since 배고파요 — is finally official: 아파요. 눕다 joins the ㅂ-irregulars (누워요), 목마르다 joins the 르-family (목말라요). And **낫다 opens a brand-new family**: the vanishing ㅅ — 나 + 아요 → **나아요**. It's Korean's rarest irregular; treasure it.

## Part 3 — The glue

### 1. -지 마세요 — don't

The prohibition machine. Verb stem + **지 마세요** — no vowel checks, no sound rules, nothing irregular. The easiest glue in the course, and among the most heard:

- **걱정하지 마세요** — don't worry
- 담배를 **피우지 마세요** — don't smoke
- **늦지 마세요** — don't be late (yes, it takes adjectives too)

Politeness fans: it's M9's -(으)세요 with 말다 (to refrain) in the engine bay — the request machine, reversed.

### 2. The sickness frames

Korean reports illness in three set frames, all built from machinery you own:

- **[body part]가 아파요** — it hurts: 머리가 아파요, 목이 아파요
- **감기에 걸렸어요** — caught a cold (M12's 걸리다: hangs, takes time, and now catches)
- **열이 있어요 / 힘이 없어요** — fever exists; energy doesn't (the 있다-factory, diagnostics division)

### 3. The clinic script

몸이 안 좋아요 → 여기에 누우세요 → 약을 먹은 후에 푹 쉬세요 → 빨리 나으세요. You can now be examined, prescribed, and wished well entirely within this module.

## Part 4 — Building sentences

**Spot the recycling:** 약을 먹은 후에 푹 쉬세요 and 운동한 후에 물을 마셔요 run Module 13's after-machine on new fuel — and 자기 전에 이를 닦으세요 sends M8's tooth-이 through M13's before-machine.

::sentences::

**Pharmacy note:** Korean *eats* medicine — 약을 먹다. Nobody swallows, takes, or ingests; you sit down and eat your pills like a small sad meal.

## Part 5 — Practice

Cover the right column and translate; then flip and build the Korean.

1. Don't worry. → 걱정하지 마세요.
2. My head hurts. My throat hurts too. → 머리가 아파요. 목도 아파요.
3. I caught a cold. → 감기에 걸렸어요.
4. After taking the medicine, rest well. → 약을 먹은 후에 푹 쉬세요.
5. Don't smoke in the hospital. → 병원에서 담배를 피우지 마세요.
6. Get well soon! → 빨리 나으세요!
7. I'm not feeling well. → 몸이 안 좋아요.
8. I'm so thirsty. → 너무 목말라요.

Symptom drill: point at five body parts and complain about each — [part]가 아파요. Congratulations: you are now fluent in visiting a Korean doctor and being dramatic at home.
