# Korean, Word-First — Module 68: Nod, Glance, Reach

M41 taught the body's first words — 흔들다, 숙이다, 눈빛 — and left a debt on the books: 고개, the head-as-it-turns, rode along free. This module pays it and fields the full gesture squad: the nod that agrees, the three new gaze ships, the four ways to stand (away, against, ahead, beyond), the 뛰어- run squadron on M66's chassis, and the hand-and-foot grid — four body parts from zero new roots. What the head, eyes, hands, and feet say without words, Korean says in compounds — and you already own nearly every part.

**How to use this:** read every decode before you drill — twenty of these twenty-nine words are welds of words you already know, so let the parts do the memorizing. Spend your reps on conjugation: the polite forms contract (끄덕여요, never 끄덕이어요; 뛰어들어요; 돌아서요), and the ㄹ-stems drop before ㅅ (뛰어드세요). The grid nouns (오른손, 왼손, 오른발, 왼발) come free; the -서다 line does not — drill those four as one family.

## Part 1 — Debts, welded

Almost everything here is assembled from taught parts:

- **The 고개 IOU comes due:** M41's 흔들다 and 숙이다 both ran on it for free; now the head-as-it-moves is yours, with the full collocation row — 고개를 끄덕이다 (new) / 흔들다 (M41) / 숙이다 (M41) / 돌리다 (M37). Radar vs M14's 목, the anatomical neck.
- **The gaze fleet grows:** M15's 물어보다 opened the -보다 compounds; M49's 바라보다, M50's 둘러보다, M52's 돌아보다, M56's 살펴보다, and M61's 찾아보다 followed. Today: the stare, the look-down, and the peer-into.
- **The -서다 line:** M14's 서다 standing inside four compounds — 돌아서다 (M37's 돌다), 맞서다 (M15's 맞다), 앞서다 (M6's 앞), 넘어서다 (M36's 넘다).
- **M66's chassis, new engines:** last module welded 달려/따라 onto 가다/오다; the same build now takes M28's 뛰다, 날다, and M12's 건너다.
- **The grid:** M6's 왼쪽/오른쪽 × M8's 손 / M14's 발 = four cells, zero new roots.

## Part 2 — The bricks

::vocab::

**Radar patrol:** the near-misses are mapped. 고개 is the head that MOVES (and, second sense, a mountain pass — the 아리랑 고개); M14's 목 is the neck that gets sore. 손뼉 is the native palms that strike; M51's 박수 is the hanja applause you send and receive. 짓 (the regrettable act) has NO relation to M54's 짓다 (the build-verb) — one misbehaves, one makes rice. 손잡이's thing-you-grab -이 is not the measure--이 of M57's 높이 — same letter, different factory. And 돌아서다 turns to LEAVE where M52's 돌아보다 turns to look back.

## Part 3 — The machines

### 1. The gaze fleet

Three new ships, three angles: **쳐다보다** fixes and holds (rude on people, fine on M11's 하늘), **내려다보다** looks down from above — M6's 내리다 in the weld, and 사람을 내려다보다 condescends exactly like English — and **들여다보다** peers INTO: the enter-sense of M28's 들다 aims the eyes inside a 거울, a 핸드폰, a 문제.

### 2. The -서다 line

M14's 서다, standing four ways: **돌아서다** turns the back (hearts too: 마음이 돌아서다), **맞서다** plants the feet against (문제에 맞서다), **앞서다** stands at the front of the race, and **넘어서다** has already crossed and planted its flag — 목표를 넘어서다. Face, oppose, lead, surpass: posture as biography.

### 3. The 뛰어- squadron

M66's compound chassis, one module later: M28's 뛰다 welds onto 들다 (뛰어들다 — the pool dive and the career plunge), 나가다 (뛰어나가다 — the school bell's verb), 오다 (뛰어오다), and M16's 다니다 (뛰어다니다 — the downstairs neighbor's complaint). Same chassis, other engines: 날아오다 (M28's 날다 — balls, birds, rumors) and 건너오다 (M12's 건너다 — across the street, across the sea).

### 4. The grid and the grip

왼쪽/오른쪽 + 손/발 gives the four cells — and the culture runs on them: 오른손 gives and shakes, 왼손 supports, 왼발 gets footballers scouted. 손발 runs the pair as a team (the gap deck grades the teamwork), 손잡이 is the thing-you-grab, 손수건 the hand-towel, and 악수 (握手, grab-hand) closes the module the way meetings close: 마주 서서, right hand out.

## Part 4 — Building sentences

**Spot the recycling:** M38 runs the clock twice (-(으)면서 under the nod, -자마자 under the school bell), M53's -는데 and M52's -아/어 버리다 stage the turned back, M20's -(으)면 and M36's 보이다 shrink the worries from the mountaintop, M42's -잖아요 protests the stare, and M25's -(으)ㄹ 때 frames the first handshake.

::sentences::

**Gap deck:** teamwork as matching extremities (손발이 맞다), wide feet as a wide network (발이 넓다), and composure as the unblinked eye (눈 하나 깜짝 안 하다).
