# Korean, Word-First — Module 84: Loose Ends

The last module of a ring is where the promises get kept. Ring 1 walked past six Grade-A words — 급, 호, 잎, 양복, 공중전화, 장미 — and three of them were named in shipped notes as riding frozen or waiting their turn; this is the turn. Ten Grade-C companions come out of the same drawers (the 급 drawer, the 호 drawer, the 양- shelf, the two 공중s), and the real payload is grammar: four endings that no module owns although every drafter and half the notes have been paraphrasing around them — **-(으)ㄹ게요** (the promise), **-기 때문에** (the formal because — the IOU M29's 때문 note wrote out in words), **-(으)ㄹ 수도 있다** (the might), and **-(으)려면** (if you mean to). Sixteen nouns, four glue parts, a ring closed.

**How to use this:** the vocabulary is small on purpose — read every decode once, then spend your reps on the four glue parts, each of which is a contrast against an ending you already own (-(으)ㄹ 거예요 from M8, -(으)니까 from M10, -(으)ㄹ 수 있다 from M10, -(으)면 from M20). The new endings are not new machinery; they are old machinery with one more piece welded on.

## Part 1 — The last six, and their company

Every word on this shelf is a receipt for something an earlier module said:

- **The 급 drawer — three hanja on one syllable:** M81's 고급 note called 級 "a FIRST" and left 학급, 등급, 초급 and 중급 frozen; today 급 stands bare (TOPIK's 삼 급, taekwondo's coloured belts) and 학급 and 특급 thaw around it. M16's 월급 is pay-給 — a character shown here for the first time — and M38's 급하다 is urgent-急, which 급히 and 긴급 both ride. Same sound, three ranks of meaning.
- **The 호 drawer — the label counter alone:** M76's 호선 read the number-號 inside the subway line; 호 is that 號 by itself (삼백일 호, 제일 호, 제팔 호 태풍), 호실 puts it on a door in full, and 번지 puts M12's 번 on the ground — the lot number the road-name addresses replaced in 2014.
- **The leaf and the rose:** M49's 나뭇잎 note said the bare word "waits its own turn" and M82's 꽃잎 rode it a second time — 잎 lands, a homophone of M14's 입 until a vowel follows; M76's 송이 note called 장미 백 송이 the proposal cliché with 장미 riding frozen — 薔薇, two hanja that spell one flower, and the ring ends on it.
- **The 양- shelf and the two 공중s:** the ship-prefix M40's 양파 decoded stamps 양복, 양주, and one sense of 양식; the other 양식 is a form (樣式), and the verb tells them apart; 정장 lands the dress-裝 M80's 포장 note held frozen. 공중전화 is 公衆, the public's phone, filed frozen by M83's 전화기 note; 공중 alone is 空中, mid-air — 공중화장실 versus 공중에서 is the module's best radar.

::vocab::

**Radar patrol:** three 급s — 級 in 급/학급/특급, 給 in M16's 월급, 急 in 급히/긴급 and M38's 급하다 · four 호s — 號 here, 濠 in M27's 호주, 湖 in M22's 호수, and none at all in M23's 호텔 · 입/잎 — the mouth and the leaf say 입 alone and part company at 이비/이피 · the 양 of 양복/양식/양주 is ocean-洋, not the amount-量 of M63's 양 or the sun-陽 of M49's 태양 · and 공중 is 公衆 in the payphone and the public restroom, 空中 in the air.

## Part 2 — The glue: -(으)ㄹ게요 — the promise

Vowel and ㄹ stems take **ㄹ게요**, consonant stems **을게요** — 제가 **할게요** (I'll do it), 먼저 **갈게요** (I'll go ahead), 나중에 **전화할게요**, 다시 **올게요**, 금방 **갈게요**. Said 할께요: the ㄱ tenses in the mouth and stays soft on the page, the way 할 거예요 says 할 꺼예요. Casual: -(으)ㄹ게. First person only, spoken, and made TO the listener — it answers the other person, and it can be withdrawn if they object. That is what separates it from the two endings you already have. **-(으)ㄹ 거예요 from M8** announces a plan, or guesses — g8_future_probably: "who the subject is decides"; the **volunteer -겠- from M49** pledges with the will engaged — 제가 하겠습니다 in the meeting; **-(으)ㄹ게요** is that pledge in shirtsleeves — 제가 할게요 in the kitchen, the same will in the 요-register. The mealtime vow of M18, 잘 먹겠습니다, has a twin here: 잘 먹을게요, to the friend who is paying. Radar: -(으)ㄹ게요 never takes a third-person subject (no 비가 올게요) and never asks a question — a promise, never a forecast.

**Calibration:** 갈 거예요 tells you what I intend · 가겠습니다 tells the room I have committed · 갈게요 tells YOU, and waits a beat to see if you mind.

## Part 3 — The glue: -기 때문에 — the formal because

M29's 때문 note wrote the IOU in words — "The formal -기 때문이에요 answers 왜냐하면 — recognize now, produce in Ring 2" — and its 왜냐하면 note gave the shape: "Opens the answer and usually lands on -기 때문이에요. English says because mid-sentence; Korean announces it first." Build it: the -기 nominalizer from M13 + M29's 때문 + 에 — because of [verb]-ing: 비가 **오기 때문에** 못 가요. Nouns take 이기 때문에 (학생**이기 때문에** — because I'm a student), the past takes -았/었기 때문에 (**늦었기 때문에**), and at the end of a sentence it is **-기 때문이에요** — 왜냐하면 시험이 있기 때문이에요. The because-family, three rows: **-아/어서** (Ring 1) is neutral and spoken, and never precedes a command; **-(으)니까 from M10** is spoken and the one that precedes an order — the rule of thumb M10 wrote, "if the sentence ends in 주세요/-(으)세요/-(으)ㄹ까요, the reason takes **니까**"; **-기 때문에** is written, formal, objective — the newspaper's, the essay's, the announcement's — and, like -아/어서, never precedes a command. Radar vs the noun-때문에 of M29: 눈 때문에 is the same 때문 — the verb version only needs -기 to become a noun first; s_m84_typhoon_express runs the noun form beside this module's verb form so you can hear the seam.

**Calibration:** 늦어서 죄송해요 is the mouth · 늦었기 때문에 is the report · 늦었으니까 먼저 드세요 is the mouth about to give an instruction. Pick the row by who is listening and whether an order follows.

## Part 4 — The glue: -(으)ㄹ 수도 있다 — the might

M10 built the can-machine — "Same hook as the future (-(으)ㄹ), then 수 (a way) + 있다/없다" — and called it "Ability as existence — the 있다 factory's grandest product". Put 도 from M2 on the 수 and ability becomes possibility: 비가 **올 수도 있어요** (it might rain — a way for rain exists, even that one), 늦을 수도 있으니까 먼저 드세요, 그럴 수도 있죠 (that's possible — the conceding shrug, with -죠 from M51). M34's 어쩌면 note ran it once without naming it — "어쩌면 우리가 이길 수도 있어요 (we might just win)" — and today it is named. Sort it against the guess-family: -(으)ㄹ 거예요 from M8 is a prediction you would bet on; 것 같다 from M34 is a hunch; -나 보다 from M34 "wants a clue you can point at"; the evidence -겠- from M49 reads the sky in front of you. -(으)ㄹ 수도 있다 is the weakest of the five — it only says the door is not locked — and its most-used form is the hedge with -(으)니까 from M10: ~수도 있으니까 + advice. Radar: -(으)ㄹ 줄 알다 from M55 is know-how, not possibility — that module drew the line: "-(으)ㄹ 수 있다 says a way EXISTS; -(으)ㄹ 줄 알다 says you've LEARNED the trick".

**Calibration:** 올 거예요 (the app said so) · 올 것 같아요 (I feel it) · 오나 봐요 (look at the umbrellas) · 오겠어요 (look at the sky) · 올 수도 있어요 (nobody has ruled it out). Reach for the last when you want to be right either way.

## Part 5 — The glue: -(으)려면 — if you mean to

M50 gave you intention — "Vowel and ㄹ stems take **려고**, consonant stems **으려고** — then say what you did about it" — and M20 gave you -(으)면. -(으)려면 is the two welded: -(으)려고 하면 contracted — if you intend to X, then you must, or you should. Its natural closers are -아/어야 돼요 from M10 and -(으)세요 from M9: 인천공항에 **가려면** 공항버스를 타세요 — on M83's 인천공항 and 공항버스; **합격하려면** 열심히 공부해야 돼요 — M44's 합격 verbed, with M10's 열심히; 양복을 **사려면** 백화점에 가세요. The fixed phrase after it is M12's 걸리다: 한국어를 잘하려면 시간이 걸려요 — mean to speak it well and it takes time. Radar: -(으)면 alone is if it happens; -(으)려면 is if you MEAN to make it happen — 비가 오면 (if it rains) against 비를 피하려면 (if you mean to dodge it — M36's 피하다). The subject of -(으)려면 always has a will; rain never does.

**Calibration:** 가면 — when you get there · 가려고 — meaning to go, and here is what I did · 가려면 — if going is the plan, here is the rule. Three hinges on one stem: the condition, the intention, the intention made conditional.

## Part 6 — Building sentences
