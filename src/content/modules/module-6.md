# Korean, Word-First — Module 6: Places, Positions, and Finding Your Way

You can say what happened and when. Now: **where.** This module hands you the city — banks, pharmacies, convenience stores, the subway — and the machinery for locating anything in it. The big idea takes one sentence to say and a module to absorb: **Korean has no prepositions. It has position *nouns*.**

**How to use this:** The location frame repeats in almost every sentence here on purpose. By the eighth repetition your brain will stop translating and start seeing the frame — that's the goal.

---

## Part 1 — "On the desk" is "the desk's topside"

English puts its space-words *before* the noun: **on** the desk, **behind** the station. Korean flips it — the space-word is a **noun that follows**, and then 에 seals the phrase:

- 책상 **위**에 — desk **topside**-at → *on the desk*
- 역 **뒤**에 — station **back**-at → *behind the station*
- 가방 **안**에 — bag **inside**-at → *in the bag*
- 은행하고 마트 **사이**에 — bank-and-mart **between**-at → *between the bank and the supermarket*

Eight little nouns run the whole system: 위 · 아래 · 앞 · 뒤 · 옆 · 안 · 밖 · 사이. (Watch the homograph: position 안 *follows* its noun — 가방 안에; negation 안 *precedes* its verb — 안 먹어요. Same syllable, opposite neighborhoods.)

And the three-way here/there: **여기** (near me) · **거기** (near you) · **저기** (far from both) — plus the question that unlocks every map: **어디** (where).

## Part 3 — The glue

### 1. The location frame

**[thing]이/가·은/는 + [landmark] + [position] + 에 있어요**

- 화장실이 **어디에** 있어요? — where's the bathroom?
- 은행은 **병원 옆에** 있어요 — the bank is next to the hospital.

있어요 does triple duty you already know: *exists*, *have*, and now *is located*. One verb, all of English's "there is / it's at / I have."

### 2. (으)로 — heading and means

Consonant + **으로**, vowel or ㄹ + **로**:

- **Direction (the heading, not the endpoint):** 왼쪽**으로** 가요 — go left(ward). Compare: 학교**에** 가요 (destination) vs 학교**로** 가요 (that-a-way).
- **Means:** 지하철**로** 가요 — go by subway. (But the vehicle as an object: 버스**를** 타요 — *ride the bus*.)

### 3. Brackets: from … to …

- **Time:** 부터 ~ 까지 — 아홉 시**부터** 여섯 시**까지** (from 9 to 6)
- **Space:** 에서 ~ 까지 — 집**에서** 학교**까지** (from home to school)

까지 closes both brackets; only the opener changes. And 에서 quietly shows its third face here: *from* a place — the same particle that marks where actions happen and where you get off the taxi (여기**에서** 내려요).

## Part 4 — Building sentences

**Frame first, then swap parts.** Watch 옆/앞/뒤/위/안/사이 rotate through the same skeleton while the skeleton itself never moves.

::sentences::

**Getting someone's attention to ask any of this:** 저기요! — literally "over there!", functionally "excuse me!" It works on waiters, passersby, and lost-looking tourists (you, for now).

## Part 5 — Practice

Cover the right column and translate; then flip and build the Korean.

1. Where is the bathroom? → 화장실이 어디에 있어요?
2. The bank is next to the hospital. → 은행은 병원 옆에 있어요.
3. My phone is in my bag. → 핸드폰이 가방 안에 있어요.
4. I go to work by subway. → 지하철로 회사에 가요.
5. I work from 9 a.m. to 6 p.m. → 오전 아홉 시부터 오후 여섯 시까지 일해요.
6. I walk from home to school. → 집에서 학교까지 걸어요.
7. The pharmacy is close. → 약국은 가까워요.
8. I get off here. → 여기에서 내려요.

Room check: pick three objects around you and place each one out loud — [thing]이/가 [somewhere] 위에/안에/옆에 있어요. Your actual room is now drill material.
