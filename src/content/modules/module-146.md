# Korean, Word-First — Module 146: Fresh Starts, Welcome and Looking Back

A departure is postponed. A family welcomes you. Work turns acquaintances into friends. The same grammar can carry all three changes; the vocabulary tells you which scene you are in.

**How to use this:** Group the words by scene, learn the checklist, then open the sentences. Follow chunk IDs across the three layers rather than trying to preserve English order in Korean.

---

## Part 1 — Change the state, change the viewpoint

**Close, then closer.** 친하다 from M19 describes a relationship. 친해지다 makes it develop, using the change pattern from M35. 함께 일하다 보니 gives that development a setting: as we worked together, a result emerged.

**Three different beginnings.** 출국 is leaving a country. 입사 is joining a company. 참석 is attending an event. None tells you the whole journey: you still need a place, a date, or an event to know exactly what happened.

**The sound is not the sense.** This 연기하다 means postpone, not act. This 개다 clears the weather, not folds clothing. This 고소하다 describes a nutty or savory taste; it is not the legal act of suing. Learn the scene with the spelling.

**A family direction.** 외갓집 points to the mother's family home. 반기다 tells you how people receive an arrival: gladly. One word supplies the destination; the other supplies the welcome.

**Earlier vocabulary:** 참석 → M69; 제삿날 → M69; 반기다 → M69. These words return in the examples as review; the 33 entries below are this pack’s new checklist.

::vocab::

고급스럽다 describes an impression of quality. 최고급 places something at the top of a quality scale. They can describe the same tableware, but they make different claims.

## Part 2 — Building sentences

The glue is already yours. Pay attention to whether the second clause adds a benefit, discovers a result, or concedes a point.

**Spot the recycling:** M145's 전시장 becomes a meeting place; 금메달 pairs with 상금; 식기 gains a luxurious appearance.

- **-(으)ㄹ 뿐만 아니라 from M89:** add a second reward or quality.
- **-더니 / -았/었더니 from M88:** connect observation or action with what follows.
- **-는 바람에 from M86:** explain an unwelcome consequence.
- **-더라도 from M95:** agreeing with a goal does not settle the method.

Eight sentences put the vocabulary into familiar grammar frames.

::sentences::

In 식욕이 없어요, the appetite is the Korean subject. Natural English supplies a person who has no appetite; the gloss keeps the Korean structure visible.

**Gap deck:** 코끝이 찡하다 puts emotion at the tip of the nose. 식욕이 없다 makes appetite, rather than the person, the grammatical subject.
