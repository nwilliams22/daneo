# Korean, Word-First — Module 77: The Calendar's Corners

M38 built the moment-words (순간, 새벽, 아까, 방금); M46 built the time-since shelf (이전, 이후, 시절, 지난해, 한때, 무렵). This module fills the corners they left: the native day-count closes (나흘, 닷새), the 날-welds and 그-welds arrive (전날, 이튿날, 그다음, 그해, 어젯밤), the year ladder gets both ends (재작년, 금년), the clock gets its soft spots (점심때, 저녁때, 밤중, 밤낮), the seasons become periods (여름철, 겨울철, 사계절, 무더위), and six schedule words close the shelf (일상생활, 규칙적, 스케줄, 임시, 본래, 만일). Twenty-six words, almost every one a weld of parts you already own. The organizing thesis: **Korean tells time by welding — a pointer or a span onto a day, a night, a year — and the welds obey sound rules you already hold.** No new grammar; a lot of new calendar.

**How to use this:** read each decode once and the word is half-memorized — 그 + 해, 어제 + ㅅ + 밤, 물 + 더위. Spend your reps on the register pairs instead: 저번 / 지난번, 금년 / 올해, 이튿날 / 다음 날, 어젯밤 / 어제 저녁. One meaning, two dress codes — speak the spoken one, read the news with the other.

## Part 1 — The welds pay their receipts

This shelf is mostly debts coming due:

- **The 흘-family closes:** M52's 사흘 note ended 'four days is 나흘, five 닷새 — the shelf's untaught tail'. Paid: 하루 (M8) · 이틀 (M52) · 사흘 (M52) · 나흘 · 닷새 · … · 열흘 (M52). Native spans for native trips, 3박 4일 included.
- **The 날-shelf and the 그-rail fill in:** 전날 / 그날 (M29) · 이날 (M46) / 이튿날 / 첫날 (M46) / 마지막 날 (M15) — and 그때 (M25) · 그동안 (M29) · 그날 (M29) · 그다음 · 그해. M29's 그 note called it 'Korean's closest thing to THE'; welded onto 다음 and 해 it becomes the storyteller's whole toolkit.
- **The year ladder gets both ends:** 재작년 → 작년 (M25) → 올해 (M25) / 금년 → 내년 (M25). M25's 올해 note said 'no Sino version needed' — 금년 is that version, and it lives on tax forms.
- **Seasons become periods:** M49's 철 was the market's season-word (딸기 철); welded onto M11's 여름 and 겨울 it becomes a period with rules — 여름철 건강, 겨울철 난방. And M11's 계절 note gets its receipt: 'Korea is proudly a land of 사계절 — four distinct seasons'.
- **Two IOUs from Band 4 clear:** M38's 일상 note wrote '일상생활 = daily life (M29's 생활 welded on)', and M39's 규칙 note promised '규칙적인 생활 = a regular routine (the -적 suffix Ring 2 owes you, previewing itself)'. Both words land today, the second through the -적 factory from M45.

**Radar patrol:** 금년 is now-今, not the gold-金 of 금요일 (M5) and 금 (M60) — identical in Hangul · 무더위's 무 is 물 with its ㄹ gone, not the nothing-無 of 무료 (M20) and 무조건 (M39) · 임시's 臨 (facing) is not the charge-任 of 책임 (M39) · 재작년's 再 is a third 재, after 재료 (M40) and the 재미 inside 재미있다 (M7) · 저번's 這 means 'this' in Chinese and 'the one before' in Korean · and 밤낮 puts the night first — English's day-and-night, reversed on purpose.

## Part 3 — Four sound rules at the weld, no new grammar

### 1. The rules, live

Every weld on this shelf obeys a rule you already hold. **The linking ㅅ:** 어제 + 밤 → 어젯밤 — the wedge M40's 고춧가루 note filed and M46's 오랫동안 reused. **ㄹ → ㄷ:** 이틀 + 날 → 이튿날 — the ㄷ that M18's 숟가락 wears where 젓가락 has ㅅ. **The ㄹ-drop:** 물 + 더위 → 무더위 — the move M49's 소나무 showed ('솔 (the old pine word) + 나무 (M22), the ㄹ stepping out at the weld'). **The nasal slide:** 재작년 is said 재장년 and 이튿날 is said 이튼날, the same slide M25's 작년 wears and M46's 첫날 taught; and **the ㄴ+ㄹ merge:** 본래 is said 볼래, exactly as M57's 원래 says 월래. Four rules, one shelf — read the weld and you can already pronounce it.

### 2. Two registers, one calendar

M46's 지난해 note drew the line — 'anchors say 지난해, the break room says 작년 (M25)' — and it runs the whole shelf. **Spoken:** 저번에, 어젯밤, 점심때, 저녁때, 밤낮이 바뀌다. **Print:** 금년, 수년, 임시, 본래, 만일 — and the narrative pair 이튿날 / 그해, which belong to stories and datelines rather than to either. Say 저번 주 to a friend; read 수년간 in the paper; write neither where the other goes.

**Calibration:** weld or space? The spacing interlude's rule holds — compounds 'weld solid' 'when they've fused into one concept': 그다음 is one word (after that), 다음 날 stays two (M8's 다음 + 날) and 이튿날 is its welded, story-register twin; 어제 저녁 (two words) is yesterday at dinner, 어젯밤 (welded) is later and darker; 점심시간 is the scheduled slot, 점심때 the vague hour — and 아침때 does not exist, because the morning is simply 아침에.

## Part 4 — Building sentences

**Spot the recycling:** -자마자 from M38 clocks the folktale morning, -기로 하다 from M48 books four days on Jeju, -겠- from M49 forecasts the 무더위 in 합니다체 (M4), 만일 announces -(으)면 from M20 exactly as M33's 만약 does, 마다 from M31 and 씩 from M43 run the morning walk, -아/어 버리다 from M52 regrets the flipped clock, 대로 from M54 relays the government's notice, and -잖아요 from M42 sells the cafe from last time.
