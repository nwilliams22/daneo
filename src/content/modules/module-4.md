# Korean, Word-First — Module 4: People, Family, and Being Polite

So far every sentence has come in exactly one politeness setting: the friendly-neutral -요 form. That was deliberate — it's the safest register in the language. But Korean grammar encodes **who you're talking to and who you're talking about**, and this is the module where that system opens up. The bricks are people — family, jobs, titles — because you can't practice respect without someone to respect.

**How to use this:** The glue section is bigger than usual and the vocab has a twist (four words for "older sibling"). Take it slow; everything else in the course builds on the -요 form you already own, so nothing here invalidates what you know.

---

## Part 1 — One dial, three settings

Every Korean sentence is spoken at a register. You already own the middle one:

| Register | "I'm going" | Where it lives |
|----------|-------------|----------------|
| 합니다체 (formal) | 갑니다 | News, presentations, interviews, first meetings, announcements |
| **해요체 (polite — yours)** | **가요** | Daily life default: shops, coworkers, most adults |
| 반말 (casual) | 가 | Close friends, younger family, kids |

Separately from the dial, Korean **elevates the person you're talking about** with the honorific marker -(으)시- and a few swap-in words. Talking *to* someone and talking *about* someone are two different systems, and this module gives you the starter kit for both.

## Part 3 — The glue

### 1. The missing "is": 이에요 / 예요

Until now every sentence ended in a verb. To say A *is* B — names, jobs, identities — Korean glues the copula straight onto the noun:

- ends in a consonant → **이에요**: 선생님**이에요** (is a teacher)
- ends in a vowel → **예요**: 가수**예요** (is a singer)

That's the machine behind Module 1's 저는 [이름]이에요. Negative ("is not") is 이/가 아니에요 — recognize it now, drill it later.

### 2. The formal gear: 합니다체

Drop 다, then: vowel stem → **-ㅂ니다**, consonant stem → **-습니다**. Questions swap 다 for 까: 갑니까?

| 해요체 (yours) | 합니다체 (formal) |
|--------------|------------------|
| 가요 | 갑니다 |
| 먹어요 | 먹습니다 |
| 일해요 | 일합니다 |
| 학생이에요 | 학생**입니다** |

You already know more 합니다체 than you think: 감사**합니다**, 죄송**합니다**, 만나서 반갑**습니다**. When to produce it: self-introductions, presentations, anything with a lectern. Mostly you'll *hear* it — airports, announcements, the news.

### 3. Honoring the subject: -(으)시-

When the person **doing** the action deserves respect — teachers, parents, grandparents, customers — the verb takes -(으)시-. In the -요 form that surfaces as **-(으)세요**:

- 오다 → 오**세요** (a respected person comes)
- 바쁘다 → 바쁘**세요** (a respected person is busy)

A few verbs don't take the marker — they get **replaced**:

| Plain | Honorific |
|-------|-----------|
| 먹다/마시다 | 드시다 |
| 있다 | 계시다 |
| 자다 | 주무시다 |

Even some *nouns* upgrade: 나이 → 연세 (age), 집 → 댁 (house). Two rules of thumb: never honor yourself, and when in doubt about someone's status — honor them.

### 4. 반말, previewed

Mechanically it's almost a joke: **drop the 요.** 가요 → 가. 먹어요 → 먹어. 미안해요 → 미안해. Swap 저 for 나, 안녕하세요 for 안녕. Socially it's the opposite of a joke: 반말 uninvited is genuinely rude. Use it only with close friends your age, younger family, and kids — or after the magic words 말 놓으세요 ("put the formal speech down"). Module 19 makes you fluent in it; for now, just recognize it.

## Part 4 — Building sentences

**Same skeleton, new endings.** Watch the copula glue onto nouns, 합니다체 starch up a Module 2 sentence, and honorifics track who deserves them.

::sentences::

**Talking to someone by name:** attach **씨** to their given name — 민수 씨, 지은 씨. Never to your own name, and never to a bare surname (김 씨 sounds like you're addressing the help in a period drama).

## Part 5 — Practice

Cover the right column and translate; then flip and build the Korean.

1. My family lives in Korea. → 우리 가족은 한국에 살아요.
2. My mother is a teacher. → 어머니는 선생님이에요.
3. I am a student. *(formal)* → 저는 학생입니다.
4. The teacher is coming. *(honorific)* → 선생님이 오세요.
5. Grandmother is eating. *(the swapped verb!)* → 할머니가 밥을 드세요.
6. My little sibling doesn't know Korean. → 동생은 한국어를 몰라요.
7. I eat with my family on weekends. → 주말에 가족하고 밥을 먹어요.
8. I'm going now. *(to a close friend)* → 지금 가.

Register check, no peeking: how do you apologize to a stranger you bumped into, and to a friend whose coffee you spilled? (죄송합니다 / 미안해!)
