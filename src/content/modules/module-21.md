# Korean, Word-First — Module 21: Clothes and Shopping II

Module 9 taught you to buy things; this module teaches you to buy *clothes* — which in Korean means learning that wearing isn't one verb but a **taxonomy**: 입다 for the body, 신다 for the feet, 쓰다 for the head, 끼다 for anything that grips. The garnish is **-아/어 보이다** — *looks / seems* — the compliment engine, and 보다's fourth career.

> **Warm-up (Module 20):** build these out loud — Module 20 blocks, combinations you've never seen written: *If the wi-fi is free, I'll go* (와이파이가 무료면 갈 거예요) · *Charge your phone* (핸드폰을 충전하세요) · *Delete the photo!* (사진 지워!). If the blocks don't snap together, visit Review first.

**How to use this:** the accessories name themselves — 목걸이 is M14's neck + M20's 걸다 (a neck-hanger), 귀걸이 is an ear-hanger, 잠옷 is sleep-clothes. Two-thirds of this checklist is old parts, newly snapped together.

## Part 1 — The wear-verb taxonomy

Korean assigns wearing by body zone. The full map, half of it already yours:

| verb | zone | examples |
|---|---|---|
| **입다** (M8) | body | 옷, 바지, 코트, 한복 |
| **신다** | feet | 신발, 양말, 운동화 |
| **쓰다** (M10!) | head | 모자, 안경 — the fifth job of 쓰다 |
| **끼다** | fitted things | 반지, 장갑, 안경 (shared custody) |
| **하다** (M1!) | accessories | 목걸이, 귀걸이, 목도리 |

One removal verb undoes them all: **벗다**. And 쓰다's résumé is now: write, use, spend, taste-bitter, wear-on-head — five jobs, one ㅡ-drop verb.

The clothing suffixes assemble categories: **-복** (교복, 수영복, 한복 — uniform, swimsuit, Korea-clothes), **-화** (운동화, 등산화 — shoes by purpose), **-걸이** (목걸이, 귀걸이 — things that hang).

## Part 3 — The glue

### 1. -아/어 보이다 — looks / seems

Conjugate as for -아/어요, then hand it to **보이다** (to be visible): the thing *appears* that way.

- 오늘 **멋있어 보여요** — you look great today
- **피곤해 보여요** — you look tired (said with care, answered with M16's 야근)
- **어려 보여요** — you look young: possibly Korea's most reliable compliment

This is 보다's fourth career — see (M1), watch-an-exam (M15), try (M16), and now *seem*. One verb family, most of your social toolkit.

### 2. The fitting-room arc

입어 보세요 (M16's try-machine — wear and see) → 잘 어울려요! (it suits you) → 사이즈가 안 맞아요 (M15's 맞다: right answers then, right fit now) → 좀 깎아 주세요 (market stalls only; department stores will smile and decline).

### 3. Shopping compositions

M20's hinge keeps paying: **비싸면** 안 살 거예요 (budget, grammaticalized) · 인터넷에서 옷을 **사면** 싸요 (the delivery-lobby economy, explained).

## Part 4 — Building sentences

**Spot the recycling:** 집에 오면 잠옷으로 갈아입어요 runs M20's hinge into M12's 갈아- (transferring between outfits instead of trains), and 사이즈가 안 맞아요 gives M15's 맞다 its second career.

::sentences::

**Compliment bingo:** 멋있어 보여요 · 어려 보여요 · 잘 어울려요 — three sentences that cost nothing and are worth everything. The gap deck's 옷이 날개다 explains the underlying physics.

## Part 5 — Practice

Cover the right column and translate; then flip and build the Korean.

1. You look great today! → 오늘 멋있어 보여요!
2. You look tired. Are you okay? → 피곤해 보여요. 괜찮아요?
3. Try it on. → 입어 보세요.
4. It really suits you! → 잘 어울려요!
5. The size doesn't fit. → 사이즈가 안 맞아요.
6. If it's expensive, I won't buy it. → 비싸면 안 살 거예요.
7. When I get home, I change into pajamas. → 집에 오면 잠옷으로 갈아입어요.
8. Hanbok is really beautiful. → 한복이 진짜 예뻐요.

Closet drill: narrate getting dressed with the right verb per zone — 입어요, 신어요, 써요, 껴요, 해요. Five verbs, one outfit; the taxonomy only sticks on your own body.
