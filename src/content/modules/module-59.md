**How to use this:** the compounds do half the memorizing for you — 땅콩 is ground-bean, 콩나물 is bean-greens, 양배추 is Western-cabbage: read every split before you drill. Spend your reps on the five farming verbs instead (심다, 가꾸다, 따다, 갈다, 썩다) — each one moonlights far from the field, and the second jobs are where the frequency rank comes from.

## Part 1 — The rice civilization

Korean splits rice three ways, and the split IS the culture:

- **벼 → 쌀 → 밥:** the plant in the paddy (벼 — riding frozen), the grain in the sack (쌀 — today's), the bowl on the table (밥 — Module 1, day one). English has one word; Korea, which measured wealth in it and taxes by it, needed three.
- **The 농-family:** 農 the farm-root runs 농사 (the doing — what grandfather does), 농업 (the industry — what the ministry manages), 농촌 (the village that does it). Not the basket-농 of M17's 농구; different court entirely.
- **논 and 밭:** wet paddy (논 — riding frozen) and dry field (밭) split the land between them — 논밭 together just means farmland. The 논 grows the 쌀; the 밭 grows this module.
- **The maker-verb's biggest client:** M54's 짓다 built houses, rice, names, and prescriptions. Today it takes its oldest commission: 농사를 짓다 — farming as composition, a year's living assembled from soil and weather.
- **The processed aisle labels itself:** 식품 is food-goods (the three-boxes-品 of M43's 제품), 식용유 is eat-use-oil, 음료 is drink-stuff (the 료 of M40's 재료) — Sino job descriptions, the 냉장고 school of naming (M13) running a whole supermarket.

## Part 3 — The word machines

### 1. The farming verbs all moonlight

Every field verb has a second job in town: 따다 picks apples AND licenses (자격증을 따다 — credentials get plucked, not won); 갈다 grinds coffee, plows fields, AND swaps out phones (핸드폰을 갈다); 가꾸다 tends the 정원 (M54) AND your 피부 (M41); 썩다 rots fruit, teeth (M8's 이), and politics (썩은 정치 — M32's noun, wearing the badge from M24). The field trained the verbs; the city hired them.

**Calibration:** three ways to raise a thing — M35's 키우다 grows it up (children, dogs, companies), M35's 기르다 does the same in its Sunday clothes (habits, beards, character), and 가꾸다 grooms what's already growing. Fields take all three; only 가꾸다 implies the result is worth looking at.

### 2. The powder line and the young-animal suffix

M40's 고춧가루 note promised '가루 (powder; 밀가루 is flour)' — today 가루 lands and the shelf assembles: 밀가루 (frozen 밀 + 가루), 후춧가루 (frozen 후추 + the linking-ㅅ + 가루 — the same ㅅ-weld as the chili powder). And one suffix unlocks the barnyard: -아지 makes animals young — 소 → 송아지, and M22's 강아지 turns out to have been 개 + 아지 all along. What 갈다 does, 가루 is; what -아지 touches, stays small.

## Part 4 — Building sentences

**Spot the recycling:** the honorific 께서 (M47) and time-since -(으)ㄴ 지 (M46) measure grandfather's forty years, M53's -는데 reports back from the market, M40's 담그다 and -아/어 두다 soak the rice, M31's 마다 plants the spring, M52's -아/어 버리다 seals the orchard's verdict, 만큼 (M47) weighs the ox against the family, and -죠 (M51) states the obvious about flour.

**Gap deck:** beans where you planted beans (콩 심은 데 콩 나고 팥 심은 데 팥 난다 — the harvest proverb M40's 뿌리다 note promised), scripture in an ox's ear (소 귀에 경 읽기), and the monkey who falls anyway (원숭이도 나무에서 떨어진다).
