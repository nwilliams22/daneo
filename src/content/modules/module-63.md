# Korean, Word-First — Module 63: Right and Wrong

Band 6 keeps slicing the long tail, and this slice is the judgment pack: the words Korean grades the world with — correct, fitting, excellent, botched. The centerpiece is hiding in plain sight: M10's 잘 and the spaced 잘 못 you've deflected with since Ring 1 fuse into a four-verb family — 잘못 (the fault), 잘못하다 (do wrong), 잘되다 (go well), 잘못되다 (go wrong) — that runs Korean's entire ledger of credit and blame, from the apology's load-bearing sentence (제 잘못이에요) to the blessing handed to every exam-taker (다 잘될 거예요). Around it, the quality bar assembles: 옳다 for choices, 정확하다 for watches, 훌륭하다 for people, and 곱다 — frozen in two earlier gap decks — finally thaws.

**How to use this:** the 잘/잘못 family is the module — drill the four as one axis: do it well, do it wrong, it goes well, it goes wrong. Then spend your reps on the precision pairs (맞다/옳다, 간단/단순, 심하다/심각하다, 세다/강하다): Band 6 words earn their frequency rank by NOT being interchangeable.

## Part 3 — The 잘/잘못 machine

### 1. The skill row and the blame row

M17 taught the skill row: 잘하다 / 못하다 — good at, bad at. Today adds the blame row: **잘못하다** — not doing it badly but doing it WRONG. 노래를 못해요 earns sympathy; **잘못했어요** asks forgiveness — the confession one rung below 미안해요 (M4), because it doesn't just regret, it admits. The spacing IS the law: 잘 못 해요 (spaced) pleads inability; 잘못했어요 (fused) accepts guilt. One space bar between an excuse and a confession.

### 2. The 되다 row

Flip both verbs to M10's 되다 and events grade themselves: **잘되다** = go well (잘되고 있어요 — it's coming along; 다 잘될 거예요 — the national blessing), **잘못되다** = go wrong (주소가 잘못됐어요 — the address is wrong). And the euphemism dramas run on: 잘못되면 = 'if something should happen…' — the worst case, unnamed.

**Calibration:** 실수 slips, 잘못 offends — a 실수 gets a laugh, a 잘못 gets an apology, and M15's 틀리다 stays in the answer key. The sentence deck runs all three notches: once is 실수, twice is 잘못.
