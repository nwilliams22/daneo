# Korean, Word-First — Module 58: Titles, Not Names

The course has been hinting since M4's 씨 and M9's 사장님 that Korean addresses ROLES, not names; this pack finally files the whole system. The people of Korean society, sorted: the -長 ladder from the classroom 반장 to the chaebol 회장, the 선배/후배 axis every campus and office runs on, the workforce register (노동자, 종업원, 공무원, 국회의원), the ages of a person from 소년 to 성인, the household's address words (여보, 사모님), and the army every male biography bends around. No new glue — Band 6 packs carry organizing machines, and this one carries the org chart.

**How to use this:** learn the two -장 economies FIRST — half the shelf hangs off one syllable. Drill the office titles with 님 attached (과장님, 부장님), because that is the only way you will ever say them to a face. And treat 아가씨, 녀석, and 형님 as comprehension-first words: you need to catch them instantly and deploy them almost never.

## Part 1 — One syllable runs two economies

- **Chief-長 vs place-場:** 사장님 (M9) and 부장님 (M16) always wore chief-長 — M39's 장점 note named it in passing ('사장님/부장님's chief-장'). Meanwhile 시장 (M3), 광장 (M23), 주차장 (M37), 공장 (M43), and 경기장 (M51) all pave place-場. Two economies, one syllable: one runs people, one runs real estate. The collision is a city — 시장 the market is 市場, city-place; put a chief on the same city and 시장 (市長) is the MAYOR. Context, or the 님, decides.
- **The four person-factories, reunited:** -자 (M27's 환자 note), -원 (M43's 사원), -가 (M48's 작가), -생 (M4's 학생) — every factory the course has opened gets a shift this module: 노동자, 책임자 · 공무원, 종업원, 회원, 국회의원, 인원 · 전문가 · 신입생, 졸업생.
- **The 先/後 axis:** 선생님 (M4) has carried before-先 since Module 4 — 先生, born-ahead — and 오후 (M5) has carried after-後. 선배/후배 are those two syllables running Korea's entire rank system.
- **The 少-pair:** 청소년 (M35) decoded 'green-few-years'; today 소년 and 소녀 hand you the few-years themselves — and the radar against 소설's small-小 (M48).
- **The 님-economy audits itself:** 선생님 (M4), 손님 (M9), 기사님 (M12), 부장님 (M16), 교수님 (M15) — five modules of grazing, one system: 님 honors whatever role it lands on. Today it lands on 고객, 회원, 사모, and every rung of the new ladder.

## Part 2 — The bricks

Almost every entry on this shelf is arithmetic you already own — a taught root plus a person-factory, or a unit plus chief-長. Read the decodes before you drill; the only true strangers here are the native register words (녀석, 아가씨, 여보), and those come with usage manuals attached.

::vocab::

**Radar patrol:** 군 files three ways — 軍 the military, 君 the dated Mr. (김 군), 郡 the rural county · 과장 the section chief (課長) is not 과장 the exaggeration (誇張) · and the register minefield is mapped entry by entry: 아가씨 (service-speech, not street-speech), 녀석 (downward only), 형님 (respect or racketeering, context decides).

## Part 3 — Two machines

### 1. The 長-machine — the org chart in one syllable

Bolt chief-長 onto any unit and its boss pops out: the office ladder runs 사원 (M43) → 과장 → 부장 → 사장 (M9) → 회장, the school runs 반장 → 회장 → 총장, and the machine mints on demand — 팀 (M16) + 長 = 팀장, the team lead (riding frozen until its module). Rule of thumb: if calling it plus 님 makes a person turn around, it's chief-長; if you can park in it, it's place-場.

### 2. Titles, not names — the address system

Korean speaks TO roles: 과장님 in the hallway, 기사님 (M12) in the taxi, 고객님 on the phone, 회원님 at the gym, 사모님 at the boss's dinner table. The 님-ladder (M16) supplies the rungs; 께서 (M47) makes the grammar agree when the role outranks you. Rank itself is sorted twice — by age (M47's family ladder) and by ENTRY DATE: the 선배/후배 axis, where a 25-year-old honors a 23-year-old who simply enrolled first.

**Calibration:** when in doubt, title UP — 사장님 flatters any shopkeeper, 선생님 (M4) flatters any stranger, and nobody was ever demoted by an extra 님. The words this module warns about (아가씨, 녀석) fail precisely because they title DOWN.
