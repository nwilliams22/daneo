# Korean, Word-First — Module 83: Kit and Carriage

The last thirty-four Grade-B words in the pool, and with them the B-list closes. Four drawers: the machine shelf (전화기, 수화기, 사진기, 청소기, 도자기 — and the 機/器 split M51's 악기 note drew), ten Sino tails (학생증, 연락처, 좌석, 자가용, 준비물, 화장품, 향수, 운동복, 국기, 수표 — a suffix each), the kit (바늘, 빗, 담요, 속옷, 책가방, 유리창, 신문지, 글씨, 받침, 축구공, 권투, 배구 — the bag, the bed, and the gym), and the carriage (고속버스, 시내버스, 공항버스, 지하도, and three proper nouns taught as real words: 김포공항, 인천공항, 동대문시장). The organizing thesis: **a Sino tail tells you what a thing IS FOR; a native weld tells you what it is MADE OF** — 화장품 is goods for makeup, 유리창 is a window made of glass — and the module ends at the airport and the all-night market, where the next trip starts. Thirty-four nouns, no new grammar, a dozen debts paid to the letter.

**How to use this:** read every decode before you drill — almost nothing here is a stranger, and the nine firsts (證, 座, 服, 旗, 陶, 瓷, 排, 鬪, 浦) do the rest. Spend your reps on the register pairs (전화 / 전화기, 자리 / 좌석, 차 / 자가용, 카메라 / 사진기, 이불 / 담요) and on the 기 file: four hanja on one syllable, and the compound tells you which.

## Part 1 — Tails and welds

Two ways Korean builds a thing-word, and this shelf runs both:

- **The machine shelf, sorted by hanja:** M51's 악기 note drew the line — "The 기 is tool-器 — a different hanja from 비행기's machine-機 (M12), same career" — and the shelf obeys it: a MOTOR gets 機 (전화기, 사진기, 청소기, with M12's 비행기 and M13's 세탁기), a VESSEL or a hand-tool gets 器 (수화기, 도자기, 악기). 도자기 is 器 in its literal job at last: a vessel.
- **The Sino tails, one suffix each:** 證 proof (학생증 — the 증 of M9's 영수증, finally read), 處 place (연락처 — the 처 of M36's 상처), 席 seat (좌석 — M64's 참석하다), 用 use (자가용 — M43's 비용), 物 thing (준비물 — M22's 동물), 品 goods (화장품 — M43's 제품), 服 clothing (운동복 — the -복 M21's 교복 named), 旗 flag (국기 — M26's 태극기), 票 ticket (수표 — M12's 표), 紙 paper (신문지 — M80's 봉지). Each tail says what the thing is FOR.
- **The native welds, parts you own:** 속옷 = M28's 속 + M7's 옷, 책가방 = M1's 책 + M6's 가방, 유리창 = M36's 유리 + the 창 of M65's 창밖, 축구공 = M17's 축구 + M17's 공, 글씨 = M32's 글 + 씨, and the bus fleet (고속버스, 시내버스, 공항버스 on M6's 버스). Each weld says what the thing is MADE OF or carries.
- **The proper nouns, taught as words:** the app has done it since M31's 인천 and 동대문 — 김포공항 — M60's 금 + a gold-inlet 浦 + M12's 공항 — flies 국내선, 인천공항 flies the world, and 동대문시장 sells the night. Real notes, real ledger rows, real drills.
- **The debts, paid verbatim:** M36's 유리 promised 유리창; M79's 팬티 filed 속옷 frozen; M37's 돌리다 ran 청소기; M50's 터미널 parked the 고속버스; M81's 시외 drew the 시내버스; M31's 인천 and 동대문 named their airport and their market; M41's 향기 mentioned 향수 in passing; M36's 도둑 quoted the needle proverb; M60's 매다 held a seat for 메다, which M66 filled and 책가방 now rides.

## Part 2 — The bricks

**Radar patrol:** 기 is machine-機 in 전화기, tool-器 in 수화기, flag-旗 in 국기, energy-氣 in 향기 · 증 certifies in 학생증 (證) and sickens in M53's 증상 (症) · 복 clothes you in 운동복 (服) and blesses you in M25's 복 (福) · 권 makes a fist in 권투 and counts books in M5's 권 · 배 pushes in 배구 (排), never the stomach-배 of M3 · 씨 in 글씨 is a native suffix, not the Mr./Ms. of M4 · 빗 the comb, M49's 빛 the light, and 빚 the debt all say 빋 alone · and 향수 is perfume (香水) unless it is homesickness (鄕愁) — the shop sells one, the exchange student has the other.

## Part 3 — Two registers, no new grammar

### 1. The spoken thing and the printed thing

Half this shelf is the printed twin of a word you already say: 전화 is the call, 전화기 the device; M20's 카메라 in the mouth, 사진기 on the museum label; M2's 차 in the mouth, M50's 자동차 on the form, 자가용 when the point is whose it is; M12's 자리 is the spot you ask about, 좌석 is what the ticket prints. Learn them as pairs — the meaning never moves, only the paperwork.

### 2. The suffix rail

A Sino tail can be pulled off and reused, which is why one module can hand you ten: -증 certifies (학생증, 신분증, 자격증), -복 dresses (운동복, 체육복, 교복), -기 motorizes or holds (전화기, 청소기 / 수화기, 도자기), -버스 routes (고속버스, 시내버스, 공항버스, 마을버스). Read the tail first and the head second; the tail is the category, the head is the job.

**Calibration:** when you need the object, reach for the tail word (전화기가 고장 났어요 — the DEVICE is broken); when you need the act, keep the bare noun (전화 왔어요 — a call came). Say 자리 to a stranger and 좌석 to a ticket machine; say 차 to a friend and 자가용 to explain why you are not on the 시내버스. The register is not politeness — it is which desk the word sits on.

## Part 4 — Building sentences

**Spot the recycling:** ~(으)ㄹ 예정이다 from M57 books the airport bus, -(으)면 from M20 runs both the student discount and the changed contact details (through M35's 바뀌다 and M37's 알리다), -자마자 from M38 lifts the handset, -아/어 놓다 from M40 banks the vacuumed floor and the wiped windows before Mom arrives, -(으)ㄹ 줄 모르다 from M55 explains the volleyball, 마다 from M31 schedules the standing commute, and -는데 from M53 turns the corner at the three a.m. market.

::sentences::

**Gap deck:** the needle thief who grows into a cow thief (바늘 도둑이 소 도둑 된다), the lap blanket that heats the Korean office (무릎 담요), and the all-night wholesale run that stocks every clothing shop in the country (동대문 새벽 시장).
